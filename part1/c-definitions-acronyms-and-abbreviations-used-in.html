Definitions, Acronyms, and Abbreviations Used in This Manual

The Manual's master glossary: defines what the Standard/Guidance/Option/Support headings mean, then defines every word and phrase, California-Vehicle-Code-defined term, and acronym/abbreviation used throughout the California MUTCD. Because this chapter is definitional rather than regulatory, it is presented below as a structured reference list rather than Standard/Guidance/Option/Support callouts — except Section 1C.01, which is itself the Standard defining those very headings.

1C.01Definitions of Headings Used in This Manual

Standard only.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01When used in this Manual, the text headings of Standard, Guidance, Option, and Support shall be defined as follows: (A) Standard — a statement of required, mandatory, or specifically prohibitive practice regarding a traffic control device. In limited, location-specific cases, the results of a documented engineering study (see Section 1D.03) might indicate a deviation from one or more requirements of a Standard provision to be appropriate. All Standard statements are labeled, and the text appears in bold type. The verb "shall" is typically used; "should" and "may" are not used in Standard statements. Standard statements are sometimes modified by Option statements. (B) Guidance — a statement of recommended practice in typical situations, with deviations allowed if engineering judgment or engineering study indicates the deviation to be appropriate. All Guidance statements are labeled, and the text appears in unbold italic type. The verb "should" is typically used; "shall" and "may" are not used in Guidance statements. Guidance statements are sometimes modified by Option statements. (C) Option — a statement of practice that is a permissive condition and carries no requirement or recommendation. Option statements sometimes contain allowable modifications to a Standard or Guidance statement. All Option statements are labeled, and the text appears in unbold type. The verb "may" is typically used; "shall" and "should" are not used in Option statements. (D) Support — an informational statement that does not convey any degree of mandate, recommendation, authorization, prohibition, or enforceable condition. Support statements are labeled, and the text appears in unbold type. The verbs "shall," "should," and "may" are not used in Support statements.

This four-way classification is the backbone of every other chapter in this Manual — every numbered paragraph you will encounter elsewhere in this training reference is tagged with exactly one of these four callout colors, matching this definition precisely.

1C.02Definitions of Words and Phrases Used in This Manual

Standard (introductory paragraphs and the glossary itself) · Support (errata note) — presented below as a reference list.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Unless otherwise defined in this Section, or in other Parts of this Manual, words or phrases shall have the meaning(s) as defined in the "Uniform Vehicle Code," "AASHTO Transportation Glossary (Highway Definitions)," "California Vehicle Code," or other appropriate publications.
  2. 02Where a term defined in this Section or elsewhere in this Manual has a different definition in another resource or in common use, the definition herein shall govern for purposes of the applicability of the provisions of this Manual.
  3. 03The following words and phrases, when used in this Manual, shall have the meanings set out in the glossary below.

Glossary of Words and Phrases (Paragraph 3)

Terms are numbered exactly as in the source (California-added terms carry a letter suffix, e.g. "25a," inserted at their alphabetical position).

1. Accessible Pedestrian Signal
A device that communicates information about pedestrian signal timing in a non-visual format such as audible tones and/or speech messages and vibrating surfaces.
2. Accessible Pedestrian Signal Detector
A device designated to assist a pedestrian who has vision or physical disabilities in activating the pedestrian phase.
3. Active Grade Crossing
A grade crossing equipped with automatic traffic control devices, such as flashing-light signals, gates, and/or traffic control signals, that are activated upon detection of approaching rail traffic.
4. Actuated
A type of traffic control signal operation in which some or all signal phases are operated on the basis of actuation.
5. Actuation
Initiation of, a change in, or an extension of a traffic signal phase or a sign legend through the operation of any type of detector.
6. Advance Preemption
The notification of approaching rail traffic forwarded to the highway traffic signal controller unit or assembly by the railroad or light rail transit equipment in advance of activation of the railroad or light rail transit warning devices.
7. Advance Preemption Time
The period of time that is the difference between the required maximum highway traffic signal preemption time and the activation of the railroad or light rail transit warning devices.
8. Advisory Speed
A recommended speed for all vehicles operating on a section of highway, based on the highway design, operating characteristics, and conditions.
9. Agency
An organization with responsibility for providing, maintaining, and/or operating a public or private road system.
10. Alley
A street or highway intended to provide access to the rear or side of lots or buildings in urban areas and not intended for through vehicular traffic. Per CVC § 110, an "alley" is any highway having a roadway not exceeding 25 feet in width primarily used for access to the rear or side entrances of abutting property; the City and County of San Francisco may designate by ordinance or resolution as an "alley" any highway having a roadway not exceeding 25 feet in width.
11. Annual Average Daily Traffic (AADT)
The total volume of traffic passing a point or segment of a highway facility in both directions for one year, divided by the number of days in the year. Periodic daily traffic volumes are normally adjusted for hours of the day counted, days of the week, and seasons of the year to arrive at AADT.
12. Application
In regard to a traffic control device, the act of deciding to use a device, generally or at a particular location for a particular condition.
13. Approach
All lanes of traffic moving toward an intersection or a midblock location from one direction, including any adjacent parking lane(s).
14. Arterial Highway (Street)
A general term denoting a highway primarily used by through traffic, usually on a continuous route or a highway designated as part of an arterial system.
15. Automated Vehicle
See Driving Automation System.
16. Automatic Lane
See Exact Change Lane, within the definition of Toll Collection.
17. Average Daily Traffic (ADT)
The average 24-hour volume — the total volume during a stated period divided by the number of days in that period. Normally, periodic daily traffic volumes over several days, not adjusted for day of week or season.
18. Average Day
A day representing traffic volumes normally and repeatedly found at a location, typically a weekday when volumes are influenced by employment, or a weekend day when volumes are influenced by entertainment or recreation.
19. Backplate
See Signal Backplate.
20. Barrier-Separated Lane
A preferential lane or other special-purpose lane separated from the adjacent general-purpose lane(s) by a physical barrier.
21. Beacon
A highway traffic signal with one or more signal indications that operates in a flashing mode. Types of beacons include: (a) Emergency-Vehicle Hybrid Beacon — a special type of beacon (see Hybrid Beacon); (b) Intersection Control Beacon — used only at an intersection to control two or more directions of travel; (c) Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon — a special type of beacon (see Hybrid Beacon); (d) Rectangular Rapid-Flashing Beacon (RRFB) — a pedestrian- and/or bicycle-activated device comprising two horizontally arranged, rapidly flashed, rectangular-shaped yellow indications used to provide supplemental emphasis for a pedestrian, school, or trail crossing warning sign at a marked crosswalk across an uncontrolled approach; (e) Speed Limit Sign Beacon — used only to supplement a SPEED LIMIT sign; (f) Stop Beacon — used only to supplement a STOP sign, a DO NOT ENTER sign, or a WRONG WAY sign; and (g) Warning Beacon — used only to supplement an appropriate warning or regulatory sign or marker.
22. Bicycle
A pedal-powered vehicle upon which the human operator sits. Also see Electric Bicycle (CVC § 312.5) and Pedicab (CVC § 467.5). Per CVC § 231, a bicycle is a device upon which a person may ride, propelled exclusively by human power, except as provided in § 312.5, through a belt, chain, or gears, and having one or more wheels; a person riding a bicycle is subject to CVC §§ 21200 and 21200.5; an electric bicycle is a bicycle. Also see CVC § 39000 and Streets & Highways Code (SHC) § 890.2.
23. Bicycle Box
A designated area on the approach to a signalized intersection, between an advance motorist stop line and the crosswalk or intersection, intended to provide bicyclists a visible place to wait in front of stopped motorists during the red signal phase.
24. Bicycle Facilities
A general term denoting improvements and provisions that accommodate or encourage bicycling, including parking and storage facilities, and shared roadways not specifically defined for bicycle use.
25. Bicycle Lane (see Class II Bikeway)
A portion of a roadway designated for preferential or exclusive use by bicyclists; a "bicycle lane" or "bike lane" is a Class II bikeway per SHC § 890.4(a). Typically delineated by longitudinal pavement markings and bicycle lane symbol or word markings and, if used, signs. Other types include: (a) Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane (see Class II Bikeway) — separated from the adjacent general-purpose lane(s) by a pattern of standard longitudinal pavement markings wider than a normal or wide lane line marking (SHC § 890.4); (b) Counter-Flow Bicycle Lane (see Class II and Class IV Bikeways) — a one-directional bicycle lane providing a lawful path of travel opposite the direction of general traffic on a one-directional roadway, designated by the same traffic control devices used for other bicycle lanes (SHC § 890.4); and (c) Separated Bicycle Lane (see Class II or Class IV Bikeway) — an exclusive facility located within or directly adjacent to the roadway, physically separated from motor vehicle traffic by a vertical element (SHC § 890.4).
25a. Bicycle Path or Bike Path
See Class I Bikeway. Per CVC § 231.5, a "bicycle path" or "bike path" is a Class I Bikeway or "shared-use path" as defined in SHC § 890.4(a).
25b. Bicycle Path Crossing
Per CVC § 231.6: (1) a "bicycle path crossing" is either (a) that portion of a roadway within the prolongation or connection of the boundary lines of a bike path at intersections where the intersecting roadways meet at approximately right angles, or (b) any portion of a roadway distinctly indicated for bicycle crossing by lines or other surface markings; and (2) there shall not be a bicycle path crossing where local authorities have placed signs indicating no crossing.
26. Bicycle Signal Face
A signal face that displays only bicycle symbol signal indications, exclusively controlling a bicycle movement from a designated bicycle lane or separate facility such as a shared-use path.
27. Bicycle Symbol Signal Indication
A red, yellow, or green signal indication that displays a bicycle symbol rather than a circular or arrow indication.
28. Bikeway
A generic term for any road, street, path, or way specifically designated in some manner for bicycle travel, whether exclusive to bicycles or shared with other modes. Per SHC § 890.4, "bikeway" means all facilities that provide primarily for, and promote, bicycle travel.
28a. Bike Route
See Class III Bikeway.
28b. Buffered Bicycle Lane
A bicycle lane separated from the adjacent general-purpose lane or parking lane by a pattern of standard longitudinal markings; the buffer area might include chevron or diagonal markings.
29. Blank-Out Sign
A sign that displays a single predetermined message only when activated; when not activated, the sign legend is not visible.
29a. Buffer-Separated Bicycle Lane
See Class II Bikeway.
30. Buffer-Separated Lane
A preferential lane or other special-purpose lane separated from the adjacent general-purpose lane(s) by a pattern of standard longitudinal pavement markings wider than a normal or wide lane line marking. The buffer area might include rumble strips, textured pavement, or channelizing devices such as tubular markers or traversable curbs, but not a physical barrier.
31. Business Identification Sign Panel
A panel containing a word legend or logo used to identify a business on a Specific Service sign.
31a. Business Activity District
Per CVC § 22358.9(b), a portion of a highway and the contiguous property that includes central or neighborhood downtowns, urban villages, or zoning designations prioritizing commercial land uses at the downtown or neighborhood scale, meeting at least three of: (1) no less than 50 percent of contiguous property fronting the highway is retail or dining commercial use (including outdoor dining) opening directly onto adjacent sidewalks; (2) parking (parallel, diagonal, or perpendicular) alongside the highway; (3) traffic control signals or stop signs regulating traffic flow at intervals of no more than 600 feet; and (4) marked crosswalks not controlled by a traffic control device.
31b. Business District
Per CVC § 235, a portion of a highway and contiguous property where (a) on one side, for 600 feet, 50 percent or more of the contiguous frontage is occupied by buildings used for business, or (b) on both sides collectively, for 300 feet, 50 percent or more of the contiguous frontage is so occupied; a business district may be longer if the ratio of business buildings to highway length still exists. See CVC §§ 240 and 515 to determine whether a highway is within a business or residence district.
32. Busway
A traveled way used exclusively by buses.
33. Cantilevered Signal Structure
A structure, also called a mast arm, rigidly attached to a vertical pole and used to provide overhead support of highway traffic signal faces or grade crossing signal units.
34. Center Line Markings
The yellow pavement marking line(s) that delineates the separation of traffic lanes with opposite directions of travel on a roadway. Need not be at the geometrical center of the pavement.
35. Changeable Message Sign
A sign capable of displaying more than one message (one of which might be "blank"), changeable manually, by remote control, or by automatic control. Electronic-display changeable message signs are called Dynamic Message Signs in the National ITS Architecture and Variable Message Signs in NEMA standards publications.
36. Channelizing Line
A solid wide or double white line marking used to form islands where same-direction traffic is permitted on both sides of the island.
37. Circular Intersection
An intersection with a generally circular island in its center where traffic passes to the right of the island. Includes roundabouts, rotaries, and traffic circles.
38. Circulatory Roadway
The roadway within a circular intersection on which traffic travels counterclockwise around the central island.
38a. Civil Engineer
A professional engineer in the branch of civil engineering; one who practices or offers to practice civil engineering in any of its phases (California Business and Professions Code § 6702).
38b. Class I Bikeway (such as a Bike Path or Shared-Use Path)
Provides a completely separated right-of-way designated for the exclusive use of bicycles and pedestrians, with crossflows by motorists minimized (SHC § 890.4; see Caltrans Highway Design Manual Index 1003.1 for design criteria).
38c. Class II Bikeway (such as a Bike Lane)
Provides a restricted right-of-way designated for the exclusive or semi-exclusive use of bicycles, with through travel by motor vehicles or pedestrians prohibited but vehicle parking and pedestrian/motorist crossflows permitted (SHC § 890.4; Caltrans HDM Index 1003.2).
38d. Class III Bikeway (such as a Bike Route)
Provides a right-of-way designated by signs or permanent markings and shared with pedestrians or motorists (SHC § 890.4; Caltrans HDM Index 1003.3).
38e. Class IV Bikeway (such as a cycle track, separated bikeway, or shared-use path)
Provides a right-of-way designated exclusively for bicycle travel adjacent to a roadway and separated from vehicular traffic (grade separation, flexible posts, inflexible physical barriers, or on-street parking) (SHC § 890.4; Caltrans Design Information Bulletin 89).
39. Clear Storage Distance
When used in Part 8, the distance available for vehicle storage measured between 6 feet from the rail nearest the intersection to the intersection stop line or normal stopping point. At skewed grade crossings and intersections, the 6-foot distance is measured perpendicular to the nearest rail along the center line or edge line, whichever is shorter. Where exit gates are used, distance is measured from the point where the rear of the vehicle would clear the exit gate arm; where the exit gate arm is parallel to the track(s), distance is measured along the center or edge line, whichever is shorter.
40. Clear Zone
The total roadside border area, starting at the edge of the traveled way, available for an errant driver to stop or regain control of a vehicle; might consist of a shoulder, a recoverable slope, and/or a non-recoverable traversable slope with a clear run-out area at its toe.
41. Collector Highway
A highway that, in rural areas, connects small towns and local highways to arterial highways, and in urban areas provides land access and traffic circulation within residential, commercial, and business areas and connects local highways to arterials.
42. Conflict Monitor
A device used to detect and respond to improper or conflicting signal indications and improper operating voltages in a traffic controller assembly.
43. Constant Warning Time Detection
A means of detecting rail traffic providing relatively uniform warning time for approaching through rail traffic that is not accelerating or decelerating after detection.
44. Contiguous Lane
A lane, preferential or otherwise, separated from adjacent lane(s) only by a normal or wide lane line marking.
44a. Contraflow Bicycle Lane
See Counter-Flow Bicycle Lane and Class II and Class IV Bikeways.
45. Controller Assembly
A complete electrical device mounted in a cabinet for controlling the operation of a highway traffic signal.
46. Controller Unit
The part of a controller assembly devoted to selection and timing of the display of signal indications.
47. Conventional Road
A street or highway other than an expressway or freeway.
47a. Counter-Flow Bicycle Lane
See Class II and Class IV Bikeways.
48. Counter-Flow Lane
A lane operating opposite the normal flow of traffic, designated for the peak direction of travel during at least part of the day; usually separated from off-peak direction lanes by tubular markers, other flexible channelizing devices, temporary lane separators, or a movable or permanent barrier.
49. Crashworthy
The ability of a roadside safety hardware device or appurtenance to minimize risks to vehicle occupants by allowing an impacting vehicle to be slowed before stopping, redirected, or to continue without significant resistance. See Section 1D.11 for additional information.
50. Crosswalk
(a) That part of a roadway at an intersection within the connections of the lateral lines of the sidewalks on opposite sides of the highway, measured from the curbs (or, absent curbs, from the edges of the traversable roadway), and, absent a sidewalk on one side, the part of the roadway within the extension of the lateral sidewalk lines at right angles to the center line; or (b) any portion of a roadway at an intersection or elsewhere distinctly indicated as a pedestrian crossing by pavement marking lines, possibly supplemented by contrasting pavement texture, style, or color. Per CVC § 275, a "crosswalk" is either: that portion of a roadway within the prolongation or connection of sidewalk boundary lines at intersections meeting at approximately right angles (except prolongation of such lines from an alley across a street); or any portion of a roadway distinctly indicated for pedestrian crossing by lines or other surface markings — except there shall not be a crosswalk where local authorities have placed signs indicating no crossing.
51. Crosswalk Lines
White or yellow (in school areas per CVC § 21368) pavement marking lines that identify a crosswalk.
52. Cycle Length
The time required for one complete sequence of signal indications.
52a. Cycle Track
See Class IV Bikeway.
53. Dark Mode
The lack of all signal indications at a signalized location; most commonly associated with power failures, ramp meters, hybrid beacons, beacons, and some movable bridge signals.
54. Dedicated Lane*
A lane on a freeway or expressway that provides access to: (a) either an exit lane or the mainline, but not both, at a freeway or expressway exit; or (b) only one roadway at a freeway or expressway split.
55. Delineator
A retroreflective device mounted at the side of the roadway in a series to indicate the alignment of the roadway, especially at night or in adverse weather.
56. Design Vehicle
The longest vehicle permitted by statute of the road authority (State or other) on that roadway.
57. Designated Bicycle Route
A system of bikeways designated by the jurisdiction having authority, with appropriate directional and informational route signs, with or without specific bicycle route numbers.
58. Detectable*
Having a continuous edge within 6 inches of the surface so that pedestrians with vision disabilities can sense its presence and receive usable guidance information.
59. Detector
A device used for determining the presence or passage of motor vehicles (including motorcycles), bicycles, or pedestrians.
60. Detection Plate
A smooth continuous plate used on pedestrian channelizing devices to facilitate the use of low-vision canes; the bottom edge shall be no more than 2 inches above the walkway and the top edge at least 8 inches above the walkway, sharing the same vertical plane as the hand trailing edge of the pedestrian channelizing device.
61. Diagnostic Team
A group of knowledgeable representatives of the parties of interest in a grade crossing or group of grade crossings (23 CFR Part 646.204), including at minimum representatives of the highway agency/authority with jurisdiction over the roadway, the railroad company and/or transit agency responsible for the track and signals, and the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC).
61a. Divided Highway
A highway with separated roadbeds for traffic in opposing directions (Caltrans HDM Index 62.3(5)(f)).
62. Downstream
A location encountered by traffic subsequent to an upstream location as it flows in an "upstream to downstream" direction. Example: the downstream end of a lane line separating a turn lane from a through lane on an intersection approach is the end closest to the intersection.
63. Driveway
An access from a roadway to a building, site, or abutting property. A "private road or driveway" is a way or place in private ownership used for vehicular travel by the owner and those with express or implied permission, but not other members of the public (CVC § 490).
64. Driving Aisle
Circulation area for motor vehicles within a parking area, typically between rows of parking spaces, providing one-way or two-way travel. Driving aisles are exempt from MUTCD provisions.
65. Driving Automation System
Technology that automates some or all aspects of the driving task to assist or replace the human vehicle operator. See Section 5A.03 for automation-level descriptions.
66. Dropped Lane
See Lane Drop.
67. Dual-Arrow Signal Section
A signal section designed to include both a yellow arrow and a green arrow.
68. Dynamic Envelope
The clearance required for light rail transit traffic or a train and its cargo overhang due to any combination of loading, lateral motion, or suspension failure (see Figure 8C-3).
69. Dynamic Exit Gate Operating Mode
A mode of operation where exit gate operation is based on the presence of vehicles within the minimum track clearance distance.
70. Dynamic Message Sign
See Changeable Message Sign.
71. Edge Line Markings
White or yellow pavement marking lines that delineate the right or left edge(s) of a traveled way.
71a. Electric Bicycle
Also see Bicycle (CVC § 231) and Pedicab (CVC § 467.5). Per CVC § 312.5(a), a bicycle equipped with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of less than 750 watts: (a) "class 1" (low-speed pedal-assisted) — motor assists only while pedaling and ceases assistance at 20 mph; (b) "class 2" (low-speed throttle-assisted) — motor may propel the bicycle exclusively but not above 20 mph; and (c) "class 3" (speed pedal-assisted) — motor assists only while pedaling, ceasing at 28 mph, and equipped with a speedometer.
71b. Electric Personal Assistive Mobility Device (EPAMD)
A self-balancing, nontandem two-wheeled device not greater than 20 inches deep and 25 inches wide that can turn in place, designed to transport one person, with an electric propulsion system averaging less than 750 watts (1 horsepower) and a maximum speed on a paved level surface of no more than 12.5 mph (CVC § 313).
71c. Electrically Motorized Board
Any wheeled device with a floorboard designed to be stood upon, not greater than 60 inches deep and 18 inches wide, designed to transport one person, with an electric propulsion system averaging less than 1,000 watts and a maximum speed on a paved level surface of no more than 20 mph; may also be powered by human propulsion (CVC § 313.5).
71d. Electrical Engineer
A professional engineer in the branch of electrical engineering; one who practices or offers to practice electrical engineering in any of its phases (California Business and Professions Code § 6702.1).
72. Electronic Toll Collection (ETC) Account Only Lane
A non-attended toll lane restricted to vehicles with a registered toll payment account.
73. Emergency-Vehicle Hybrid Beacon
See Hybrid Beacon.
74. Emergency-Vehicle Traffic Control Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
75. Engineer
See Professional Engineer.
76. Engineering Judgment
The evaluation of available pertinent information — including, but not limited to, the safety and operational efficiency of all road users — and the application of appropriate principles, experience, education, provisions, and practices contained in this Manual and other sources, for the purpose of deciding upon the design, use, installation, or operation of a traffic control device (see Section 1D.03). Engineering judgment shall be exercised by a professional engineer with appropriate traffic engineering expertise, or by an individual working under such an engineer's supervision, through procedures and criteria established by the engineer. Documentation of engineering judgment is not required.
77. Engineering Study
The analysis and evaluation of available pertinent information — including, but not limited to, the safety and operational efficiency of all road users — and the application of appropriate principles, engineering judgment, experience, education, provisions, and practices contained in this Manual and other sources, for the purpose of deciding upon the design, use, installation, or operation of a traffic control device (see Section 1D.03). An engineering study shall be performed by a professional engineer with appropriate traffic engineering expertise, or by an individual working under such an engineer's supervision, and shall be documented in writing.
77a. Engineering and Traffic Survey
See CVC § 627.
78. Entrance Gate
An automatic gate that can be lowered across the lanes approaching a grade crossing to block road users from entering the grade crossing.
79. Exclusive Alignment
A light rail transit track(s) or bus rapid transit busway that is grade-separated or protected by a fence or traffic barrier, with no grade crossings; motor vehicles, bicycles, and pedestrians are prohibited within the right-of-way. Includes subways and elevated structures.
80. Exit Gate
An automatic gate that can be lowered across the lanes departing a grade crossing to block road users from entering the grade crossing by driving in opposing lanes.
81. Exit Gate Clearance Time
For Four-Quadrant Gate systems at grade crossings, the amount of time provided to delay descent of the exit gate arm(s) after entrance gate arm(s) begin to descend.
82. Exit Gate Operating Mode
For Four-Quadrant Gate systems at grade crossings, the mode of control used to govern operation of the exit gate arms.
83. Expressway
A divided highway with partial control of access. Per CVC § 314, an "expressway" is a portion of highway that is part of (a) an expressway system established by a county under SHC § 941.4, or (b) an expressway system established by a county before January 1, 1989, as described in SHC § 941.4(g).
84. Fail-Safe
When used in Part 8, a railroad signal design philosophy applied so that a hardware failure or software error either prohibits the system/device from assuming or maintaining an unsafe state, or causes it to assume a state known to be safe.
85. Flagger
A person who actively controls the flow of vehicular traffic into and/or through a temporary traffic control zone using hand-signaling devices or an Automated Flagger Assistance Device (AFAD).
86. Flasher
A device used to turn highway traffic signal indications on and off at a repetitive rate of approximately once per second.
87. Flashing
An operation in which a light source, such as a traffic signal indication or LEDs in a sign, is turned on and off repetitively.
88. Flashing-Light Signals
A warning device consisting of two red signal indications arranged horizontally that flash alternately when rail traffic is approaching or present at a grade crossing.
89. Flashing Mode
A mode of operation in which at least one traffic signal indication in each vehicular signal face of a highway traffic signal is turned on and off repetitively.
90. Four-Quadrant Gate System
An exit gate system that includes entrance and exit gates controlling and blocking road users on all lanes entering and exiting the grade crossing.
91. Freeway
A divided highway with full control of access. Per CVC § 332, owners of abutting lands have no right or easement of access to or from their abutting lands, or only a limited or restricted right or easement of access.
92. Full-Actuated
A type of traffic control signal operation in which all signal phases function on the basis of actuation.
93. Gate
An automatically- or manually-operated traffic control device used to physically obstruct road users so that they are discouraged from proceeding past a particular point, or from entering a particular grade crossing, ramp, lane, roadway, or facility.
94. General-Purpose Lane
A highway lane or set of lanes, other than a Managed Lane or Preferential Lane, that all or most traffic allowed on that highway is also allowed to use. Certain vehicle classes (e.g., commercial vehicles, or vehicles exceeding a certain weight or size) might be prohibited; a general-purpose lane might also be restricted to certain uses such as passing, turning, or as an auxiliary lane.
95. Gore Area
See Physical Gore and Theoretical Gore.
96. Grade Crossing
The general area where a highway and a railroad and/or light rail transit route cross at the same level, including the tracks, highway, and traffic control devices for traffic traversing that area.
97. Grade Crossing Warning System
The flashing-light signals, with or without automatic gates, together with necessary control equipment used to inform road users of the approach or presence of rail traffic at a grade crossing.
98. Guide Sign
A sign showing route designations, highway names, destinations, directions, distances, services, points of interest, or other geographical, recreational, or cultural information.
99. High-Occupancy Vehicle (HOV)
A motor vehicle carrying at least two (or more, if signs for a specific roadway indicate a higher minimum occupancy) persons, including carpools, vanpools, and buses.
100. Highway
A general term for a public way for purposes of travel by vehicles and vulnerable road users, including the entire area within the right-of-way. Per CVC § 360, a "highway" is a way or place of whatever nature, publicly maintained and open to public use for vehicular travel; highway includes street (see CVC § 590).
101. Highway-Light Rail Transit Grade Crossing
The general area where a highway and a light rail transit route cross at the same level, including the LRT tracks, highway, and traffic control devices for traffic traversing that area.
102. Highway-Rail Grade Crossing
The general area where a highway and a railroad cross at the same level, including the railroad tracks, highway, and traffic control devices for traffic traversing that area.
103. Highway Traffic Signal
A power-operated traffic control device by which traffic is warned or directed to take some specific action; does not include power-operated signs (except as provided in Chapters 4S and 4T), steadily-illuminated raised pavement markers, gates, flashing-light signals, warning lights, or steady-burning electric lamps. Includes: (a) Flashing Beacon — see Beacon; (b) In-Roadway Warning Lights — a special type installed in the roadway surface to warn road users of a condition on or adjacent to the roadway that might not be readily apparent and might require reduced speed and/or a stop; (c) Lane-Use Control Signal — a signal face or comparable display on a full-matrix Changeable Message Sign displaying indications to permit or prohibit use of specific lanes or a part-time shoulder, or to indicate impending prohibition; and (d) Traffic Control Signal (Traffic Signal) — placed at intersections, movable bridges, fire stations, midblock crosswalks, alternating one-way single-lane sections, private driveways, or other locations requiring conflicting traffic to stop and proceed in an orderly manner (excludes pedestrian and emergency-vehicle hybrid beacons); includes vehicular, pedestrian, and bicycle symbol signal indications. Special traffic control signals include: (1) Emergency-Vehicle Traffic Control Signal — directs conflicting traffic to stop so an authorized emergency vehicle can proceed; (2) Movable Bridge Traffic Control Signal — installed at a movable bridge to notify traffic to stop while the roadway is closed for the bridge to open; (3) Portable Traffic Control Signal — a temporary, mobile-support component designed to be easily transported, deployed, or relocated as part of a temporary or permanent signal installation; (4) Pre-Signal — signal faces upstream from a signalized intersection, operated with the downstream signal to keep the area between the two stop lines clear of queued vehicles (also used to prevent queuing within the minimum track clearance distance at a grade crossing; supplemental near-side faces are not pre-signals); (5) Queue Cutter Signal — an independently-controlled signal at a grade crossing controlling one direction of travel to keep the minimum track clearance distance clear, activated by a downstream queue detection system, time of day, approaching rail traffic, an approaching bus, or a combination; (6) Ramp Control Signal — controls merging traffic onto a freeway at an entrance ramp or freeway-to-freeway connection; and (7) Temporary Traffic Control Signal — installed for a limited time using fixed or portable signal units.
104. HOV Lane
Any preferential lane designated for exclusive use by high-occupancy vehicles for all or part of a day, including a designated lane on a freeway, other highway, street, or independent roadway on a separate right-of-way.
105. Hybrid Beacon
A special type of beacon intentionally placed in dark mode between periods of operation that, when operated, displays both steady and flashing traffic control signal indications. Includes: (a) Emergency-Vehicle Hybrid Beacon — warns and controls traffic at an unsignalized location to assist authorized emergency vehicles entering or crossing a street or highway; and (b) Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon — warns and controls traffic at an unsignalized location to assist pedestrians crossing at a marked crosswalk.
106. Identification Marker
A shape, color, and/or pictograph used as a visual identifier for a destination guide signing system of a community wayfinding system or a shared-use path system for an area.
107. Inherently Low Emission Vehicle (ILEV)
Any vehicle that, because of inherent fuel system design properties, will not have significant evaporative emissions even if its evaporative emission control system has failed.
108. In-Roadway Warning Lights
See Highway Traffic Signal.
109. Interchange
A system of interconnecting roadways providing for traffic movement between two or more highways that do not intersect at grade.
110. Interchange Lane Drop
See Lane Drop.
111. Preemption Interconnection*
The electrical connection between the railroad or light rail transit active warning system and the highway traffic signal controller assembly for the purpose of preemption.
112. Intermediate Interchange
An interchange with an urban or rural route that is not a major or minor interchange as defined in this Section.
113. Intersection
(a) The area embraced within the prolongation or connection of the lateral curb lines, or, if none, the lateral boundary lines of the roadways of two highways that join one another at, or approximately at, right angles, or the area within which vehicles traveling on different highways joining at any other angle might come into conflict (see CVC § 365); (b) the junction of an alley, driveway, or site roadway with a public roadway or highway does not constitute an intersection unless the public roadway/highway at the junction is controlled by a traffic control device; (c) if a highway includes two roadways separated by a median, every crossing of each roadway of the divided highway by an intersecting highway is a separate intersection if the opposing left-turn paths cross and there is sufficient interior storage for the design vehicle (see Figure 2A-5); and (d) at a location controlled by a traffic control signal, regardless of the distance between the separate intersections defined in (c): (1) if a stop line, yield line, or crosswalk has not been designated on the roadway (median) between the separate intersections, the two intersections and the median between them are considered one intersection; (2) where a stop line, yield line, or crosswalk is designated on the intersection approach, the area within the crosswalk and/or beyond the designated line is part of the intersection; and (3) where a crosswalk is designated on the departure from the intersection, the intersection includes the area extending to the far side of that crosswalk.
114. Intersection Control Beacon
See Beacon.
115. Interval
The part of a signal cycle during which signal indications do not change.
116. Island
A defined area between traffic lanes for control of vehicular movements, for toll collection, or for pedestrian or bicyclist refuge; includes all end protection and approach treatments. Within an intersection area, a median or an outer separation is considered an island.
117. Jughandle Turn
A left-turn or U-turn that, with special geometry, is made by initially making a right-turn or diverging to the right (or, with other special geometry, a right-turn or U-turn made by initially making a left-turn or diverging to the left).
118. Lane Drop
A through lane that becomes a mandatory turn lane on a conventional roadway, or a through lane that becomes a mandatory exit lane on a freeway or expressway. The end of an acceleration lane, and through-lane reductions that do not involve a mandatory turn or exit, are not lane drops.
119. Lane Line Markings
White pavement marking lines that delineate the separation of traffic lanes with the same direction of travel on a roadway.
120. Lane Reduction*
Elimination of a through lane by a gradual narrowing of the travel pavement (taper) through physical construction or pavement markings, at which traffic in the lane being eliminated must merge into the adjacent through lane and continue in the same direction. Can occur outside the influence of an intersection or interchange, or within an interchange a short distance downstream of the gore of an exit ramp. Through lanes that become a mandatory turn or exit are lane drops rather than lane reductions.
121. Lane-Use Control Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
122. Legend
See Sign Legend.
123. Lens
See Signal Lens.
124. Light Rail Transit Traffic (Light Rail Transit Equipment)
Every device in, upon, or by which any person or property can be transported on light rail transit tracks, including single-unit LRT cars (streetcars, trolleys) and assemblies of multiple LRT cars coupled together.
124a. Limit Line
A solid white line not less than 12 nor more than 24 inches wide, extending across a roadway or any portion thereof, indicating the point at which traffic is required to stop in compliance with legal requirements (CVC § 377).
124b. Limit Line Bicycle Detection Zone
A Reference Bicycle-Rider must be detected in a 6 x 6 feet area immediately behind the limit line, centered either in a normal-width lane or, if the lane is more than 12 feet wide, centered 6 feet from the left lane line; for a lane of 20 feet or greater, two minimum 6 x 6 feet areas constitute the zone.
125. Loading Zone
A specially marked, signed, or designated area for the loading or unloading of vehicles (passenger or freight).
126. Locomotive Horn
An air horn, steam whistle, or similar audible warning device (49 CFR Part 229.129) mounted on a locomotive or control cab car. "Locomotive horn," "train whistle," "locomotive whistle," and "train horn" are used interchangeably in the railroad industry.
127. Logo
A distinctive emblem or trademark that identifies a commercial or non-commercial business, program, or organization.
128. Longitudinal Markings
Pavement markings generally placed parallel and adjacent to the flow of traffic, such as lane lines, center lines, edge lines, channelizing lines, and others.
129. Louver
See Signal Louver.
130. Low-Volume Rural Road*
A category of paved or unpaved conventional or special-purpose roadways with an AADT of less than 400 vehicles, lying outside built-up or urbanized areas of cities, towns, and communities.
131. Major Interchange
An interchange with another freeway or expressway, or with a high-volume multi-lane highway, principal urban arterial, or major rural route, where interchanging traffic is heavy or includes many road users unfamiliar with the area.
132. Major Street
The street normally carrying the higher volume of vehicular traffic.
133. Malfunction Management Unit
See Conflict Monitor.
134. Managed Lane
A highway lane or set of lanes, or a highway facility, for which variable operational strategies (direction of travel, tolling, pricing, and/or vehicle type or occupancy requirements) are implemented and managed in real time in response to changing conditions. Typically buffer- or barrier-separated, parallel to general-purpose lanes, with access restricted to designated locations; on some highways all lanes are managed.
135. Manual Lane
See Attended Lane, within the definition of Toll Collection.
135a. Markings
All lines, words, or symbols, except signs, officially placed within the roadway to regulate, warn, or guide traffic.
136. Maximum Highway Traffic Signal Preemption Time
The maximum time needed, following initiation of the preemption sequence, for the highway traffic signals to complete timing of the right-of-way transfer time, queue clearance time, and separation time.
137. Median
The portion of a highway separating opposing directions of the traveled way, or the area between two roadways of a divided highway measured from edge of traveled way to edge of traveled way; excludes turn lanes. Median width might differ between intersections, interchanges, and opposite approaches of the same intersection.
138. Minimum Track Clearance Distance
The length along a highway over the track(s) where a vehicle could be struck by rail traffic, measured from a point upstream on the approach to a point downstream on the departure from the grade crossing (see Section 8A.07).
139. Minor Interchange
An interchange where traffic is local and very light, such as with land service access roads; classified as local where the sum of exit volumes is estimated below 100 vehicles per day in the design year.
140. Minor Street
The street normally carrying the lower volume of vehicular traffic.
141. Mixed-Use Alignment
A light rail transit track(s), busway, or bus-only lane(s) where LRT or BRT vehicles operate in mixed traffic with all road user types, including streets, transit malls, and pedestrian malls with shared right-of-way; LRT/BRT traffic does not have right-of-way over other road users at grade crossings and intersections, even if traffic-signal-controlled and some approaches are used exclusively by LRT/buses.
141a. Motorcycle
A motor vehicle with a seat or saddle for the rider, designed to travel on not more than three wheels in contact with the ground (CVC § 400).
141b. Motor-driven Cycle
Any motorcycle with a motor displacing less than 150 cubic centimeters; does not include a motorized bicycle (CVC § 405).
141c. Motorized Bicycle
Or "moped" — a two- or three-wheeled device with fully operative pedals for human propulsion (or no pedals if powered solely by electrical energy), an automatic transmission, and a motor producing less than 4 gross brake horsepower, capable of a maximum speed of not more than 30 mph on level ground (CVC § 406).
141d. Motorized Quadricycle / Motorized Tricycle
A four-wheeled ("quadricycle") or three-wheeled ("tricycle") device designed to carry not more than two persons including the driver, with an electric motor or a motor with automatic transmission developing less than two gross brake horsepower and a maximum speed of not more than 30 mph on level ground; utilized only by a person who by reason of physical disability is otherwise unable to move about as a pedestrian, or by a senior citizen (CVC § 407).
141e. Motorized Scooter
Any two-wheeled device with handlebars, either a floorboard designed to be stood upon or a seat and footrests, powered by an electric motor; may also be powered by human propulsion (CVC § 407.5).
142. Movable Bridge Resistance Gate
A type of traffic gate located downstream of the movable bridge warning gate that provides a physical deterrent to vehicle and/or pedestrian traffic when in the appropriate position.
143. Movable Bridge Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
144. Movable Bridge Warning Gate
A type of traffic gate designed to warn, but not primarily to block, vehicle and/or pedestrian traffic when in the appropriate position.
145. Multi-Lane
More than one lane moving in the same direction. A multi-lane street, highway, or roadway has a basic cross-section of two or more through lanes in one or both directions; a multi-lane approach has two or more lanes moving toward the intersection, including turning lanes.
146. Neutral Area
The paved area between the channelizing lines separating an entrance or exit ramp, or a channelized turn/entering lane, from the adjacent through lane(s).
146a. Night or Nighttime
Equivalent to "darkness" defined by CVC § 280: any time from one-half hour after sunset to one-half hour before sunrise, and any other time when visibility is not sufficient to clearly discern a person or vehicle on the highway from a distance of 1,000 feet.
147. Object Marker
A device used to mark obstructions within or adjacent to the roadway.
148. Occupancy Requirement
Any restriction regulating use of a facility, or one or more lanes of a facility, for any period of the day based on a specified minimum number of persons in a vehicle.
149. Occupant
A person driving or riding in a car, truck, bus, or other vehicle.
150. On-Street Parking
Parking within or along, and accessed directly from, a public roadway or a site roadway open to public travel.
151. Open-Road ETC Lane
A non-attended lane designed to allow toll payments to be electronically collected from vehicles traveling at normal highway speeds; typically physically separated from the toll plaza, often following the mainline alignment, with cash-toll plaza lanes on a different alignment after diverging from the mainline (or a subset of it).
152. Open-Road Tolling Point
The location along an Open-Road ETC lane where roadside or overhead detection and receiving equipment are placed and vehicles are electronically assessed a toll.
153. Opposing Traffic
Vehicles traveling in the opposite direction. At an intersection, vehicles entering from an approximately straight-ahead approach are opposing traffic; vehicles entering from approaches on the left or right are conflicting traffic, not opposing traffic.
154. Option Lane*
A lane on a freeway, expressway, or conventional road multi-lane exit or split that widens on approach to allow access, without changing lanes, to: (a) both an exit lane and the mainline at a freeway or expressway exit; or (b) both diverging roadways at a freeway, expressway, or conventional road split.
155. Overhead Sign
A sign placed such that a portion or all of the sign or its support is directly above the roadway or shoulder, with vehicles traveling below it. Typical installations: cantilever-arm signs, signs on structures spanning the full pavement width, signs on mast arms or span wires (independently or supporting signals), and signs on highway bridges crossing over the roadway.
155a. Park or Parking
Standing of a vehicle, occupied or not, other than temporarily for loading or unloading merchandise or passengers (CVC § 463).
156. Parking Area
A parking lot or parking garage separated from a roadway; parallel, perpendicular, or angle parking spaces along a roadway are not a parking area.
157. Parking Space
An area marked or designated for storage of a vehicle while the driver is not present.
158. Preemption Clearance Interval*
The part of a traffic signal sequence displayed as a result of a preemption request, during which vehicles are given the opportunity to clear the railroad or light rail transit tracks, or a movable bridge, before the train or boat arrives for which the signal is being preempted.
159. Preemption Time Variability*
The result that occurs when the traffic signal controller enters the Preemption Clearance Interval with less than the maximum design Right-of-Way Transfer Time, or the speed of an approaching train varies.
160. Passive Grade Crossing
A grade crossing where none of the automatic traffic control devices associated with an Active Grade Crossing Warning System are present, and where traffic control devices consist entirely of signs and/or markings.
161. Pathway
A general term for a public way for travel by authorized users outside the traveled way and physically separated from the roadway by open space or a barrier, either within the highway right-of-way or on an independent alignment. Includes shared-use paths, but not sidewalks.
162. Pathway Grade Crossing
The general area where a pathway and railroad and/or light rail transit tracks cross at the same level, including the tracks, pathway, and traffic control devices for pathway traffic traversing that area.
163. Paved
Having a roadway surface with both a structural (weight-bearing) and a sealing purpose, such as bituminous surface treatment, mixed bituminous concrete, or Portland cement concrete.
164. Pedestrian
A person on foot, in a wheelchair, or on other devices determined by local law to be equivalent, which might include skates or a skateboard. Per CVC § 467: (a) a "pedestrian" is a person who is afoot or using (1) a means of conveyance propelled by human power other than a bicycle, or (2) an electric personal assistive mobility device; and (b) "pedestrian" includes a person operating a self-propelled wheelchair, motorized tricycle, or motorized quadricycle who, by reason of physical disability, is otherwise unable to move about as a pedestrian.
165. Pedestrian Change Interval
An interval during which the flashing UPRAISED HAND (symbolizing DON'T WALK) signal indication is displayed.
166. Pedestrian Clearance Time
The time provided for a pedestrian crossing in a crosswalk, after leaving the curb or edge of pavement, to travel to the far side of the traveled way or to a median.
167. Pedestrian Facility
A general term for a location where improvements and provisions have been made to accommodate or encourage pedestrian activity.
168. Pedestrian Hybrid Beacon
See Hybrid Beacon.
169. Pedestrian Signal Head
A signal head containing the WALKING PERSON (symbolizing WALK) and UPRAISED HAND (symbolizing DON'T WALK) symbols, installed to direct pedestrians at a traffic control signal.
169a. Pedicab
Also see Bicycle (CVC § 231) and Electric Bicycle (CVC § 312.5). Per CVC § 467.5, any of: (a) a bicycle (including electric) with three or more wheels that transports passengers on attached seats, operated by a person, used to transport passengers for hire; (b) a bicycle (including electric) that pulls a trailer, sidecar, or similar device transporting passengers, used for hire; or (c) a four-wheeled, primarily/exclusively pedal-powered device with seating for eight or more passengers, unable to exceed 15 mph, used to transport passengers for hire.
170. Permissive Mode
A mode of traffic control signal operation in which left or right turns are permitted after yielding to pedestrians, if any, and/or opposing traffic, if any. A CIRCULAR GREEN indication permits both left and right turns unless otherwise prohibited by another device; a flashing YELLOW ARROW or flashing RED ARROW indication permits the turn indicated by the arrow.
171. Physical Gore
A longitudinal point where a physical barrier or the lack of a paved surface inhibits road users from crossing from a ramp or channelized turn/entering lane to the adjacent through lane(s), or vice versa.
172. Pictograph
A pictorial representation used to identify a governmental jurisdiction, an area of jurisdiction, a governmental or other public transportation agency or provider, a military base or branch of service, a governmental-approved university or college, a governmental-approved institution, or a toll payment system.
173. Plaque
A traffic control device intended to communicate specific information to road users through a word, symbol, or arrow legend placed immediately adjacent to a sign to supplement its message. Unlike a sign, a plaque cannot be used alone; its designation includes a "P" suffix.
174. Platoon
A group of vehicles or pedestrians traveling together as a group, voluntarily or involuntarily, because of traffic signal controls, geometrics, or other factors.
174a. Pocket Bike
Per CVC § 473, a two-wheeled motorized device with a seat or saddle for the rider, not designed or manufactured for highway use; does not include an off-highway motorcycle as defined in CVC § 436.
175. Portable Traffic Control Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
176. Post-Exit Ramp Lane Reduction
See Lane Reduction.
177. Post-Mounted Sign
A sign placed to the side of the roadway such that no portion of the sign or its support is directly above the roadway or shoulder.
178. Posted Speed Limit
A speed limit determined by law or regulation and displayed on Speed Limit signs.
179. Preemption
The transfer of normal operation of a traffic control signal or hybrid beacon to a special control mode of operation.
180. Preferential Lane
A highway lane or set of lanes, or a highway facility, reserved for the exclusive use of one or more specific types of vehicles or vehicles with a specific minimum number of occupants.
181. Pre-Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
182. Pretimed Operation
A type of traffic control signal operation in which none of the signal phases function on the basis of actuation.
183. Primary Signal Face
One of the required or recommended minimum number of signal faces for a given approach or separate turning movement, not including near-side signal faces required because far-side signal faces exceed the maximum distance from the stop line.
184. Principal Legend
Place names, street names, and route numbers displayed on guide signs.
185. Priority Control
A means by which the assignment of right-of-way is obtained or modified.
186. Private Road
See Site Roadways Open to Public Travel (CVC § 490).
187. Professional Engineer (P.E.)*
An individual who has fulfilled education and experience requirements and passed examinations that, under State licensure laws, permit the individual to offer engineering services within areas of expertise directly to the public (California Business and Professions Code § 6704). (a) Engineer — a person registered under the California Professional Engineers Act as a professional engineer, including any branch thereof (§ 6706). (b) Professional Engineer — a person engaged in the professional practice of rendering service or creative work requiring education, training, and experience in engineering sciences and application of special mathematical, physical, and engineering knowledge in such professional or creative work as consultation, investigation, evaluation, planning, or design of public or private utilities, structures, machines, processes, circuits, buildings, equipment, or projects, and supervision of construction to secure compliance with specifications and design (§ 6701).
188. Protected Mode
A mode of traffic control signal operation in which left or right turns are permitted only when a left or right GREEN ARROW signal indication is displayed.
189. Public Road
Any road, street, or similar facility under the jurisdiction of and maintained by a public agency and open to public travel (see Site Roadways Open to Public Travel).
190. Push Button
A button to activate a device or signal timing for pedestrians, bicyclists, or other road users.
191. Push Button Information Message
A recorded message actuated by pressing a push button when the walk interval is not timing, providing the name of the street the associated crosswalk crosses and possibly other information about intersection signalization or geometry.
192. Push Button Locator Tone
A repeating sound informing approaching pedestrians that a push button exists to actuate pedestrian timing or receive additional information, enabling pedestrians with vision disabilities to locate the push button.
193. Queue Clearance Time
When used in Part 8, the time required for the design vehicle of maximum length, stopped just inside the minimum track clearance distance, to start up and move through and clear the entire minimum track clearance distance.
194. Queue Cutter Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
195. Quiet Zone
A segment of a rail line containing one or more consecutive public highway-rail grade crossings at which locomotive horns are not routinely sounded per 49 CFR Part 222.
196. Rail Traffic
Every device in, upon, or by which any person or property can be transported on rails or tracks and to which all other traffic must yield the right-of-way by law at grade crossings, including trains, one or more coupled locomotives (with or without cars), other railroad equipment, and light rail transit operating in exclusive or semi-exclusive alignments. Light rail transit in a mixed-use alignment, to which other traffic is not required to yield by law, is a vehicle and not rail traffic.
197. Raised Pavement Marker
A device mounted on or in a road surface, generally not exceeding about 1 inch above the surface for a permanent marker (about 2 inches for a temporary flexible marker), intended as a positioning guide and/or to supplement or substitute for pavement markings; might be recessed into or flush with the pavement surface.
198. Ramp Control Signal (Ramp Meter)
See Highway Traffic Signal.
199. Red Clearance Interval
An interval that follows a yellow change interval and precedes the next conflicting green interval.
199a. Reference Bicycle-Rider
A minimum 4-foot-tall person, weighing a minimum 90 lb, riding an unmodified minimum 16-inch-wheel bicycle with non-ferromagnetic frame, non-ferromagnetic fork and cranks, aluminum rims, stainless steel spokes, and headlight.
199b. Registered Engineer
See Professional Engineer.
200. Regulatory Sign
A sign that gives notice to road users of traffic laws or regulations.
200a. Residence District
Per CVC § 515, a portion of a highway and contiguous property, other than a business district, where (a) on one side, within a quarter mile, contiguous property fronting the highway is occupied by 13 or more separate dwelling houses or business structures, or (b) on both sides collectively, within a quarter mile, occupied by 16 or more; may be longer than one-quarter mile if the ratio still exists. See CVC §§ 235 and 240 to determine whether a highway is within a business or residence district.
201. Retroreflectivity
A property of a surface that allows a large portion of light from a point source to be returned directly back to a point near its origin.
202. Road
See Roadway.
203. Road User
A vehicle operator, bicyclist, or pedestrian, including persons with disabilities, within the highway or on a site roadway open to public travel (see Site Roadways Open to Public Travel).
204. Roadway
That portion of a highway improved, designed, or ordinarily used for vehicular travel and parking lanes, exclusive of the sidewalk, berm, or shoulder, even if used by persons riding bicycles or other human-powered vehicles. Where a highway includes two or more separate roadways, "roadway" refers to any such roadway separately, not to all collectively (CVC §§ 527 and 530).
205. Roadway Network
A geographical arrangement of intersecting roadways.
206. Roundabout
A circular intersection with yield control at entry, permitting a vehicle on the circulatory roadway to proceed, with deflection of the approaching vehicle counterclockwise around a central island.
207. Rumble Strip
A series of intermittent, narrow, transverse areas of rough-textured, slightly raised, or depressed road surface that extend across the travel lane to alert vehicle operators to unusual conditions, or that are located along the shoulder, center line, or within pavement-marking islands to alert road users they are leaving the travel lanes.
208. Rural Highway
A roadway type normally characterized by lower volumes, higher speeds, fewer turning conflicts, and less pedestrian conflict.
209. Scanning Graphic
A graphic designed for scanning by machine, including bar codes, quick-response (QR) codes, other matrix bar code formats, or similar graphics.
209a. Scenic Highway
An officially designated portion of the State Highway System traversing areas of outstanding scenic beauty that, together with adjacent scenic corridors, requires special scenic conservation treatment.
210. School
A public or private educational institution recognized by the State education authority for one or more grades K through 12, or as otherwise defined by the State. A "private school" is any school, whether conducted for profit or not, giving training similar to a public school at or below twelfth grade, including schools owned or operated by a church (CVC § 492).
211. School Zone
A designated roadway segment approaching, adjacent to, and beyond school buildings or grounds, or along which school-related activities occur. Per CVC § 22352(a)(2)(B), applies when approaching or passing a school building or grounds contiguous to a highway posted with a standard "SCHOOL" warning sign while children are going to or leaving school during school hours or the noon recess period; also applies to school grounds not separated from the highway by a fence, gate, or other physical barrier while in use by children and the highway is posted accordingly.
212. Semi-Actuated
A type of traffic control signal operation in which at least one, but not all, signal phases function on the basis of actuation.
213. Semi-Exclusive Alignment
A light rail transit track(s) or bus rapid transit busway in a separate right-of-way, or along a street or railroad right-of-way, where motor vehicles, bicycles, and pedestrians have limited access and cross only at designated locations (e.g., grade crossings) where road users must yield to LRT/BRT traffic.
213a. Separated Bicycle Lane
See Class II or Class IV Bikeway.
214. Separate Turn Signal Face
A signal face that exclusively controls a turn movement, displaying indications applicable only to that turn movement.
215. Separation Time
The component of maximum highway traffic signal preemption time during which the minimum track clearance distance is clear of vehicular traffic prior to the arrival of rail traffic.
216. Serviceable
A condition in which a traffic control device appears (day and night) and operates as intended, beyond which it requires replacement due to damage or wear. Whether a device is serviceable depends on the device type: if it can be serviced with minimal effort/replacement parts so it continues to appear and operate as intended and remains substantially intact, it is serviceable; if damaged or non-operational beyond reasonable repair, it is likely no longer serviceable.
217. Shared Roadway
A roadway officially designated and marked as a bicycle route but open to motor vehicle travel, with no bicycle lane designated. Shared Roadway (No Bikeway Designation) — a roadway that permits bicycle use but is not officially designated as a bikeway.
218. Shared Turn Signal Face
A signal face controlling both a turn movement and the adjacent through movement, always displaying the same color of circular signal indication as the adjacent through signal face(s).
219. Shared-Use Path (Class I or IV Bikeway)
A bikeway outside the traveled way, physically separated from motorized vehicular traffic by open space or a barrier, either within the highway right-of-way or on an independent alignment. Also used by pedestrians (including skaters, manual and motorized wheelchair users, and joggers) and other authorized motorized and non-motorized users.
220. Shoulder
A longitudinal area contiguous with the traveled way used for accommodation of stopped vehicles for emergency use and for lateral support of base and surface courses, graded for emergency stopping; might be paved or unpaved. A paved shoulder might be opened to part-time travel by some or all vehicles and might also be available for pedestrians and/or bicycles absent other facilities. See Caltrans HDM Index 62.1(9) for design criteria.
221. Sidewalk
That portion of a street between the curb line (or lateral line of a roadway) and the adjacent property line, or on private-property easements, that is paved/improved and intended for pedestrian use. Per CVC § 555, a "sidewalk" is that portion of a highway, other than the roadway, set apart by curbs, barriers, markings, or other delineation for pedestrian travel.
222. Sidewalk Extension
A pedestrian facility at an intersection or midblock crosswalk that extends the sidewalk by physically and visually narrowing the roadway.
223. Sidewalk Grade Crossing
The portion of a highway-rail or highway-light rail transit grade crossing where a sidewalk and tracks cross at the same level, including the tracks, sidewalk, and traffic control devices for sidewalk users traversing that area.
224. Sign
With regard to controlling traffic, any traffic control device intended to communicate specific information to road users through a word, symbol, and/or arrow legend. Does not include highway traffic signals, pavement markings, delineators, or channelization devices. Signs whose purpose is unrelated to traffic control are addressed in Section 1A.02.
225. Sign Assembly
A group of signs, located on the same support(s), that supplement one another in conveying information to road users.
226. Sign Illumination
Either internal or external lighting that shows a similar color by day or night; street or highway lighting is not considered to meet this definition.
227. Sign Legend
All word messages, logos, pictographs, and symbol and arrow designs intended to convey specific meanings; the border, if any, is not part of the legend.
228. Sign Panel
A separate panel or piece of material containing a word, logo, pictograph, symbol, and/or arrow legend affixed to the face of a sign.
229. Signal*
See Highway Traffic Signal.
230. Signal Backplate
A thin strip of material extending outward from and parallel to a signal face on all sides of a signal housing to provide a background for improved visibility of the signal indications.
231. Signal Coordination
The establishment of timed relationships between adjacent traffic control signals.
232. Signal Dimming
A reduction in light output from a signal indication, hybrid beacon, or RRFB indication, typically for nighttime, to a value below the minimum specified intensity for daytime conditions. Using a variety of daytime intensity levels, all above the minimum specified intensity, is not signal dimming.
233. Signal Face
An assembly of one or more signal sections provided for controlling one or more traffic movements on a single approach.
234. Signal Head
An assembly of one or more signal faces provided for controlling traffic movements on one or more approaches.
235. Signal Housing
The part of a signal section that protects the light source and other required components.
236. Signal Indication
The illumination of a signal lens or equivalent device.
237. Signal Lens
The part of the signal section that redirects light coming directly from the light source and its reflector, if any.
238. Signal Louver
A device mounted inside a signal visor to restrict visibility of a signal indication from the side, or to limit visibility to a certain lane or lanes or a certain distance from the stop line.
239. Signal Phase
The right-of-way, yellow change, and red clearance intervals in a cycle assigned to an independent traffic movement or combination of movements.
240. Signal Section
The assembly of a signal housing, signal lens (if any), and light source with necessary components for displaying one signal indication.
241. Signal Sequence (Sequence of Indications)
The order of appearance of signal indications during successive intervals of a signal cycle.
242. Signal System
Two or more traffic control signals operating in signal coordination.
243. Signal Timing
The amount of time allocated for the display of a signal indication.
244. Signal Visor
The part of a signal section that directs the signal indication specifically to approaching traffic and reduces the effect of direct external light entering the signal lens.
245. Signing
Individual signs or a group of signs, not necessarily on the same support(s), that supplement one another in conveying information to road users.
246. Simultaneous Preemption
Notification of approaching rail traffic forwarded to the highway traffic signal controller unit or assembly and railroad or light rail transit active warning devices at the same time.
247. Site Roadways Open to Public Travel*
Roadways and bikeways on sites of shopping centers, office parks, airports, schools, universities, sports arenas, recreational parks, and similar business, governmental, and/or recreation facilities, publicly or privately owned, where the public is allowed to travel without full-time access restrictions. Excludes: (1) roadways where access is restricted at all times by gates and/or guards to residents, employees, or other specifically-authorized persons; and (2) private highway-rail grade crossings. Does not include parking areas or driving aisles within them. The MUTCD national standard and Caltrans standards/specifications for traffic control devices are not applicable to privately owned and maintained roads or commercial establishments unless the city or county enacts an ordinance or resolution to that effect (CVC §§ 21100, 21100.1, 21107, 21107.5, 21107.6, 21107.7).
247a. Private Road or Driveway
A way or place in private ownership used for vehicular travel by the owner and those with express or implied permission, but not by other members of the public (CVC § 490).
248. Special-Purpose Road
A low-volume, low-speed road serving recreational areas or resource development activities.
249. Speed
Classified as: (a) Average Speed — the summation of instantaneous or spot-measured speeds at a specific location divided by the number of vehicles observed; (b) Design Speed — a selected speed used to determine the geometric design features of a roadway; (c) 85th-Percentile Speed — the speed at or below which 85 percent of motor vehicles travel; (d) Operating Speed — a speed at which a typical vehicle or the overall traffic operates, possibly defined by average, pace, or 85th-percentile speeds; and (e) Pace — the 10 mph speed range representing the speeds of the largest percentage of vehicles in the traffic stream.
250. Speed Limit
The maximum (or minimum) speed applicable to a section of highway as established by law or regulation.
250a. Speed Measurement Markings
A white transverse pavement marking placed on the roadway to assist enforcement of speed regulations.
251. Speed Zone
A section of highway with a speed limit established by law or regulation, which might differ from a legislatively specified statutory speed limit.
252. Splitter Island
A median island used to separate opposing directions of traffic entering and exiting a roundabout.
252a. State Highway
Any highway owned and operated by Caltrans.
253. Station Crossing
A pathway grade crossing associated with a station platform.
254. Statutory Speed Limit
A speed limit established by legislative action (Federal or State law) typically applicable to a particular class of highways with specified design, functional, jurisdictional, and/or location characteristics, not necessarily displayed on Speed Limit signs.
255. Steady (Steady Mode)
The continuous display of a signal indication for the duration of an interval, signal phase, or consecutive signal phases.
256. Stop Line
A solid white pavement marking line extending across approach lanes indicating the point at which a stop is intended or required; for all purposes, limit line(s) as defined by CVC § 377 mean stop line(s).
257. Street
See Highway. Per CVC §§ 590 and 591, a "street" is a way or place of whatever nature, publicly maintained and open to public use for vehicular travel.
258. Supplemental Signal Face
A signal face that is not a primary signal face but is provided for a given approach or separate turning movement to enhance visibility or conspicuity.
259. Swing Gate
A self-closing fence-type gate designed to swing open away from the track area and return to the closed position upon release.
260. Symbol
The approved design of a pictorial or graphical representation of a specific traffic control message for signs, pavement markings, traffic control signals, or other devices, as shown in the MUTCD.
261. Temporary Traffic Control Signal
See Highway Traffic Signal.
262. Temporary Traffic Control Zone
An area of a highway, pedestrian, or bicycle facility where road user conditions are changed because of a work zone or incident through the use of temporary traffic control devices, flaggers, uniformed law enforcement officers, or other authorized personnel.
263. Theoretical Gore
A longitudinal point at the upstream end of a neutral area at an exit ramp or channelized turn lane where the channelizing lines separating it from the adjacent through lane(s) begin to diverge, or at the downstream end of a neutral area at an entrance ramp or channelized entering lane where the channelizing lines intersect.
263a. Through Highway
A highway or portion thereof at whose entrance vehicular traffic from intersecting highways is regulated by stop signs or traffic control signals, or is controlled, when entering on a separated right-turn roadway, by a yield-right-of-way sign.
264. Through Train
A train movement that continues without stopping or reversing direction throughout the entire length of the rail traffic detection circuit approaching a highway-rail grade crossing.
265. Timed Exit Gate Operating Mode
A mode of operation where the exit gate descent at a grade crossing is based on a predetermined time interval.
266. Toll Booth
A shelter where a toll attendant is stationed to collect tolls or issue toll tickets, typically located adjacent to a toll lane on a toll island.
267. Toll Collection
Manual or electronic methods and elements used to collect a fee for use of a toll facility. Methods include: (a) Electronic Toll Collection (ETC) — a cashless system for automated collection of tolls from moving or stopped vehicles via wireless technologies (radio-frequency communication or optical scanning); classified as systems requiring registered toll accounts (via transponder, barcode decal, or license plate scanning to automatically deduct the toll), systems not requiring registered accounts (license plates optically scanned and invoices mailed to the vehicle owner), or systems allowing both; (b) Open-Road Tolling (ORT) — a system allowing ETC from vehicles traveling at posted speeds, possibly used with toll plazas, and typically also used on managed lanes and ETC-only facilities; and (c) Manual Toll Collection — collection from stopped vehicles via cash, toll tickets, tokens, or credit cards (possibly with receipts), by machine or toll booth attendant, including: (1) Toll-Ticket System — the user stops to receive a ticket denoting the point of entry, and upon exit surrenders the ticket and is charged based on distance traveled; (2) Attended Lane (Manual Lane) — a toll lane adjacent to a toll booth occupied by a human toll collector who makes change, issues receipts, and performs related functions, typically requiring vehicles to stop; and (3) Exact Change Lane (Automatic Lane) — a non-attended toll lane with a receptacle for depositing coins totaling the exact toll amount, typically requiring vehicles to stop.
268. Toll Island
A raised island on which a toll booth or other toll collection and related equipment is located.
269. Toll Lane
An individual lane within a toll plaza in which a toll payment is collected or, for toll-ticket systems, a toll ticket is issued.
270. Toll Plaza
The location where tolls are collected, consisting of a grouping of toll booths, toll islands, toll lanes, and typically a canopy; might be located on highway mainlines (sometimes called a barrier toll plaza because it interrupts traffic flow) or on interchange ramps.
271. Toll Road (Facility)
A road or facility open to traffic only by payment of a user toll or fee. A "toll highway" or "toll road" is a publicly owned way or place open to public use for vehicular travel, use of which requires payment of a fee (CVC § 611).
272. Traffic
Pedestrians, bicyclists, ridden or herded animals, vehicles, streetcars, and other conveyances, singly or together, while using any highway or site roadway open to public travel for purposes of travel. Per CVC § 620, "traffic" includes pedestrians, ridden animals, vehicles, street cars, and other conveyances, singly or together, while using any highway for purposes of travel.
273. Traffic Control Device
All signs, signals, markings, channelization devices, or other devices that use colors, shapes, symbols, words, sounds, and/or tactile information for the primary purpose of communicating a regulatory, warning, or guidance message to road users on a street, highway, pedestrian facility, bikeway, pathway, or site roadway open to public travel. See Section 1A.02 for items that are not traffic control devices.
274. Traffic Control Signal (Traffic Signal)
See Highway Traffic Signal.
275. Train
One or more coupled locomotives, with or without cars, that operates on rails or tracks and to which all other traffic must yield the right-of-way by law at highway-rail grade crossings.
276. Transverse Markings
Pavement markings generally placed perpendicular and across the flow of traffic, such as shoulder markings, word/symbol/arrow markings, stop lines, crosswalk lines, parking space markings, and others.
277. Traveled Way
The portion of the roadway for the movement of vehicles, exclusive of the shoulders, berms, sidewalks, and parking lanes.
278. Turn Bay
A lane for the exclusive use of turning vehicles formed on the approach to the location where the turn is to be made; drivers who wish to turn must generally move out of a through lane into the newly-formed turn bay. A through lane that becomes a turn lane is a lane drop, not a turn bay.
279. Two-Stage Bicycle Turn Box
A designated area at an intersection intended to provide bicyclists a place to wait for traffic to clear before proceeding in a different direction of travel.
280. Uncontrolled Approach
An approach on which vehicles are not controlled by a traffic control signal, hybrid beacon, STOP sign, or YIELD sign.
281. Upstream
A location encountered by traffic prior to a downstream location as it flows in an "upstream to downstream" direction. Example: the upstream end of a lane line separating a turn lane from a through lane on an intersection approach is the end furthest from the intersection.
282. Urban Street
A street type normally characterized by relatively low speeds, wide ranges of traffic volumes, narrower lanes, frequent intersections and driveways, significant pedestrian traffic, and more businesses and houses.
283. Variable Message Sign
See Changeable Message Sign.
284. Vehicle
Every device in, upon, or by which any person or property can be transported or drawn upon a highway, except trains and light rail transit operating in exclusive or semi-exclusive alignments; LRT equipment in a mixed-use alignment, to which other traffic is not required to yield by law, is a vehicle. Per CVC § 670, a "vehicle" is a device by which any person or property may be propelled, moved, or drawn upon a highway, except a device moved exclusively by human power or used exclusively on stationary rails or tracks.
285. Vibrotactile Pedestrian Device
An accessible pedestrian signal feature that communicates, by touch, information about pedestrian timing using a vibrating surface.
286. Visibility-Limited Signal Face or Visibility-Limited Signal Section
A signal face or section designed (or shielded, hooded, or louvered) to restrict visibility of a signal indication from the side, to a certain lane or lanes, or to a certain distance from the stop line.
287. Walk Interval
An interval during which the WALKING PERSON (symbolizing WALK) signal indication is displayed.
288. Warning Light
A portable, powered, yellow, lens-directed, enclosed light used in a temporary traffic control zone in either a steady-burn or flashing mode.
289. Warning Sign
A sign that gives notice to road users of a situation that might not be readily apparent.
290. Warrant
A threshold condition based on average or normal conditions that, if satisfied as part of an engineering study, results in analysis of other traffic conditions or factors to determine whether a traffic control device or other improvement is justified. Warrants are not a substitute for engineering judgment; meeting a warrant is not conclusive justification for installing the device.
291. Wayside Horn System
A stationary horn (or series of horns) located at a grade crossing, used with train-activated or LRT-activated warning systems to provide audible warning of approaching rail traffic to road users on the highway or pathway approaches, as a supplement or alternative to sounding a locomotive horn.
292. Worker
A person on foot whose duties place them within the right-of-way of a street, highway, or pathway, such as construction and maintenance forces, survey crews, utility crews, incident responders, and law enforcement personnel directing traffic, investigating crashes, or handling lane closures, obstructed roadways, and disasters within the right-of-way.
293. Wrong-Way Arrow
A slender, elongated, white pavement marking arrow placed upstream from the ramp terminus to indicate the correct direction of traffic flow, intended primarily to warn wrong-way road users that they are going in the wrong direction.
294. Yellow Change Interval
The first interval following the green or flashing arrow interval during which the steady yellow signal indication is displayed.
295. Yield Line
A row of solid white isosceles triangles pointing toward approaching vehicles, extending across approach lanes to indicate the point at which the yield is intended or required to be made.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 1C

  • Section 1C.01 is the single most important paragraph in the entire Manual to memorize: it defines exactly what Standard, Guidance, Option, and Support mean, and every other chapter's callouts map directly to it.
  • Section 1C.02's glossary (295 base terms, plus lettered California additions) is the authoritative definition source whenever a term used elsewhere in the Manual seems ambiguous or conflicts with everyday usage — the definition here governs for MUTCD purposes.
  • Many terms cross-reference the California Vehicle Code directly (e.g., Crosswalk, Highway, Intersection, Pedestrian, Vehicle) — when CVC and colloquial usage diverge, the CVC-cited language controls for legal purposes in California.
  • "Engineering Judgment" requires no written documentation, but "Engineering Study" must be documented in writing — a distinction that matters throughout the Manual whenever a Standard allows a documented deviation.
  • "Site Roadways Open to Public Travel" is a load-bearing definition that determines where the MUTCD applies at all (shopping centers, office parks, airports, schools, etc.) and explicitly excludes gated-access roads, private grade crossings, parking areas, and driving aisles.
  • Section 1C.03 is the key to decoding every acronym in this Manual and in California-specific traffic engineering practice (Caltrans, CTCDC, TOPD, SWITRS, etc.).
