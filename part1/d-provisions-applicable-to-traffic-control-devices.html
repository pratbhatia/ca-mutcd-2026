Part 1 · General · Chapter 1D

Provisions Applicable to Traffic Control Devices in General

Sets out the cross-cutting rules that apply to every traffic control device regardless of type: purpose and design principles, jurisdictional authority, engineering study/judgment, the 13-color code, public-domain/copyright status, advertising prohibition, standard abbreviations, and placement, maintenance, and crashworthiness requirements.

1D.01Purpose and Principles of Traffic Control Devices

Support · Guidance · Standard — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01The purpose of traffic control devices, and the principles for their use, is to promote highway safety, inclusion, and mobility of all road users, and efficiency, by providing for the orderly movement of road users on streets, highways, bikeways, and site roadways open to public travel throughout the Nation. See Section 1A.03 for additional information on target road users.
  2. 02This Manual contains the basic principles that govern the design and use of traffic control devices for all streets, highways, bikeways, and site roadways open to public travel (see Section 1C.02), regardless of type or class or the public agency, official, or owner having jurisdiction. The Manual specifies restrictions on the use of a device intended for limited application or for a specific system. These principles must be given primary consideration in the selection and application of each device.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03To be effective, a traffic control device should: (A) fulfill a need; (B) command attention; (C) convey a clear, simple meaning; (D) command respect from road users; and (E) give adequate time for proper response.
  2. 04Design, placement, operation, maintenance, and uniformity are aspects that should be carefully considered by the engineer to maximize a device's ability to be consistent with the five principles in Paragraph 3. Vehicle speed, geometrics, other relevant factors, and road-user types should be carefully considered as elements that govern the design, operation, placement, and location of various traffic control devices.
  3. 05The proper use of traffic control devices should provide the road user with the information necessary to safely, efficiently, and lawfully use the streets, highways, pedestrian facilities, and bikeways.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06Traffic control devices used on site roadways open to public travel shall have the same shape, color, and meaning as those required by the MUTCD for use on public highways, except as provided otherwise elsewhere in this Manual. Sign size exceptions are noted in each Part as applicable.

1D.02Responsibility and Authority for Traffic Control Devices

Standard · Support · Standard · Support · Standard · Option · Standard · Support · Option · Support · Standard · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The responsibility for the design, placement, operation, maintenance, and uniformity of traffic control devices in compliance with this Manual shall rest with the public agency or official having jurisdiction, or, for site roadways open to public travel, with the private owner or private official having jurisdiction.
  2. 02All regulatory traffic control devices shall be supported by laws, ordinances, or regulations.
  3. 03Traffic control devices, public announcements or notices, and other signs or messages within the highway right-of-way shall be placed only as authorized by a public authority or official having jurisdiction, or, for site roadways or private toll roads open to public travel, by the private owner or private official having jurisdiction, for the purpose of regulating, warning, or guiding traffic.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03aSee Sections 1A.01, 1B.01, and 1C.02 (phrase "Site Roadways Open to Public Travel") for authority and applicability of the CA MUTCD on various types of public and private roadway facilities.
  2. 03bDelegation of maintenance activities to local authorities is usually exercised under the authority of Streets and Highways Code § 130.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04When the public agency or official having jurisdiction over a street or highway — or, for site roadways open to public travel, the private owner or private official having jurisdiction — has granted proper authority, others such as contractors and public utility companies shall be allowed to install temporary traffic control devices in temporary traffic control zones. Such devices shall comply with the provisions of this Manual.
  2. 05Signs and other devices that do not have any traffic control purpose that are placed within the highway right-of-way shall not be located where they will interfere with, or detract from, traffic control devices.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06States are encouraged to adopt, through policy or legislation, the provisions of 23 CFR 750.108 that restrict outdoor advertising from resembling traffic control devices.
  2. 07CVC references are used throughout this California MUTCD when the subject matter relates to State law.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 08CVC § 21400 provides that Caltrans shall, after consultation with local agencies and public hearings, adopt rules and regulations prescribing uniform standards and specifications for all official traffic control devices placed pursuant to the Code.
  2. 09CVC § 21401 provides that only official traffic control devices conforming to the uniform standards and specifications promulgated by Caltrans shall be placed upon a street or highway.
  3. 10CVC §§ 21350 and 21351 give basic authority to Caltrans and local authorities, in their respective jurisdictions, to place and maintain official traffic control devices.
Option — Permissive
  1. 11Local authorities may adopt rules and regulations by ordinance or resolution for regulating traffic by means of official traffic control devices meeting the requirements of CVC § 21400 (see CVC § 21100(d)).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 12Except as provided in Paragraph 14, local agencies responsible for the development or operation of bikeways or roadways where bicycle travel is permitted shall: (A) utilize the minimum safety design criteria established by Caltrans for planning and construction of bikeways and roadways where bicycle travel is permitted; and (B) utilize the uniform specifications and symbols for signs, markers, and traffic control devices established by Caltrans to designate bikeways, regulate traffic, improve safety and convenience for bicyclists, and alert pedestrians and motorists to the presence of bicyclists on bikeways and roadways where bicycle travel is permitted.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 13See Streets and Highways Code §§ 890.4, 890.6, 890.8, and 891.
Option — Permissive
  1. 14Local agencies may utilize minimum safety design criteria other than those established by Caltrans (see Streets and Highways Code §§ 890.6 and 891) if all of the following conditions are met: (A) the alternative criteria have been reviewed and approved by a qualified engineer considering the unique characteristics and features of the proposed bikeway and surrounding environs; (B) the alternative criteria, or the project description referencing them, are adopted by resolution at a public meeting after proper notice and opportunity for public comment; and (C) the alternative criteria adhere to guidelines established by a national association of public agency transportation officials.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 15The use of unauthorized traffic control devices is prohibited by CVC § 21465.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 16Prohibited traffic control devices constitute a public nuisance and shall be removed per CVC § 21467.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 17This does not modify or limit the authority of the Public Utilities Commission to erect or maintain traffic control devices as authorized by law (see CVC § 21468).
  2. 18Private advertising is prohibited on any highway right-of-way by § 5403(a) of the Business and Professions Code. "Highway" in this context includes roads, streets, boulevards, lanes, courts, places, commons, trails, ways, or other rights-of-way or easements used for or laid out and intended for public passage of vehicles or of vehicles and persons (§ 5213 of the Business and Professions Code). Also see CVC § 360 for the definition of "highway."
  3. 19The California Public Utilities Commission is the state regulatory agency with statutory authority over highway-rail grade crossings and highway-light rail transit grade crossings (see Public Utilities Code § 1202(a)).

1D.03Engineering Study and Engineering Judgment

Support · Standard · Support · Guidance · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Definitions of professional engineer, engineering study, and engineering judgment are provided in Section 1C.02.
  2. 01aSee CVC § 627 for the definition and requirements of "Engineering and Traffic Survey," abbreviated in this manual as E&TS.
  3. 02The application of engineering study and engineering judgment is a fundamental principle of the use of traffic control devices. For this reason, in most cases, the selection of a particular device is not required by a Standard provision but is determined by engineering study or engineering judgment. Many Standard provisions specifically require, explicitly or by implication, the application of engineering study or judgment. Site-specific conditions might make it impossible or impracticable to comply with a Standard at that location; in such limited cases, a deviation is allowed provided the agency or official having jurisdiction fully documents, through an engineering study, the engineering basis for the deviation.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03This Manual describes the application of traffic control devices, but shall not be a legal requirement for their installation.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04The MUTCD does not mandate, or intend to imply, that an engineer must make the final decision whether to implement or execute a determination or advice by installing or constructing the device to specification in the field. Rather, the engineer (or an individual under supervision, or other individual duly authorized by State law to practice engineering) develops an engineering-based solution including specifications for selection and placement of devices, but responsibility for the final decision to implement that solution rests with the agency having jurisdiction over the roadway, after consultation with and based on advice from the engineer.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05The decision to use a particular device at a particular location should be made on the basis of either an engineering study or the application of engineering judgment by an engineer, someone under the direct supervision of an engineer, or other individual duly authorized by State law to practice engineering. This Manual should not be considered a substitute for engineering judgment, which should be exercised in the selection and application of traffic control devices, as well as in the location and design of the roads and streets the devices complement.
  2. 06Early in the location and design process for roads and streets, engineers should coordinate that location and design with the design and placement of the traffic control devices to be used with them.
  3. 07Jurisdictions, or owners of site roadways or private toll roads open to public travel, with traffic control responsibility but without an engineer on staff trained and/or experienced in traffic control devices should seek engineering assistance from others, such as the State transportation agency, their county, a nearby large city, or a traffic engineering consultant.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08The provisions of this Manual are intended to be interpreted and applied by engineers or those under an engineer's supervision. The construction of the provisions is informed by the bases referenced in Paragraphs 9 and 10.
  2. 09The National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying (NCEES) has defined the practice of engineering as "any service or creative work requiring engineering education, training, and experience in the application of engineering principles and the interpretation of engineering data to engineering activities that potentially impact the health, safety, and welfare of the public." The practice of engineering is therefore subject to regulation in the public interest and regulated by State licensing boards to safeguard health, safety, and welfare. NCEES defines an engineer as "an individual who is qualified to practice engineering by reason of engineering education, training, and experience in the application of engineering principles and the interpretation of engineering data."
  3. 10The U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM) defines the professional knowledge of engineering as "the comprehensive, in-depth knowledge of mathematical, physical, and engineering sciences applicable to a specialty field of engineering that characterizes a full 4-year engineering program leading to a bachelor's degree, or the equivalent," and the professional ability to apply engineering knowledge as the ability to (a) apply fundamental and diversified professional engineering concepts, theories, and practices with versatility, judgment, and perception; (b) adapt and apply methods and techniques of related scientific disciplines; and (c) organize, analyze, interpret, and evaluate scientific data in solving engineering problems.
  4. 11Requisite technical training in applying MUTCD principles might be available from the State's Local Technical Assistance Program (LTAP) for needed engineering guidance and assistance.

1D.04Design of Traffic Control Devices

Guidance · Standard · Option · Guidance · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Devices should be designed so that features such as size, shape, color, composition, lighting or retroreflection, and contrast combine to draw attention to the device; so that size, shape, color, and simplicity of message combine to produce a clear meaning; so that legibility and size combine with placement to provide adequate response time; and so that uniformity, size, legibility, and reasonableness of message combine to command respect.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01aTraffic control devices shall comply with CVC § 21466.5 for light brilliance so as not to impair the vision of drivers.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02Except for symbols and colors, minor modifications in the specific design elements of a device may be made based on an engineering study or engineering judgment, in accordance with Paragraph 3, provided the essential appearance characteristics are preserved.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Aspects of the standard design of a traffic control device should not be modified unless there is a demonstrated need in unusual circumstances, based on an engineering study or engineering judgment.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04An example of acceptably modifying a device's design would be modifying the Combination Horizontal Alignment/Intersection (W1-10) sign to show intersecting side roads on both sides rather than on just one side of the major road within the curve.

1D.06Public Domain, Copyrights, and Patents

Standard · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Traffic control device design or application provisions in this Manual shall be in the public domain. Traffic control devices in this Manual shall not be protected by a patent, trademark, or copyright, except for the Interstate Shield, the 511 Travel Information pictograph, the National Scenic Byway graphic, and any items under the stewardship of or owned by FHWA. The Caltrans logos (the "CT" symbol and the "Caltrans" logotype) are registered service marks and, when used on any traffic control device, shall be presented in a uniform and consistent manner per Caltrans' Deputy Directive DD-33-R1.
  2. 02A traffic control device design or application shall not be eligible for official experimentation (see Section 1B.05) or interim approval (see Section 1B.07) unless it is in the public domain. Express abandonment of any and all forms of proprietary protection — patents, trademarks, or copyrights — related to the device's design and application shall satisfy the public-domain requirement.
  3. 03The requirement for the traffic control device to be in the public domain shall not apply to individual components used in the assembly or manufacture of the device.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04The limitation on patented, trademarked, or copyrighted traffic control devices applies to the message the device conveys to the road user. If a patent or other protection covers the device's communication to the road user through its appearance, audible message, or other aspects of the conveyed message (such as the order in which signal indications change from green to yellow to red), the device is considered protected and not in the public domain, and is precluded from inclusion in this Manual. This limitation exists to ensure uniformity of messaging of individually approved devices. It does not apply to other aspects of a device (internal controls, circuitry, electronics, mechanics, or housing) so long as the appearance, audible message, or other conveyed aspects remain freely reproducible without infringing proprietary rights. This Manual does not prohibit such other aspects (or components, parts, or manufacturing processes) from being protected through patent, trademark, or copyright. Examples of acceptable protected components include sign sheeting or retroreflectivity technology, internal electronic components of traffic signal controllers, and breakaway sign support mechanisms.
  2. 05Pictographs, as defined in Section 1C.02, are embedded in traffic control devices, but pictographs themselves are not considered traffic control devices for the purposes of Paragraph 4.
  3. 06Business identification logos, as defined in Section 1C.02, are embedded in traffic control devices, but the logos themselves are not considered traffic control devices for the purposes of Paragraph 4.

1D.07Advertising

Standard · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Traffic control devices or their supports shall not bear any advertising message or any other message not related to traffic control.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Acknowledgment signs (see Section 2H.13), Specific Service signs (see Chapter 2J), and Tourist-Oriented Directional signs (see Chapter 2K) are not considered advertising.

1D.09Placement and Operation of Traffic Control Devices

Standard · Option · Guidance · Support · Guidance · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Before any highway, site roadway open to public travel (see Section 1C.02), detour, or temporary route is opened to public travel, all traffic control devices necessary for safe operation shall be in place. Detour signs shall be erected at the nearest points of detour from the portion of a highway, or any bridge, closed to traffic while under construction or repair, per CVC § 21363 and Section 6I.02.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02Temporary traffic control devices, as provided for in Part 6 of this Manual, may be used in place of permanent devices that have yet to be installed for safe operation.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Placement of a traffic control device should be within the road user's view so that adequate visibility is provided. To aid in conveying the proper meaning, the device should be appropriately positioned with respect to the location, object, or situation to which it applies. The location and legibility of the device should be such that a road user has adequate time to make the proper response in both day and night conditions.
  2. 04Traffic control devices should be placed and operated in a uniform and consistent manner as part of maintaining uniformity in traffic control.
  3. 04aTraffic control devices used on a part-time basis should be in operation only during the time periods that they are required.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05Inconsistent placement or use of a device can result in disrespect for the device at locations where it is needed and appropriate.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06Unnecessary traffic control devices should be removed. The fact that a device is in good physical condition should not be a basis for deferring needed removal or change.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 07Section 2A.02 contains information on excessive use of signs and other considerations that can reduce the effectiveness of signs and other traffic control devices.

1D.10Maintenance of Traffic Control Devices

Guidance · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Functional maintenance of traffic control devices should be used to determine if certain devices need to be changed to meet current traffic conditions.
  2. 02Physical maintenance of traffic control devices should be performed to retain the legibility and visibility of the device, and to retain its proper functioning.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Clean, legible, properly mounted devices in good working condition command the respect of road users.

1D.11Crashworthiness of Traffic Control Devices and Other Roadside Appurtenances

Standard · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01In accordance with various Sections of this Manual, certain traffic control devices and their supports, and/or related appurtenances shall be crashworthy (see Section 1C.02). Crashworthiness provisions in this Manual shall apply to all streets, highways, and site roadways open to public travel.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Roadside appurtenances include permanent and portable sign supports, other permanent or temporary traffic control devices, and other roadside fixtures that are not traffic control devices — such as longitudinal barriers, bridge railings, and crash cushions — within the clear zone. Crashworthiness of a device or appurtenance is determined by nationally established standards such as the "Manual for Assessing Safety Hardware" (MASH), 2016, AASHTO. Information on FHWA's crashworthiness policy for devices on the National Highway System and other roadways is available at the FHWA Office of Safety website.
  2. 03See Caltrans' Traffic Safety Systems Manual and its Supplement for uniform practices and guidance on traffic safety systems. See Section 1A.05 for information regarding this publication.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 1D

  • Every traffic control device must serve one of five effectiveness principles — fulfill a need, command attention, convey a clear/simple meaning, command respect, and give adequate response time (1D.01) — and responsibility for design, placement, operation, maintenance, and uniformity rests with the public agency, official, or private owner having jurisdiction (1D.02).
  • Device selection is normally governed by engineering study (documented in writing) or engineering judgment (no documentation required) rather than by a rigid Standard requirement — this Manual is explicitly not itself a legal requirement for installation (1D.03).
  • Only 11 of the Manual's 13 official colors currently carry an assigned general meaning; Coral and Light Blue remain reserved for future FHWA-determined uses (1D.05).
  • Traffic control devices in this Manual must be public-domain and free of patent/trademark/copyright protection on their road-user-facing message — with narrow named exceptions (Interstate Shield, 511 pictograph, National Scenic Byway graphic, Caltrans' "CT"/"Caltrans" service marks) — and may never carry advertising (1D.06–1D.07).
  • Word-message abbreviations are tightly controlled: Table 1D-1 abbreviations are of general application, Table 1D-2 abbreviations are reserved for portable changeable message signs only (unless paired with a prompt word), and Table 1D-3 abbreviations are prohibited outright due to documented misreadings.
  • Devices and their supports/appurtenances within the clear zone must meet nationally recognized crashworthiness standards (MASH 2016, AASHTO) per 1D.11.
