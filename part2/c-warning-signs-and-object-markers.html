Part 2 · Signs · Chapter 2C

Warning Signs and Object Markers

Design, sizing, placement, and application requirements for the W-series warning signs, supplemental warning plaques, and object markers used to alert road users to unexpected conditions on California streets and highways.

2C.01 Application of Warning Signs

Standard · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The use of warning signs shall be based on an engineering study or on engineering judgment.
  2. 02Warning signs shall be retroreflective or illuminated (see Section 2A.21).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03The use of warning signs should be kept to a minimum, as unnecessary use tends to breed disrespect for all signs. Where the condition or activity is seasonal or temporary, the warning sign should be removed or covered when the condition or activity does not exist.

In practice: because warning-sign overuse is explicitly called out as counterproductive, an engineer asked to add a warning sign should first confirm the condition is genuinely unexpected to a reasonably alert road user, not merely a generic hazard.

2C.02 Design of Warning Signs

Standard · Option · Support · Standard · Option · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Paragraph 2 of this Section, or unless specifically designated otherwise, all warning signs shall be diamond-shaped (a square with one diagonal vertical) with a black legend and border on a yellow background. Warning signs shall be designed per the sizes, shapes, colors, and legends in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (see Section 1A.05) and Caltrans' California Sign Specifications.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02A warning sign larger than the size shown in the Oversized column of Table 2C-1 or Table 2C-1(CA) for that sign may be diamond-shaped, rectangular, or square.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03A shape other than diamond is typical for overhead installations.
  2. 04Section 2A.05 describes allowable methods to accommodate a diamond-shaped warning sign where lateral space is constrained — urban locations, narrow median barriers, or adjacent retaining walls — including displaying the standard legend in a vertically oriented rectangle.
  3. 05Use of LEDs in the border and legend of warning signs is described in Section 2A.12.
Option — Permissive
  1. 06Except for symbols on warning signs, minor design modifications may be made provided the essential appearance characteristics are met. Modifications may be made to the symbols on combined horizontal alignment/intersection signs (Section 2C.09) and intersection warning signs (Section 2C.41) to approximate the geometric configuration of the intersecting roadway(s).
  2. 07Word-message warning signs other than those provided in this Manual may be developed by Caltrans (per CVC § 21400) and installed by State or local highway agencies for conditions not otherwise addressed by standard signs (see Section 2A.04).
  3. 08Warning signs regarding conditions associated with pedestrians, bicyclists, and playgrounds, and their related plaques, may have a black legend and border on a yellow or fluorescent yellow-green background.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09Warning signs regarding conditions associated with school buses and schools, and their related supplemental plaques, shall have a black legend and border on a fluorescent yellow-green background (see Section 7B.01).
Option — Permissive
  1. 10Consistent with Section 4S.03, a Warning Beacon may be used in combination with a standard warning sign.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 11Refer to Section 2A.09 for the use of educational plaques.

2C.03 Size of Warning Signs and Plaques

Standard · Support · Standard · Option · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Section 2A.07, warning sign sizes shall be as shown in Table 2C-1 and Table 2C-1(CA).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01aRefer to Paragraph 2 of Section 2C.02 and Paragraph 6 of this Section for sizes larger than shown in Tables 2C-1 and 2C-1(CA).
  2. 02Section 2A.07 explains the applicability of the various columns in Table 2C-1.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Except as provided in Paragraph 5, the minimum size for all diamond-shaped warning signs facing traffic on a multi-lane conventional road where the posted speed limit is higher than 35 mph shall be 36 x 36 inches.
  2. 04The minimum size for supplemental warning plaques not included in Table 2C-1 shall be as shown in Table 2C-2.
Option — Permissive
  1. 05If a diamond-shaped warning sign is placed on the left-hand side of a multi-lane roadway to supplement the same sign on the right-hand side, the minimum size in the Single Lane column of Table 2C-1 may be used.
  2. 06Signs and plaques larger than those in Tables 2C-1 and 2C-2 may be used (see Section 2A.11).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07The minimum size for diamond-shaped warning signs facing traffic on exit and entrance ramps at major interchanges connecting an Expressway or Freeway with an Expressway or Freeway (see Section 2E.11) should be the size shown for the mainline roadway classification. If no size is given in the Freeway column, use the Expressway size; if neither is given, use the Oversized size.
  2. 08The minimum size for diamond-shaped warning signs facing traffic on exit and entrance ramps at all other interchanges (see Section 2E.11) should be 36 x 36 inches.

2C.04 Placement of Warning Signs

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Placement guidance is in Sections 2A.13 through 2A.18.
  2. 02The time needed for detection, recognition, decision, and reaction is the Perception-Response Time (PRT). Table 2C-3 aids in determining warning sign location; its distances can be adjusted for roadway features, other signing, and to improve visibility.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Warning signs should be placed to provide adequate PRT; the distances in Table 2C-3 should be applied with engineering judgment.
  2. 04Minimum spacing between warning signs with different messages should be based on estimated PRT for driver comprehension of, and reaction to, the second sign.
  3. 05Placement effectiveness should be periodically evaluated under both day and night conditions.

2C.05 Horizontal Alignment Warning Signs — General

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01A variety of horizontal alignment warning signs (see Figure 2C-1), pavement markings (Chapter 3B), and delineation (Chapter 3G) advise motorists of a change in roadway alignment. Uniform application relative to the amount of alignment change conveys a consistent message, establishing driver expectancy and promoting effective roadway operations. Sections 2C.05 through 2C.12 address the design and application of horizontal alignment warning signs.
  2. 02Treatments used in advance of or within a change in horizontal alignment include: (A) Horizontal alignment signs — Turn (W1-1), Curve (W1-2, W1-10 series, W1-11, W1-13, W1-15), Reverse Turn (W1-3), Reverse Curve (W1-4), Winding Road (W1-5), Exit Speed (W13-2), Ramp Speed (W13-3), and Combination Horizontal Alignment/Advisory Exit or Ramp Speed (W13-6 through W13-11) (Sections 2C.07, 2C.09, 2C.12); (B) Advisory Speed (W13-1P) plaque (Section 2C.59); (C) Chevron Alignment (W1-8) signs (Section 2C.08); (D) Delineators (Chapter 3G); (E) One-Direction Large Arrow (W1-6) sign (Section 2C.10); (F) Raised retroreflective pavement markers (Sections 3B.15–3B.17); (G) Sign or marking conspicuity enhancements (Section 2A.11); (H) Wide edge lines (Section 3A.04); (I) Pavement word, symbol, and arrow markings (Sections 3B.20–3B.22); (J) Rumble strips (Chapter 3K); (K) Vehicle Speed Feedback Sign (Section 2C.13); and (L) Speed reduction markings (Section 3B.28).
  3. 03Considerations other than traffic control devices — improved surface friction (high-friction surface treatments), pavement edge treatments, lighting improvements, increased superelevation, and rumble strips — might also be used in advance of or within an alignment change.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04Except as provided in Section 2C.06, device selection for a horizontal alignment change should consider one or more of: (A) approach speed; (B) recommended advisory speed for the change; (C) the difference between speed limit and advisory speed (speed differential); (D) daily traffic volumes; (E) the typical mix of vehicle types; (F) sight distance through the change; (G) other traffic control devices already used on the same roadway segment; (H) crash history of the change; and (I) presence of driveways or intersections within the curve radius.

2C.70 Object Marker Design and Placement Height

Support · Standard · Support · Guidance · Option · Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Types 1, 2, and 3 object markers mark obstructions within or adjacent to the roadway. Type 4 object markers mark the end of a roadway.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02When used, object markers (Figures 2C-17 and 2C-17(CA)) shall not have a border and shall be one or more of the following types: Type 1 — a diamond-shaped sign at least 18 inches on a side, either a yellow (OM1-1) or black (OM1-2) sign with nine yellow retroreflective devices (minimum 3-inch diameter each) mounted symmetrically, or an all-yellow retroreflective sign (OM1-3). Type 2 — either a marker (OM2-1V or OM2-1H) with three yellow retroreflective devices (minimum 3-inch diameter each) arranged horizontally or vertically on a white sign at least 6 x 12 inches, or an all-yellow horizontal/vertical retroreflective sign (OM2-2V or OM2-2H) at least 6 x 12 inches. Type 3 — a striped marker, 12 x 36 inches, a vertical rectangle with alternating black and retroreflective yellow stripes sloping downward at 45 degrees toward the side traffic is to pass; minimum stripe width 3 inches. Type 4 — a diamond-shaped sign at least 18 inches on a side, either a red (OM4-1) or black (OM4-2) sign with nine red retroreflective devices (minimum 3-inch diameter each) mounted symmetrically, or an all-red retroreflective sign (OM4-3). Type L(CA) Utility Pole marker (Figure 2C-17(CA)) — yellow retroreflective material consisting of three 2 x 12 inch horizontal rectangles arranged vertically on a utility pole. Type Q(CA) object marker (Figure 2C-17(CA)) — a vertical tubular marker 18 to 24 inches high with a minimum cross-sectional dimension of 2¼ inches; the yellow retroreflective material shall consist of three 3-inch bands or a single 9-inch band.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Type 3 object markers with stripes beginning at the upper right and sloping down to the lower left are right object markers (OM3-R). Those beginning at the upper left and sloping down to the lower right are left object markers (OM3-L). Those with chevron stripes sloping down to both lower corners are center object markers (OM3-C).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04When marking obstructions within the roadway, or obstructions 8 feet or less from the shoulder or curb, the minimum mounting height (from the bottom of the marker to the near edge of the traveled way) should be 4 feet.
  2. 05When marking obstructions more than 8 feet from the shoulder or curb, the clearance from the ground to the bottom of the marker should be at least 4 feet.
  3. 06Object markers should not present a vertical or horizontal clearance obstacle for pedestrians.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07Where object markers or markings apply to an obstruction that by its nature requires a lower or higher mounting, vertical mounting height may vary according to need.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Section 9C.09 covers use of object markers on shared-use paths.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 2C

  • Warning signs are diamond-shaped, black-on-yellow (or black-on-fluorescent-yellow-green for pedestrian/bicycle/school/playground conditions) by default, sized from Table 2C-1/2C-1(CA), and should be used sparingly — overuse breeds sign disrespect.
  • Device selection for horizontal curves is not discretionary: Table 2C-4 Chart A (AADT/roadway type) determines whether a device is needed, and Chart B (speed differential) dictates whether pavement markings, an advance warning sign, delineators, or chevrons are required.
  • Table 2C-3's advance-placement distances are built from Perception-Response Time and AASHTO stopping/decision-sight-distance models; Condition A (lane-change/merge signs) and Condition B (potential-stop vs. speed-reduction signs) use different PRT and deceleration assumptions, so pick the correct condition column, not just the posted speed row.
  • Chevron spacing (Table 2C-5) and the Advisory Speed plaque requirement (Table 2C-6) both key off curve radius/advisory speed and speed differential — 15 mph or more differential makes the W13-1P plaque mandatory, not optional.
  • Several federal sign/size table entries were struck by California amendment in this edition — the W2-10/W2-11 Actuated Advance Intersection pair, the W4-8/W9-4 Lanes Merge/Single-Lane Transition pairing, the W16-13P WHEN FLASHING plaque, and the W25-1/2 Extended Green pair no longer have a dedicated Table 2C-1 row (WHEN FLASHING is also affirmatively barred from several sign assemblies elsewhere in the chapter).
  • Object markers come in four standard types (yellow/black Type 1 and red/black Type 4 diamonds, white/yellow Type 2, striped Type 3) plus two California-only types (Type L utility pole banding, Type Q tubular marker); Types 1 and 4 are reserved for in-roadway and end-of-roadway use respectively and must not be used for adjacent obstructions.
  • Supplemental warning plaques always ride on the same post as, and normally below, the sign they supplement — they are never freestanding — and must match that sign's legend/border/background colors (or black-on-yellow when paired with a regulatory sign).
