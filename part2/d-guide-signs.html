Guide Signs — Conventional Roads

Design, lettering, route markers, sign assemblies, destination/distance signs, street name signs, freeway-approach signing, and other guide signs used on conventional (non-freeway) roads in California.

2D.01 Scope of Conventional Road Guide Sign Standards and Application

Standard · Support · Guidance · Support · Guidance · Option · Support · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to expressways, freeways, and any road or street except as otherwise provided in this Manual.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Guide signs direct road users along streets and highways; inform them of intersecting routes; direct them to cities, towns, villages, or other important destinations; identify nearby rivers and streams, parks, forests, and historical sites; and provide information in the most simple and direct manner possible.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Primary or control destinations (those displayed consistently over longer distances along a route) should be meaningful to road users for navigation and orientation, and identifiable on official maps.
  2. 04Road user familiarity with the road should be considered in determining the need for guide signs on low-volume roads.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05Low-volume roads generally do not require guide signs to the extent needed on higher classes of roads, since guide signs are typically only beneficial to road users unfamiliar with the road.
  2. 06Guide signs, other than Street Name signs, generally are not used on low-volume rural roads except as needed to guide road users back to major roadways.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07If used on low-volume roads, destination names should be as specific and descriptive as possible; destinations such as campgrounds, ranger stations, or recreational areas should be clearly indicated so they are not mistaken for communities or locations with road user services.
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Guide signs may be used on low-volume roads at intersections to provide information for road users returning to a higher class of roads.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09Chapter 2A addresses placement, location, and other general criteria for signs.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 10A minimum spacing of 200 feet between guide signs should be maintained on conventional highways.
  2. 11A minimum spacing of 800 feet between guide signs should be maintained on freeways and expressways.

2D.02 Color, Retroreflection, and Illumination

Support · Standard · Support · Standard · Option · Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Requirements for illumination, retroreflection, and color are stated under the specific headings for individual guide signs or groups of signs; general provisions are in Sections 2A.06, 2A.21, and 2A.22.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Except as otherwise provided in this Manual, guide signs on streets and highways shall have a white message and border on a green background. All messages, borders, and legends shall be retroreflective, and all backgrounds shall be retroreflective or illuminated.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Color coding is sometimes used to help road users distinguish between multiple potentially confusing destinations, such as airport guide signs for multiple terminals/airlines, and community wayfinding signs for various traffic generators.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Except as otherwise provided, different color sign backgrounds shall not be used to color-code destinations. Color coding shall instead be accomplished with different colored square or rectangular sign panels on the face of the guide sign (Figure 2D-1).
Option — Permissive
  1. 05The different colored sign panels may include a black or white (whichever provides better contrast) letter, numeral, or other designation to identify an airport terminal or other destination.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06Section 2D.55 covers Community Wayfinding guide signs specifically.
  2. 07Refer to Caltrans' Traffic Operations Policy website for the most recent memorandum on retroreflection and illumination requirements for State highways.

2D.03 Size of Signs

Standard · Support · Option · Support · Option · Guidance · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Section 2A.07, the minimum sizes of conventional road guide signs that have standardized designs shall be as shown in Table 2D-1 and Table 2D-1(CA).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Section 2A.07 explains the applicability of the various columns in Table 2D-1 and Table 2D-1(CA).
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Signs larger than shown in Table 2D-1 and Table 2D-1(CA) may be used (Section 2A.07).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04For other guide signs, the legends are so variable that a standardized design or size is not appropriate; sign size is determined primarily by message length and the letter/spacing needed for legibility.
Option — Permissive
  1. 05Reduced letter height, reduced interline spacing, and reduced edge spacing may be used on guide signs if sign size must be limited by factors such as lane width or vertical/lateral clearance.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06Reduced spacing between letters or words should not be used merely to reduce overall guide sign size, except where engineering judgment determines it is necessary for unusual lateral-space constraints — in which case legibility distance should be the primary consideration in deciding whether to reduce spacing or letter height.
  2. 07When a reduction in prescribed size is necessary, the design used should be as similar as possible to the standard-size design.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Sign design details are provided in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (FHWA) and Caltrans' California Sign Specifications (Section 1A.05).

2D.04 Lettering Style

Standard

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The design of upper-case letters, lower-case letters, numerals, route shields, and spacing shall be as provided in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication and Caltrans' California Sign Specifications.
  2. 02Lettering for names of places, streets, and highways on conventional road guide signs shall combine lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters (Section 2A.08). The nominal loop height of lower-case letters shall be ¾ the height of the initial upper-case letter; when a mixed-case legend height is specified only by the initial upper-case letter, the lower-case height is determined by this proportion (and vice versa).
  3. 03All other word legends on conventional road guide signs shall be in upper-case letters.
  4. 04The unique letter forms of each Standard Alphabet series shall not be stretched, compressed, warped, or otherwise manipulated. Modifications to word length for a given letter height and series shall be accomplished only by the methods in Section 2D.03.

2D.05 Size of Lettering

Support · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Sign legibility is a direct function of letter size and spacing; legibility distance must be sufficient for road users to read and comprehend the sign despite inattention, blocked views, weather, or eyesight. Where conditions permit, repeating guide information on successive signs gives road users more than one opportunity to obtain it.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Design layouts for conventional road guide signs — interline spacing, edge spacing, and other specification details — shall follow the "Standard Highway Signs" publication and Caltrans' California Sign Specifications.
  2. 03Except as otherwise provided, the principal legend on post-mounted guide signs shall be at least 6 inches in height for upper-case letters, or a combination of 6-inch upper-case with 4.5-inch nominal loop height lower-case letters. On low-volume roads and urban streets with speeds of 25 mph or less, the principal legend shall be at least 4-inch upper-case, or 4-inch upper-case combined with 3-inch nominal loop height lower-case.
  3. 04Except as otherwise provided, the principal legend on overhead guide signs shall be at least 6-inch upper-case, or 6-inch upper-case combined with 4.5-inch nominal loop height lower-case.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Lettering sizes should be consistent on any particular class of highway.
  2. 06The minimum lettering and numeral sizes in this Manual (Table 2D-2) should be exceeded where conditions indicate a need for greater legibility.

2D.06 Amount of Legend

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01The longer the legend on a guide sign, the longer it takes road users to recognize and comprehend it, regardless of letter size.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Except where otherwise provided, guide signs should be limited to no more than three lines of destinations (place names, route numbers, street names, cardinal directions). Where two or more signs share the same overhead display, the amount of legend should be further minimized. Where appropriate, a distance message or action information (exit number, NEXT RIGHT, directional arrows) should be provided in addition to destinations.

2D.07 Abbreviations

Support · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Commonly recognized abbreviations reduce reading time and improve comprehension — descriptors and directional/quadrant orientations such as Boulevard (Blvd), North (N), and Southwest (SW) are examples. Figure 2D-2 shows examples of abbreviation use.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02The words NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, and WEST shall not be abbreviated when indicating cardinal directions of numbered or named highways on guide signs.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Abbreviations should be kept to a minimum but are useful when complete destination messages produce excessively long signs; if used, they should be unmistakably recognizable (Section 1D.08). Longer, commonly used street name descriptors not part of a proper name (Street, Boulevard, Avenue) should be abbreviated per Table 2D-3; shorter descriptors listed in Table 2D-4 should not be abbreviated.
  2. 04Periods, apostrophes, question marks, ampersands, or other non-letter/numeral/hyphen characters should not be used in abbreviations unless necessary to avoid confusion.
  3. 05The solidus (/) is intended for fractions only and should not separate words on the same legend line; use a hyphen instead, such as "TRUCKS – BUSES."

2D.08 Arrows

Support · Standard · Guidance · Standard · Option · Guidance · Standard · Option · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Arrows are used for lane assignment and to indicate direction toward designated routes or destinations. Figure 2D-3 shows the standard arrow designs approved for guide signs; detailed drawings appear in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Except for Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs (Section 2D.37), on overhead signs where a lane to be followed must be indicated, a down arrow shall be positioned over and point vertically downward toward the approximate center of that lane. Down arrows shall be used only on overhead guide signs restricting specific lanes to traffic bound for the indicated destination(s)/route(s), and only where an arrow can be located over each usable lane.
  2. 03More than one down arrow pointing to the same lane on a single overhead sign (or on multiple signs on the same structure) shall not be permitted.
  3. 04Where a roadway is leaving the through lanes, a directional arrow shall point upward at an angle approximating the alignment of the exit roadway near the departure point.
  4. 05The Type E directional arrow for circular intersections shall not be used on any sign not associated with a circular intersection.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06The Type A directional arrow should be used on guide signs on freeways, expressways, and conventional roads to indicate direction to a specific destination or group, except as otherwise provided (Section 2E.18).
  2. 07When a vertical, upward-pointing arrow is placed to the side of a group of destinations to indicate through movement, use Type A. When placed to the side of a single destination or under a destination/group, use Type B.
  3. 08Type B should be used on conventional road guide signs when placed at any angle to the side of a single destination, or horizontally to the side of a group.
  4. 09Type C advance turn directional arrow should be used on conventional road guide signs in advance of an intersection where a turn must be made to reach a posted destination.
  5. 10Type D should be used primarily for applications other than guide signs, except as provided in Paragraph 15.
  6. 11If Type E is used, the principles in Sections 2D.26 through 2D.29 should be followed.
Option — Permissive
  1. 12The Type A-Extended directional arrow may be used on guide signs where additional directional emphasis is needed relative to the sign's legend.
  2. 13Type C may be used to the side of an overhead guide sign's legend to accentuate a sharp turn exit maneuver (see Section 2E.25 for Exit Direction signs at low advisory ramp speeds).
  3. 14On conventional roads approaching an intersection where the Combination Lane-Use/Destination overhead sign (Section 2D.38) is not used, the Type C advance turn arrow may be used beneath an overhead sign's legend to show that a turn must be made from a mandatory movement lane to reach the destination(s) shown.
  4. 15Type D may be used on post-mounted conventional-road guide signs at lower operating speeds if the sign's text height is 8 inches or less; Type D arrows may also be used on a Street Name (D3-1 only) sign displaying two street names to indicate different travel directions for each street.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 16Type E may be used on guide signs approaching circular intersections to represent intended driver paths involving left-turn movements around the circulatory island.
Option — Permissive
  1. 17The directional and down arrows shown in Figure 2D-3 may be used on signs other than guide signs to provide directional guidance and lane assignment.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 18Arrows should be pointed at the appropriate angle to clearly convey the direction; a horizontally-oriented arrow should be used at right-angle intersections.
  2. 19On a post-mounted sign, an arrow for a straight-through movement should point upward; except as provided in Section 2D.50, an arrow for a turn should point horizontally or at an upward angle approximating the sharpness of the turn.
  3. 20At an exit, an arrow should be placed on the side of the sign that reinforces the exiting movement, using the directional arrow design.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 21If used, the Type C advance turn directional arrow shall display a right or left arrow whose shaft is bent at a 90-degree or oblique angle.
Option — Permissive
  1. 22Arrows may be placed below the principal legend or on the side consistent with the direction of movement.
  2. 23On a post-mounted sign at an exit where placing the arrow far from the roadway would create an unusually wide sign limiting the road user's view of it, the arrow may instead be placed at the bottom, centered under the legend.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 24The width across the arrowhead for Types A, B, and C should be between 1.5 and 1.75 times the upper-case letter height of the principal legend; for Type D, at least equal to that height; for down arrows on overhead signs, approximately 2 times that height.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 25Section 2D.37 covers arrows for Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs on conventional road approaches; Section 2D.41 covers arrows for Diagrammatic Advance guide signing at other intersections; Section 2D.39 covers diagrammatic arrows for Destination signs at circular intersections (Figure 2D-11).
  2. 26The "Standard Highway Signs" publication contains design details and standardized arrow sizes based on ranges of principal legend letter heights.

2D.09 Numbered Highway Systems

Support · Guidance · Standard · Support · Standard · Option · Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01The purpose of numbering and signing highway systems is to identify routes and facilitate travel.
  2. 02The Interstate and U.S. highway systems are numbered by AASHTO upon recommendations of the State highway organizations that own these systems; State and county road systems are numbered by the appropriate authorities.
  3. 03The basic policy for numbering the Interstate and U.S. systems is contained in AASHTO's published Purpose and Policy statements: (A) "Establishment and Development of United States Numbered Highways," and (B) "Establishment of a Marking System of the Routes Comprising the National System of Interstate and Defense Highways."
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04These policy principles should be followed for the systems in Paragraph 3 and any other systems, with effective coordination between adjacent jurisdictions; avoid reusing numbers already assigned to Interstate, U.S., or State routes in the same area, and keep overlapping numbered routes to a minimum.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Route systems shall be given preference in this order: Interstate, United States, State, and county. The preference shall be given by installing the highest-priority route number on the top or left of the sign, except as provided in Paragraph 7.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06Interstate route numbering shall be approved by the FHWA.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07The prioritization of route systems may be modified when a different order would better accommodate road user expectancy and provide more effective direction, such as separate decision points for routes encountered in a particular order.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Section 2D.56 covers signing of unnumbered highways to enhance route guidance and facilitate travel.
  2. 09The California Legislature designates all State highway routes and assigns their route numbers; general route descriptions and numbers are in Chapter 2, Article 3, of the Streets and Highways Code (SHC), and these numbers are used for all administrative purposes.

2D.10 Route Signs and Auxiliary Plaques

Standard · Option · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Paragraph 9 of Section 2D.29, all numbered highway routes shall be identified by route signs and auxiliary plaques.
  2. 02The signs for each system of numbered highways, distinctive in shape and color, shall be used only on that system and its approaches.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Route signs and auxiliary plaques may be proportionally enlarged where greater conspicuity or legibility is needed.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Route signs are typically mounted in assemblies with auxiliary plaques.
  2. 05Section 2D.57 covers signing for National Scenic Byways.
  3. 06Section 2D.58 covers signing for State-designated scenic byways, historic trails, and auto tour routes.

2D.11 Design of Route Signs

Standard · Option · Standard · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The design of standard route signs shall conform to the "Standard Highway Signs" publication; the design of other route signs shall be established by the authority having jurisdiction and shall generally conform to that publication.
  2. 02Interstate Route (M1-1 Shield and M1-1a Marker) signs (Figure 2D-4) shall be used on all Interstate routes and with Route Sign assemblies on intersecting highways.
  3. 03Except as otherwise provided, a 24 x 24-inch minimum size shall be used for one- or two-digit Interstate route numbers, and 30 x 24-inch minimum for three-digit numbers.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04When the Interstate Route sign is used in a route sign assembly (Section 2D.29), the M1-1a (Marker) sign — State name in white upper-case on blue background — may be used in place of the M1-1 (Shield) sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Use of the M1-1a (Marker) sign shall be limited to route sign assemblies.
  2. 06Off-Interstate Business Route (M1-2 and M1-3) signs (Figure 2D-4) shall consist of a cutout shield displaying the connecting Interstate route number and the words BUSINESS and either LOOP (route rejoins the same Interstate) or SPUR (route does not rejoin) in upper-case letters; legend and border shall be white on green, and the shield shall match the Interstate Route sign's shape and dimensions. The word INTERSTATE shall never appear on this sign.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07The Off-Interstate Business Route sign may be used on a major highway not part of the Interstate system that serves a city's business area from an interchange on the system.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 08U.S. Route (Marker) signs (Figure 2D-4) shall consist of black numerals on a white shield surrounded by a rectangular black background without a border, used on all U.S. routes and with Route Sign assemblies on intersecting highways.
  2. 09A 24 x 24-inch minimum size shall be used for one- or two-digit U.S. route numbers, 30 x 24-inch minimum for three digits.
  3. 10State Route signs shall be designed by the individual State highway agencies.
  4. 11The legend on State Route signs shall conform to the Standard Alphabets in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication.
  5. 12aThe State Route (Marker) sign (G28-2(CA)) shall be used per the sizes in Table 2D-1(CA). The State Route (Shield) sign (G28-1(CA)) shall be used per the sizes in Caltrans' California Sign Specifications.
  6. 13Where U.S. Route signs are used as components of guide signs, only the distinctive shield shape and route numerals should be used — the rectangular black background (as on the M1-4 shield) shall not be included. Where used as components of other signs with non-contrasting background colors, the rectangular background shall be retained so the distinctive shield shape remains recognizable.
  7. 14If county road authorities establish a special system of important county roads, a statewide policy including a uniform numbering system shall be established. The County Route (M1-6) sign (Figure 2D-4) shall be a pentagon with a yellow county name, route number, and border on a blue background, minimum size 24 x 24 inches.
  8. 15If a jurisdiction uses letters instead of numbers to identify routes, all references to "numbered routes" in this Chapter shall be interpreted to also include lettered routes.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 16If used with other route signs in common assemblies, the County Route sign should be of a compatible size.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 17The National Forest Route (M1-7) sign (Figure 2D-4) design shall be as detailed in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication. Route signs for other park and forest roads shall be designed with an appropriate level of distinctiveness and adequate legibility, generally complying with route sign design principles and compatible in size with other route signs used in common assemblies.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 18The State Route (Shield) sign is used on the face of guide signs; the State Route (Marker) sign is used for stand-alone assembly installations.
  2. 19For the U.S. Route (Shield) sign and both State Route (Shield and Marker) signs, refer to Figure 2D-4(CA).
  3. 20Standard details for State Route shields and markers are in Caltrans' California Sign Specifications.

In practice: California does not use the generic federal M1-5 State Route shield at all — Figure 2D-4 shows it struck through in the source, and Section 2D.11 directs agencies to the California-specific G28-1(CA) shield and G28-2(CA) marker instead (Figure 2D-4(CA), Table 2D-1(CA)).

2D.12 Design of Route Sign Auxiliary Plaques

Standard · Guidance · Option · Standard · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Route sign auxiliary plaques displaying word legends, except the Junction (M2-1P) plaque, shall have a minimum standard size of 24 x 12 inches. The Junction plaque and plaques displaying arrows shall have a minimum standard size of 21 x 15 inches. All route sign auxiliary plaques shall match the color combination of the route sign they supplement.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02The background, legend, and border of a route sign auxiliary plaque should match the route sign's colors in a route sign assembly (Section 2D.29). For a multi-background-color design such as the Interstate Route sign, the plaque's background color should match the area on which the route number is placed.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03A route sign and any auxiliary plaques used with it may be combined on a single sign as a guide sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04If a route sign and its auxiliary plaques are combined into a single guide sign, the background shall be green and the design shall comply with basic guide sign design principles; auxiliary messages shall be white legends placed directly on the green background. Auxiliary plaques shall not be mounted directly to a guide sign or other type of sign.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05Chapter 2F covers auxiliary plaques for toll highways.

2D.13 Junction Auxiliary Plaque (M2-1P)

Standard

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The Junction (M2-1P) auxiliary plaque (Figure 2D-5) shall display the abbreviated legend JCT and be mounted at the top of an assembly (Section 2D.30) directly above the route sign, the alternative route sign (Section 2D.17) that is part of the route designation, or the Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque where access is available only to one direction of the intersected route. Its minimum size shall be 21 x 15 inches for compatibility with arrow-displaying auxiliary plaques.

2D.14 Combination Junction Sign (M2-2)

Option · Standard · Guidance

Option — Permissive
  1. 01As an alternative to the standard Junction assembly where more than one route is intersected or joined, a rectangular guide sign may be used displaying the word JUNCTION above the route numbers.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02The Combination Junction (M2-2) sign (Figure 2D-5) shall have a green background with white border and lettering for the word JUNCTION.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03The Combination Junction sign should comply with Section 2D.11's provisions on incorporating route signs as components of guide signs.
  2. 04Although sign size depends on the number of routes involved, numerals should be large enough for clear legibility and comparable in size to those in the individual route signs.

2D.15 Cardinal Direction Auxiliary Plaques (M3-1P through M3-4P)

Guidance · Standard · Option · Support

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaques (Figure 2D-5) displaying NORTH, EAST, SOUTH, or WEST should be used to indicate the general direction of the entire route.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02To improve readability and recognition, the first letter of the cardinal direction words shall be ten percent larger, rounded up to the nearest whole number size.
  2. 03If used, the Cardinal Direction plaque shall be mounted directly above a route sign or, if used, an alternative route auxiliary plaque.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04The Cardinal Direction plaque may be placed either above or to the right of the Route shield when used on the face of a guide sign.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05Refer to Section 2D.50 for application in a Freeway Entrance sign package.

2D.16 Alternative Route Auxiliary Plaques (M4-1P through M4-4P)

Option · Standard

Option — Permissive
  1. 01Alternative Route auxiliary plaques (Figure 2D-5) displaying ALTERNATE, BY-PASS, BUSINESS, or TRUCK may indicate an alternate route of the same number between two points on that route.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02If used, Alternative Route auxiliary plaques shall be mounted directly above a route sign.

2D.17 ALTERNATE Auxiliary Plaques (M4-1P and M4-1aP)

2D.32 Directional Assembly

Standard · Guidance · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01A Directional assembly shall consist of a Cardinal Direction auxiliary plaque (if needed), a route sign, and a Directional Arrow auxiliary plaque, used as follows: (A) turn movements (indicated in advance by an Advance Route Turn assembly) shall be marked by a Directional assembly with a route sign for the turning route and a single-headed arrow pointing in the turn direction; the beginning of a route (indicated in advance by a Junction assembly) shall be marked by a Directional assembly with a route sign for that route and a single-headed arrow pointing in the route's direction. (B) An intersected route (indicated in advance by a Junction assembly) on a crossroad designated on both legs shall be marked by either: (1) two Directional assemblies, each with a route sign, a Cardinal Direction plaque, and a single-headed arrow pointing in the direction of movement on that route; or (2) one Directional assembly with a route sign and a double-headed arrow pointing at appropriate angles left, right, or ahead.   1. 02Straight-through movements should be indicated by a Directional assembly with a route sign for the continuing route and a vertical arrow. At major, Y, or offset intersections, additional Directional assemblies should be installed on the far right or left corner to confirm the near-side assemblies. When the near-corner position is impractical, the far right corner should be preferred, with oversized signs if necessary for legibility. Where unusual conditions exist, location should be determined by engineering judgment to provide the best combination of view and safety.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04It is more important that guide signs be readable and their information/direction readily understood at the appropriate time and place than to be located with absolute uniformity.

2D.55 Community Wayfinding Signs

Support · Standard · Guidance · Standard · Guidance · Support · Option · Standard · Guidance · Support · Standard · Guidance · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Community wayfinding guide signs are part of a coordinated and continuous system of signs directing tourists and other road users to key civic, cultural, visitor, and recreational attractions and other secondary destinations within a city or local urbanized/downtown area.
  2. 02They are a type of destination guide sign for conventional roads with a common color and/or identification marker for destinations within an overall wayfinding plan for an area.
  3. 03Figures 2D-30 through 2D-32 illustrate various examples of design and application.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Use of community wayfinding guide signs shall be limited to conventional roads; they shall not be installed on freeway or expressway mainlines or ramps. Direction to community wayfinding destinations from a freeway or expressway shall be limited to a Supplemental guide sign (Section 2E.51) on the mainline and a Destination sign (Section 2D.36) on the ramp directing road users to the area(s) where community wayfinding signs are used; individual wayfinding destinations shall not be displayed on the Supplemental guide and Destination signs except where consistent with State or agency policy on Supplemental guide signs.
  2. 05Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be used to direct to primary destinations or highway routes/streets — Destination or other guide signs shall be used for that purpose and shall have priority over any community wayfinding sign in placement, prominence, and conspicuity.
  3. 06Because regulatory, warning, and other guide signs have higher priority, community wayfinding guide signs shall not be installed where adequate spacing cannot be provided from higher-priority signs, and shall not be installed where they would obscure the road user's view of other traffic control devices.
  4. 07Community wayfinding guide signs shall not be mounted overhead.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 08If used, a community wayfinding sign system should be established at a local municipal or equivalent jurisdictional level, or for an urbanized area of adjoining municipalities forming an identifiable, cohesive geographic entity; it should not be used on a regional or statewide basis where infrequent or sparse placement would not read as a continuous, coordinated system. In such cases, Destination or other guide signs should instead direct road users to the identifiable area containing the eligible destination.
  2. 08aOn State highways, community wayfinding guide signs should be installed under an encroachment permit issued by Caltrans.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 08bPlacement of community wayfinding guide signs shall be performed by the requesting jurisdiction or agency through the normal permit process, as a fee-exempt permit.
  2. 08cThese signs shall be installed in accordance with this Manual and Caltrans' Standard Plans.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 08dCommunity wayfinding sign panels should be clearly marked to indicate ownership.
  2. 09When a system of community wayfinding guide signs is being considered, the existing guide sign system should be evaluated for applicability and general compliance with this Manual to ensure road user directional guidance is adequately addressed.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 10The design provisions of this Section for vehicular community wayfinding sign legends do not apply to signs intended only to inform or direct pedestrians or other users of a sidewalk or roadside area.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 11Because pedestrian wayfinding signs typically use smaller legends inadequate for vehicular viewing, and can provide direction to pedestrians that might conflict with vehicular direction, they should be located to minimize their conspicuity to vehicular traffic — as far from the street as practicable (e.g., the far edge of the sidewalk), or, where that is impracticable, using one or more of: (A) locating signs away from intersections with high-priority traffic control devices; (B) facing the pedestrian message toward the sidewalk and away from the street; or (C) cantilevering the sign over the sidewalk (if mounted at vehicular sign height) to remove it from the vehicular sign line of sight.
  2. 12To further minimize nighttime conspicuity to vehicular traffic, pedestrian wayfinding signs should not be retroreflective.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 13Color coding is sometimes used on community wayfinding guide signs to help road users distinguish between multiple potentially confusing traffic generator destinations in different neighborhoods or subareas.
Option — Permissive
  1. 14At the boundaries of the geographical area within which community wayfinding signing is used, an informational guide sign may be posted informing road users of the wayfinding signing and identifying the meanings of the color codes or pictographs used.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 15These informational guide signs shall have a white legend and border on a green background, a design similar to Figure 2D-1 and consistent with basic guide sign design principles, and shall not be installed on freeway or expressway mainlines or ramps.
  2. 16The color coding or a pictograph of the community wayfinding system's identification markers shall be included on the boundary informational guide sign, applying to a specific, identifiable neighborhood or geographical subarea; color coding/pictographs shall not distinguish between different destination types within the same subarea. Color coding shall use different colored square or rectangular panels on the sign face, positioned to the left of the neighborhood/area name; panel height shall not exceed 2 times the upper-case letter height of the principal legend.
Option — Permissive
  1. 17The colored panels may include a black or white letter, numeral, or designation (whichever provides better contrast) to identify the destination.
  2. 18Except for the boundary informational guide sign, community wayfinding guide signs may use background colors other than green to color-identify wayfinding destinations by geographical area, with or without the boundary sign's corresponding color-coding panels. Except as provided in Paragraph 19, other approved background colors (Section 2A.06) may also be used for community wayfinding color coding.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 19The standard colors red, orange, yellow, purple (or their fluorescent versions), fluorescent yellow-green, and fluorescent pink shall not be used as background colors for community wayfinding guide signs, to minimize confusion with critical higher-priority regulatory/warning sign color meanings.
  2. 20The minimum contrast value of legend color to background color for community wayfinding guide signs shall be at least 0.70 (70%).
  3. 21All messages, borders, legends, backgrounds, and identification markers shall be retroreflective (Sections 2A.21, 2A.22).
  4. 22Community wayfinding guide signs, exclusive of any identification marker, shall be rectangular.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 23Simplicity and uniformity in design, position, and application (Section 2A.04) are important and should be incorporated into the sign design and location plans.
  2. 24Community wayfinding guide signs should be limited to three destinations per sign (Section 2D.06).
  3. 25Abbreviations (Section 1D.08) should be kept to a minimum and limited to those commonly recognized.
  4. 26Horizontal lines contrasting with the sign background should be used to separate groups of destinations by direction.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 27The basic requirement for all highway signs, including community wayfinding signs, is that they be legible to those for whom they are intended and understandable in time to permit a proper response; Section 2A.04 has more on effective sign design.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 28Word messages should be as brief as practical, with lettering large enough for the necessary legibility distance.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 29The minimum ratio of letter height to legibility distance shall comply with Section 2A.08; lettering for destination/directional legends shall comply with the minimum letter heights in Section 2D.05.
  2. 30Interline and edge spacing shall comply with Section 2D.05.
  3. 31Except as provided in Paragraph 34, the lettering style for destination/directional legends on community wayfinding guide signs shall comply with Section 2D.04.
  4. 32Lettering for destinations shall combine lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters (Section 2D.04); all other word messages shall be in all upper-case letters.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 33Except as provided in Paragraphs 34 and 35, letters, numerals, and other characters should be composed of the Standard Alphabets detailed in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication.
Option — Permissive
  1. 34A lettering style other than the Standard Alphabets may be used on community wayfinding guide signs if an engineering study determines that its legibility and recognition values meet or exceed the Standard Alphabets for the same legend height and stroke width.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 35If a lettering style other than the Standard Alphabets is used, it shall be conventional in form — not italic, oblique, script, highly decorative, or otherwise unusual.
  2. 36Except for signs designed and located to be viewed only by pedestrians, bicyclists stopped out of the flow of traffic, or occupants of parked vehicles, Internet and e-mail addresses (including domain names and URLs) and scanning graphics for obtaining information (Section 2A.04) shall not be displayed on any community wayfinding guide sign or sign assembly.
  3. 37Arrow location and destination priority order shall follow Sections 2D.08 and 2D.36; arrows shall be of the designs in Section 2D.08.
Option — Permissive
  1. 38Pictographs (Section 1C.02) may be used on community wayfinding guide signs.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 39If a pictograph is used, its height shall not exceed 2 times the upper-case letter height of the principal legend.
  2. 40Except for pictographs, symbols not approved in this Manual for use on guide signs shall not be used on community wayfinding guide signs.
  3. 41Business logos, commercial graphics, or other forms of advertising (Section 1D.07) shall not be used on community wayfinding guide signs or sign assemblies.
Option — Permissive
  1. 42Other graphics that specifically identify the wayfinding system, including identification markers, may be used on the overall sign assembly and sign supports.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 43An identification marker is a shape, color, and/or pictograph used as a visual identifier for the community wayfinding system for an area; Figure 2D-30 shows example identification marker designs.
Option — Permissive
  1. 44An identification marker may be used in a community wayfinding sign assembly, or incorporated into the overall design of a sign, to visually identify it as part of an overall wayfinding system.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 45Identification markers shall be smaller in size and shape than the community wayfinding guide signs themselves, and shall not be designed to have an appearance that could be mistaken by road users for a traffic control device.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 46The area of the identification marker should not exceed ⅕ of the area of the community wayfinding guide sign with which it is mounted in the same sign assembly.

2D.60 Signing at Airports

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Many roadways within airport facilities (including terminal curbside roadways) are considered conventional roads because they typically have frequent driveways and at-grade intersections and might have pedestrian activity along and/or across them.
  2. 02Some airport roadways have full or partial control of access and operating speeds higher than 45 mph and are thus classified as freeways or expressways for signing purposes (Chapter 2E) — typically on approaches to the airport from other highways, on approaches to terminal/parking/patron facility access points, and on roadways providing exits to the local or regional highway network.
  3. 02aRequirements for Exit Gore signs and Exit Number plaques on State highways are in Sections 2E.22, 2E.23, 2E.25, 2E.26, and 2M.09.
  4. 03Roadways within airports and other similarly contained networks with multiple closely spaced access points to multiple destinations (terminals, parking, rental cars, other airport services) often present guide-signing challenges — closely spaced signs, excessive co-located or successive sign messaging, and the resulting informational load are of particular concern. The Transportation Research Board's Airport Cooperative Research Program Report 52, "Wayfinding and Signing Guidelines for Airport Terminal and Landside," has additional information.
  5. 04An example of major guide signing on the approaches to and within an airport facility roadway network is shown in Figure 2D-39.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05If adequate sign spacing cannot be provided due to site and roadway characteristics, measures should be taken to reduce vehicle speeds to give road users adequate time to comprehend and respond to sign messages; consider increasing sign letter heights for greater viewing distances and decision times. Where a single terminal serves a large number of airlines, airline information should be displayed on separate signs appearing in sequence to limit the number of airlines displayed at a single location. Changeable message signs (Chapter 2L) should not be used to rotate the display of airlines to an approaching road user.

In practice: various methods can reduce vehicle speeds at these locations — roadway geometric changes, traffic calming, and increased enforcement — with speed limit provisions found in Section 2B.21.

2D.101(CA) Intersection Number (G98A(CA)) Sign

Option · Support · Guidance · Standard

Option — Permissive
  1. 01The Intersection Number (G98A(CA)) sign (Figure 2D-101(CA)) may be installed on any section of a highway route to number an intersection, assisting road users in estimating their progress and identifying specific locations along the highway.
  2. 02Intersection numbering may follow either a reference location numbering or consecutive numbering system.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Reference location numbering is preferred over consecutive numbering for two reasons: (A) if new intersections are added to the route, highway agencies do not need to revise the existing numbering sequence; and (B) reference location numbering helps road users estimate destination distances and travel mileage more effectively.
  2. 04The G98A(CA) signs provide a simple and effective method for tourists to navigate safely and efficiently along a highway route with a high concentration of tourist destinations directly on or near the route.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05If used, the G98A(CA) signs should be ground-mounted or mounted on traffic signal poles at signalized intersections.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06The G98A(CA) signs shall not be installed on a highway unless the intersection numbers are published in a tourist map.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 2D

  • Conventional road guide signs default to a white message/border on green background; principal legend is at least 6-inch upper-case (or 6-inch upper-case / 4.5-inch lower-case nominal loop height) post-mounted, dropping to 4-inch on low-speed (≤25 mph) roads — always combining lower-case with initial upper-case letters for place/street/highway names, but all-upper-case for other word legends.
  • Route sign precedence is fixed by law and practice: Interstate, then U.S., then State, then county, with the highest-priority route at the top/left of an assembly — and California does not use the generic federal M1-5 State Route shield at all, substituting its own G28-1(CA) shield and G28-2(CA) marker.
  • The five sign-assembly types (Route Sign, Junction, Advance Route Turn, Directional, Confirming/Reassurance) each have a defined composition and placement window (e.g., Junction assemblies ≥400 feet in advance in rural areas, Confirming assemblies 25–200 feet beyond the intersected highway) — Trailblazer assemblies are a related but distinct sixth type using the word TO.
  • Destination signs cap at three names (four only when the matching Advance/Supplemental guide signs also show four), with the nearer destination always listed above the farther one in the same direction, and no signs at all permitted for privately owned, profit-making enterprises or shopping centers on California highways.
  • Community wayfinding guide signs are a strictly bounded category: conventional roads only, never overhead, never on freeway/expressway mainlines or ramps, capped at three destinations, barred from red/orange/yellow/purple/fluorescent-pink backgrounds, and always subordinate in priority, placement, and conspicuity to regulatory, warning, and primary destination/route guide signs.
  • California's Table 2D-101(CA) imposes concrete, quantitative eligibility thresholds (minimum attendance/enrollment/employee counts and maximum distance from the highway, varying by Major Metropolitan/Urbanized/Rural area classification) before any supplemental destination sign — zoo, museum, convention center, government center, airport, etc. — may be installed on a State highway.
