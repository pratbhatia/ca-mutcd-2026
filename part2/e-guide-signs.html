Guide Signs (Freeway and Expressway)

Chapter 2E covers guide signing for freeways and expressways — interchange advance/exit/gore signing, exit numbering, Overhead Arrow-per-Lane and Diagrammatic Advance designs, post-interchange and supplemental signing, and route/trailblazer signing. This is distinct from Chapter 2D "Guide Signs," which covers guide signing for conventional roads; the source manual gives both chapters the identical title "Guide Signs," so this page is labeled "Guide Signs (Freeway and Expressway)" to keep the two apart in navigation.

2E.01Scope of Freeway and Expressway Guide Sign Standards

Support · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01This Chapter provides a uniform and effective signing system for high-volume, high-speed motor vehicle traffic on freeways and expressways. Expressway signing requirements exceed those for conventional roads (Chapter 2D) but are less than freeway signing requirements. Because existing roads have many geometric design variables, a signing concept commensurate with prevailing conditions is the primary consideration. Section 1C.02 defines freeway and expressway.
  2. 02Guide signs for freeways and expressways are primarily identified by the name of the sign rather than an assigned sign designation. Design guidelines are in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication and Caltrans' California Sign Specifications (Section 1A.05).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03The provisions of this Chapter shall apply to any highway that meets the definition of freeway or expressway facilities.

2E.02Freeway and Expressway Signing Principles

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Signing systems are developed on the premise that they primarily benefit road users unfamiliar with the route or area, furnishing clear instructions for orderly progress to destinations. Sign installations are an integral part of the facility and are best planned concurrently with highway location and geometric design; plans should be analyzed during the earliest stages of preliminary design and correlated as final design develops. Excessive signing on many major highways usually results from a multitude of signs that are too small and poorly designed/placed.
  2. 02Freeway and expressway signing is to be considered and developed as a planned system of installations. An engineering study is sometimes necessary for individual locations, but consideration of an entire route is also necessary.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Road users should be guided with consistent signing on interchange approaches, between States, and through rural or urban areas, since geographical, geometric, and operating factors create significant urban/rural differences that signing should account for.
  2. 04Guide signs on freeways and expressways should serve distinct functions: (A) give directions to destinations, streets, or highway routes at intersections/interchanges; (B) furnish advance notice of the approach to intersections/interchanges; (C) direct road users into appropriate lanes in advance of diverging/merging movements; (D) identify routes and directions on those routes; (E) show distances to destinations; (F) indicate access to general motorist services, rest, scenic, and recreational areas; and (G) provide other navigational information.

2E.03Guide Sign Classification

Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Freeway and expressway guide signs are classified as: (A) Interchange signs (Sections 2E.21–2E.23, 2E.25–2E.44); (B) Interchange Sequence signs (Section 2E.24); (C) Post-Interchange signs (Sections 2E.47–2E.49); (D) Community Interchanges Identification signs (Section 2E.52); (E) Next Exits signs (Section 2E.53); (F) Weigh Station signs (Section 2E.54); (G) Route signs and Trailblazer Assemblies (Section 2E.55); (H) At-Grade Intersection signs (Section 2E.58); (I) General Information signs (Chapter 2H); (J) Reference Location signs (Sections 2H.11–2H.12); (K) General Service signs (Chapter 2I); (L) Rest and Scenic Area signs (Section 2I.05); (M) Tourist Information and Welcome Center signs (Section 2I.08); (N) Radio Information, Travel Information, and Roadside Assistance signs (Sections 2I.09–2I.13); (O) Carpool and Ridesharing signs (Section 2I.14); (P) Specific Service signs (Chapter 2J); and (Q) Recreational and Cultural Interest Area signs (Chapter 2M).
  2. 02Refer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 1, Item H text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.

2E.04Characteristics of Urban Signing

Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Urban conditions are characterized less by city limits than by: (A) mainline roadways with more than two lanes per direction; (B) high through-traffic volumes; (C) high entering/leaving interchange volumes; (D) closely-spaced interchanges; (E) roadway and interchange lighting; (F) three or more interchanges serving the major city; (G) a loop, circumferential, or spur route serving a sizable portion of the urban population; and (H) visual clutter from roadside development.
  2. 02Operating conditions and geometrics on urban freeways/expressways usually make special sign treatments desirable, including: (A) use of Interchange Sequence signs (Section 2E.24); (B) use of sign spreading to the maximum extent possible (Section 2E.43); (C) elimination of General or Specific Service signing (Chapters 2I and 2J); (D) reduction to a minimum of post-interchange signs (Section 2E.47); (E) display of advance signs at distances closer to the interchange with adjusted legend (Section 2E.23); (F) use of overhead signs on roadway structures and independent sign supports (Section 2E.19); (G) use of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs at interchanges with option lanes (Section 2E.40), or Diagrammatic Advance guide signs at interchanges with complex ramp geometry (Section 2E.41); and (H) frequent use of street names as the principal message.
  3. 03Lower speeds often characteristic of urban operations do not justify lower signing standards — typical traffic patterns are more complex to negotiate, so large, easy-to-read legends are just as necessary as on rural highways.

2E.05Characteristics of Rural Signing

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Rural areas ordinarily have greater distances between interchanges, permitting adequate spacing for sign sequences. However, the absence of traffic in adjoining lanes and ramps often adds monotony or inattention to rural driving, increasing the importance of signs that call for decisions or actions.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Where there are long distances between interchanges and relatively unchanging alignment, signs should be positioned for their best effect on road users. The tendency to group all signing near rural interchanges should be avoided by considering the entire route in developing signing plans. Extra effort should go to placing signs at natural target locations to command attention, particularly where the message requires a road user action.

2E.06Signing of Named Highways

Guidance · Support · Option

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Signing of named highways on freeways and expressways should comply with Section 2D.56.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Section 2M.10 contains information regarding memorial or dedication signing of routes, bridges, or highway components.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03At interchanges other than freeway-to-freeway, Interchange Guide (G77(CA) and G78(CA) series) signs may be used to direct traffic to the named freeway by displaying both the freeway name and appropriate route shield.
  2. 04In rural areas, ground-mounted freeway name signs may be installed beyond major freeway interchanges at approximately 10-mile intervals.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Freeway names should not be used on signs directing motorists to freeways in rural areas.

2E.07Designation of Destinations

Standard · Guidance · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The direction of a freeway and the major destinations or control cities along it shall be clearly identified through appropriate destination legends (see Section 2D.35). Successive freeway guide signs shall provide continuity in destination names and consistency with available map information. At any decision point, a given destination shall be indicated by way of only one route (see Figure 2E-1).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Control city legends should be used: (A) at interchanges between freeways; (B) at separation points of overlapping freeway routes; (C) on directional signs on intersecting routes guiding traffic entering the freeway; (D) on Pull-Through signs; and (E) on the bottom line of post-interchange distance signs.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Continuity of destination names is also useful on expressways serving long-distance or intrastate travel.
  2. 04The determination of major destinations or control cities is important to the quality of service the freeway provides. Control cities are selected by the States and contained in AASHTO's "Guidelines for the Selection of Supplemental Guide Signs for Traffic Generators Adjacent to Freeways, 4th Edition / Guide Signs, Part II: Guidelines for Airport Guide Signing / Guide Signs, Part III: List of Control Cities for Use in Guide Signs on Interstate Highways."
  3. 04aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 4 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Each Caltrans District should establish its list of control cities in coordination with neighboring districts and adjacent states to ensure consistent, uniform signage for through traffic on State highways. The same control cities should be used along a given route for each direction of travel.

2E.08General

Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Effective signs are legible to approaching road users and readable/comprehensible in the viewing time provided to permit proper responses. Desired design characteristics include: (a) long visibility distances; (b) large lettering, symbols, and arrows; and (c) short legends.

2E.09Color of Guide Signs

Standard · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Guide signs on freeways and expressways, except as otherwise provided in this Manual, shall have white letters, symbols, arrows, and borders on a green background.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Color requirements for route signs and trailblazers; for signs with blank-out or changeable messages; for services, rest area, and park/recreational area signs; and for certain miscellaneous signs are provided in the individual Sections dealing with the particular sign or sign group.

2E.10Retroreflection or Illumination

Standard · Support · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Letters, numerals, symbols, arrows, and borders of all guide signs shall be retroreflective. The background of all guide signs that are not independently illuminated shall be retroreflective.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Where there is no serious interference from extraneous light sources, retroreflective post-mounted signs usually provide adequate nighttime visibility.
  2. 03On freeways/expressways where much night driving uses low-beam headlights, the headlight illumination incident to an overhead sign display is relatively small.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04Overhead sign installations should be illuminated (see Section 2A.21) unless an engineering study shows retroreflection alone will perform effectively. The type of illumination chosen should provide effective and reasonably uniform illumination of the sign face and message.

2E.11Interchange Classification

Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01For signing purposes, interchanges are classified as major, intermediate, and minor (minimum letter/numeral sizes by classification are in Tables 2E-2 and 2E-4): (A) Major interchanges are subdivided into (a) interchanges with other expressways/freeways, or (b) interchanges with high-volume multi-lane highways, principal urban arterials, or major rural routes where interchanging traffic volume is heavy or includes many unfamiliar road users. (B) Intermediate interchanges are those with urban/rural routes not in the major or minor category. (C) Minor interchanges include those with local, very light traffic, such as land service access roads; where the sum of exit volumes is estimated below 100 vehicles per day in the design year, the interchange is classified as minor.

2E.12Size of Signs and Letters

Standard · Support · Option · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Section 2A.07, the sizes of freeway and expressway guide signs that have standardized designs shall be as shown in Table 2E-1.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Section 2A.07 contains information regarding the applicability of the various columns in Table 2E-1.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Signs larger than those shown in Table 2E-1 may be used (see Section 2A.07).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04The nominal loop height of lower-case letters shall be ¾ of the initial upper-case letter height (see Paragraph 3 of Section 2D.05). Other word legends (cardinal directions, action messages, special characters) shall be all upper-case with a minimum letter height of 8 inches. Interline and edge spacing shall be per Section 2E.13.
  2. 05For all freeway/expressway signs without a standardized design, message dimensions shall be determined first, then outside sign dimensions. Minimum numeral/letter sizes for expressway guide signs by interchange classification, sign type, and legend component shall be per Tables 2E-2 and 2E-3; for freeway guide signs, per Tables 2E-4 and 2E-5. Minimum sizes for overhead-mounted expressway and freeway guide signs shall be per the "Overhead" columns of Tables 2E-2 and 2E-4 respectively, except where a larger minimum is provided in the columns for the applicable interchange type.
  3. 06All names of places, streets, and highways on freeway/expressway guide signs shall be composed of lower-case letters with initial upper-case letters, using FHWA Standard Alphabet Series E (modified) per the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05).
  4. 07Lettering size on freeway/expressway signs shall be the same for both rural and urban conditions.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Sign size is determined primarily by message length and lettering size needed for legibility. Letter style/height and arrow design are standardized for freeway/expressway signs to assure uniform, effective application.
  2. 09Designs for upper- and lower-case FHWA Standard Alphabets, with recommended letter-spacing tables, are in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 10Freeway lettering sizes (Tables 2E-4 and 2E-5) should be used when expressway geometric design is comparable to freeway standards.
  2. 11Other sign letter size requirements not specifically identified elsewhere should be guided by these specifications; abbreviations should be kept to a minimum except as provided in Section 2E.16.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 12A sign mounted over a particular lane might have to be limited in horizontal dimension to the lane width, so another sign can be placed over an adjacent lane; maintaining vertical clearance might further limit overhead sign/legend size.

2E.13Interline and Edge Spacing

Guidance

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Interline spacing of upper-case letters should be approximately ¾ of the average upper-case letter height in adjacent lines.
  2. 02Spacings to the top/bottom borders should equal the average letter height of the adjacent line; lateral spacing to vertical borders should be essentially the height of the largest letter.

2E.14Sign Borders

Guidance · Support · Option

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01For guide signs larger than 120 x 72 inches, the border should be 2 inches wide; for smaller guide signs, 1.25 inches. On unusually large signs with oversized letter heights, route shields, or other legend elements, the border should be 2.5 inches wide and not exceed 3 inches. In all cases, border width should not exceed the stroke width of the principal legend lettering.
  2. 02Corner radii of sign borders should be approximately ⅛ of the minimum sign dimension on guide signs, except radii should not exceed 12 inches on any sign.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03The "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05) contains detailed information on border widths and corner radii for ranges of sign sizes.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04The sign material in the area outside of the corner radius may be trimmed.

2E.15Amount of Legend on Guide Signs

Guidance · Support

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01No more than two destination names or street names should be displayed on any Interchange Advance Guide sign or Exit Direction sign. A city name and street name on the same sign should be avoided. Where two or three signs are placed on the same supports, destinations/street names should be limited to one per sign, or a total of three in the display. Sign legends should not exceed three lines of copy, exclusive of exit number and action/distance information.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Where only one interchange serves a community, the intersecting street name is generally superfluous to the city name on Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs. Where a community is served by multiple interchanges, the city name is typically displayed on a Community Interchanges Identification sign (Section 2E.52) or Next Exits sign (Section 2E.53), with each interchange then identified by its intersecting roadway name rather than the city name.

2E.16Abbreviations

Standard

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The use of abbreviations on freeway and expressway guide signs shall comply with Section 2D.07.

2E.17Symbols

Support · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Symbols are not normally displayed on freeway/expressway guide signs. One exception is the PARK-RIDE Supplemental guide sign (Section 2E.51), which displays the Carpool symbol. In some cases, General Information symbols (Chapter 2H) might be included to shorten an unusually lengthy legend.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02When a General Information symbol is incorporated, all legend components should be balanced in size and arrangement for maximum legibility. The General Information (I series) sign, rather than the symbol alone, should be placed as a sign panel within the guide sign so the border provides adequate symbol recognition, positioned to the left of the legend it applies to, sized similarly to a route shield for that type of guide sign.

2E.18Arrows for Interchange Guide Signs

Standard · Option · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Arrows used on interchange guide signs shall be of the types shown in Figure 2D-3 and shall comply with this Section and Section 2D.08.
  2. 02Except on Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs (Section 2E.40) and Exit Direction signs for lane drops (Section 2E.28), and except as provided in Paragraph 5, directional arrows on all overhead and post-mounted Exit Direction signs shall point diagonally upward. Overhead Exit Direction sign arrows shall be on the side consistent with the exiting movement direction. Post-mounted Exit Direction sign arrows shall be at the bottom, centered under the legend.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03On overhead Exit Direction signs located fully over the tapered portion of the exit ramp at the theoretical gore, where a directional arrow to the side of the legend farthest from the roadway might create an unusually wide sign limiting the arrow's visibility, the arrow may be placed at the bottom, centered under the legend.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Directional arrows on guide signs for multi-lane exits shall be positioned below the legend over the approximate center of each lane to which the arrow applies (see Figure 2E-38).
  2. 05Down arrows shall only be used on overhead signs to indicate a lane to be followed, positioned over the approximate center of each lane pointing vertically downward. Down arrows shall be used only on overhead guide signs that restrict specific lanes to traffic bound for the indicated destination(s)/route(s), and shall not be used unless an arrow can be located over and pointed to the approximate center of each usable lane.
  3. 06More than one down arrow pointing to the same lane on a single overhead sign (or multiple signs on the same structure) shall not be permitted.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06aRefer to Section 2B.38 and Figure 2B-10(CA) for sign options that allow trucks to legally use a lane other than the rightmost lane, accomplished by placing the TRUCKS (R70A(CA)) and OK (R70B(CA)) sign panels adjacent to a down arrow.
  2. 07Directional and down arrows for guide signs are shown in Figure 2D-3. Detailed drawings and standardized sizes based on letter-height ranges are in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05), including dimensions for Overhead Arrow-per-Lane and Diagrammatic Advance guide signing.

2E.19Overhead Sign Installations

Support

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Specifications for structural supports for signs have been standardized by AASHTO. Overcrossing structures can often support overhead signs and might in some cases be the only practical location providing adequate viewing distance, eliminating the need for additional roadside sign supports. Conditions warranting overhead sign installation are given in Section 2A.14 and throughout this Chapter. Vertical clearance is discussed in Section 2A.15.

2E.20Lateral Offset

Standard · Guidance · Option · Support

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except where shielded by a rigid traffic barrier, the minimum lateral offset outside the usable roadway shoulder for post-mounted freeway/expressway signs or overhead sign supports, either side of the roadway, shall be 6 feet (also applies outside a curb). If located within the clear zone, signs shall be mounted on crashworthy supports (see definition in Section 1C.02) or shielded by appropriate crashworthy barriers.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Where practicable, a sign should not be less than 12 feet from the edge of the nearest traffic lane. Large guide signs especially should be farther removed, preferably 30 feet or more.
  2. 03Where an expressway median is 12 feet or less in width, consideration should be given to spanning both roadways without a center support.
  3. 04Where an overhead sign support cannot be placed sufficiently far from traffic, it should be designed to minimize impact forces or be adequately shielded by a suitably designed traffic barrier.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Butterfly-type sign supports and other overhead non-crashworthy sign supports shall not be installed in gores or other unshielded locations within the clear zone.
Option — Permissive
  1. 06Lesser clearances, but generally not less than 6 feet, may be used on connecting roadways or ramps at interchanges.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 07Refer to Section 2A.16 for additional information about lateral offset.

2E.21Interchange Guide Signs

Support · Standard · Guidance · Option

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01For some applications, guide signing for interchanges depends on the interchange classifications in Section 2E.11. Provisions on classification-based guide signing are found in Sections 2E.23 through 2E.26, 2E.46 through 2E.48, and 2E.51 through 2E.53.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02The signs at interchanges and on their approaches shall include Advance Interchange guide signs and Exit Direction signs. Consistent destination messages shall be displayed on these signs.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03New destination information should not be introduced into the major sign sequence for one interchange, nor should destination information be dropped.
  2. 04Guide signs placed in advance of an interchange deceleration lane should be spaced at least 800 feet apart.
  3. 05Use of Supplemental guide signing should be minimized per Section 2E.51.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06Figure 2E-2 shows a typical sequence of interchange guide signs.
  2. 07In some instances the interchange providing the most direct/preferred access to a destination might differ in opposing directions of travel due to crossroad configuration or a partial interchange.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 08For each direction of travel, guide signing to a destination should be via the exit with the most direct or preferred access, even when this results in a destination being served by different interchanges for opposing directions of travel (see Figure 2E-1).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09Refer to Section 2D.31 for additional Interchange Guide signs.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 10Exits should be identified on signs using street names, community names, control cities, and/or route markers.
  2. 11When an interchange provides more than one exit to the same street, cardinal direction identifiers should be included to distinguish between the exits.
Option — Permissive
  1. 12The Destination and Street Name with Arrow (G8(CA) series) signs may be used in freeway interchange areas.

2E.22Interchange Exit Numbering

Standard · Support · Guidance · Option

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Interchange exit numbering shall use the reference location sign exit numbering method. The consecutive exit numbering method shall not be used. Exit numbers shall correspond to the posted Reference Location or Enhanced Reference Location signs.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Reference location sign exit numbering assists road users in determining destination distances and travel mileage, assists reporting location during an incident/breakdown, assists responders, and assists agencies because the numbering sequence need not change when new interchanges are added.
  2. 03Interchange exit numbering provides valuable orientation on a freeway or expressway. Feasibility on an expressway depends largely on the extent grade separations are provided; where there is appreciable continuity of interchange facilities interrupted only by an occasional at-grade intersection, numbering helps the expressway user.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Interchange exit numbering shall be used at each freeway interchange exit. Exit numbers shall be displayed with each Interchange Advance Guide sign, Exit Direction sign, and Exit Gore sign, on a separate plaque above and abutting the sign. The Exit Number (E1-5P series) plaque (Figure 2E-9) shall include the word EXIT(S) and the appropriate exit number(s) in a single-line format.
  2. 04aRest areas, vista points, weigh stations, HOV facility exits, and HOV-to-HOV system connector ramps are not considered interchange exits and shall not be assigned or signed with exit numbers.
  3. 05Suffix letters shall only be used to supplement exit numbers where more than one exit is associated with the same reference mile point — not for identifying a downstream ramp split providing access to multiple highways or directions. The suffix letter shall be included on the Exit Number plaque, separated from the exit number by a space of between ½ and ¾ of the suffix letter's height. Suffix letters shall be assigned in ascending alphabetical order starting with A in the direction of increasing exit numbers, and descending, ending in A, in the opposite direction. Exit numbers shall not include cardinal direction initials of the cross route. Minimum numeral/letter sizes shall be per Tables 2E-2 through 2E-5. If used, the expressway exit numbering system shall comply with the freeway provisions.
  4. 06Where suffix letters are used, an exit of the same number without a suffix letter shall not also be used on the same route in the same direction (e.g., if EXIT 256 A exists, there shall not also be a plain EXIT 256 on that route/direction).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07Exit numbering should be determined based on the crossroad's location relative to reference location signs: (A) a crossroad at/after Mile 15 and before Mile 16 → EXIT 15 (Figure 2E-3, Drawings A/B); (B) a crossroad split by direction where one direction is downstream of Mile 18 and the other upstream → EXIT 18 (Figure 2E-3, Drawings A/B); (C) three closely-spaced interchanges (less than 1 mile apart) starting before Mile 16 and ending near/at Mile 17 → EXIT 15, EXIT 16, EXIT 17; (D) multiple interchanges too closely spaced to designate by mainline reference mile → use suffix letters (Figure 2E-3, Drawings C/D).
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Exit numbers may also be used with Supplemental guide signs (Section 2E.51) and Motorist Service signs (Chapters 2I and 2J).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09Where exit suffix letters are used and the number of exits differs by direction of travel, suffix lettering for each direction shall be based on the number of exits in that direction — e.g., three northbound exits and two southbound exits for Mile 25 yields EXIT 25 A / 25 B / 25 C northbound, and EXIT 25 B then EXIT 25 A southbound (Figure 2E-3, Drawing D).
  2. 10Except as provided in Section 2E.36 for Collector-Distributor Roadways, exit numbers and suffix letters shall only designate individual exit departure points directly from the freeway mainline, not ramp splits into two ramps after leaving the mainline.
  3. 11The Exit Number (E1-5P) plaque shall be positioned above the top right-hand edge (above and abutting) of the sign for an exit to the right (see Figure 2E-9).
  4. 12Because road users might not expect an exit to the left and might have difficulty maneuvering left, a Left Exit Number (E1-5bP, E1-5fP to E1-5kP) plaque (Figure 2E-9) shall be added above the top left-hand edge (above and abutting) for all numbered left-hand exits (see Figures 2E-18 and 2E-34). The word LEFT shall be a black legend on a yellow rectangular sign panel, centered above the word EXIT.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 13Example Exit Number plaque designs are in Figure 2E-9; incorporation on guide signs is illustrated in Figures 2E-9, 2E-9(CA), 2E-12, 2E-12(CA), 2E-14, 2E-35, 2E-36(CA), and 2E-41.
  2. 14Figure 2E-4 provides an example of Interstate route loops and spurs around major metropolitan areas; the general plan for numbering interchange exits is in Figures 2E-5 through 2E-8. Figure 2E-5 shows a circumferential route (a complete circle around a city, usually with two interchanges per mainline route). Figure 2E-6 shows a loop route (departs from and rejoins the same mainline route downstream; a three-digit Interstate route connecting two different Interstate routes is also defined as a loop). Standard — Mandatory Requirement

If exit numbering is not used, the main roadway Interchange Advance guide signs should use the singular EXIT in the distance messages.

2E.37Freeway-to-Freeway Interchanges

Support · Guidance · Standard · Option

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Freeway-to-freeway interchanges are major decision points where the effect of taking a wrong ramp cannot easily be corrected — reversing direction on the connecting freeway or reentering to continue on the intended course is usually not possible. Examples are shown in Figure 2E-33.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Sign messages should contain only the route shield, cardinal direction, and the name of the next control city on the route. Arrows should point as indicated in Section 2D.08, except where Overhead Arrow-per-Lane or Diagrammatic Advance signs are used per Sections 2E.39 through 2E.41.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03An off-route movement is the movement not following the through route; drivers might not expect it to be to the left or an optional lane at a split (Figures 2E-38 and 2E-39). Section 2E.22 covers the Left Exit Number plaque at splits where the off-route movement is to the left. Sections 2E.39 and 2E.40 cover Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs for splits/multi-lane exits with an option lane. Section 2E.41 covers Diagrammatic Advance guide signs for complex ramp departure geometry.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04The roadway for the off-route shall be signed as an exit. If exit numbering is used, signs shall comply with Section 2E.22. Distance messages on Advance guide signs shall comply with Section 2E.23.
  2. 05Overhead signs shall be used at a distance of 1 mile and at the theoretical gore of each connecting ramp. When Overhead Arrow-per-Lane or Diagrammatic Advance guide signs are used, they shall be located per Sections 2E.40 and 2E.41 respectively.
Option — Permissive
  1. 06The Advance guide signs at the ½-mile and 2-mile locations may also be mounted overhead.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07An Advisory Exit Speed (W13-2) sign should be used where an engineering study shows a speed reduction message is necessary for ramp signing (Section 2C.12).

  3. 03The signs for this application shall be mounted overhead. When arrows are used, each arrow shall be located over the approximate center of the lane to which it applies.
  4. 04Where one roadway of the split carries the through route, the other roadway shall be signed as an exit. If exit numbering is used, signs shall comply with Section 2E.22. Distance messages shall comply with Section 2E.23.

2E.39Signing for Option Lanes at Splits and Multi-Lane Exits

Support · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Some freeway/expressway splits or multi-lane exit interchanges contain an interior option lane serving both movements, where traffic can leave the route or remain on it (or choose either destination at a split) from the same lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02On freeways and expressways, Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign designs (Sections 2E.40 and 2E.41) shall be used for all multi-lane exits at major interchanges (Section 2E.11) that have an optional exit lane also carrying the through route (Figures 2E-36, 2E-36(CA), 2E-37, and 2E-42) and for all splits with an option lane (Figure 2E-38). Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs shall not be used on freeways/expressways for any other exit or split types, including single-lane exits and splits without an option lane.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for errors in Paragraph 2 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03The Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign design (Section 2E.40) should also be considered for multi-lane exits with an option lane at intermediate interchanges (Section 2E.11), based on factors such as the need to optimize mainline operation by maximizing option-lane usage, the extent of high-exit-volume periods, and the nature of traffic primarily using the option lane during high-volume periods.
  2. 04Signing at intermediate interchanges with an optional exit lane where the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane design is not warranted, or at multi-lane exits at minor interchanges with an optional exit lane, should use signing per Section 2E.42.

2E.40Design of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane Guide Signs for Option Lanes

Support · Standard · Option · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs (Figure 2E-35) are used where an option lane is present at freeway/expressway multi-lane exit interchanges and splits. They display an upward-pointing arrow above each lane conveying the direction(s) served at the departure point. Where an option lane is present, these signs have been shown superior to other designs because they convey positive direction about which destination/direction each approach lane serves, particularly the option lane, which is otherwise difficult to clearly sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs per Section 2E.39 shall be used at all new or reconstructed freeway/expressway locations and where replacement of existing sign support structures is necessitated by reconstruction. The sign at the exit or split shall be located at or immediately near the point where exiting lanes begin to diverge from through lanes (or, for a split, where approach lanes begin to diverge), preserving the relation of arrows to their lanes. It shall not be located at or near the theoretical gore.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03At existing or non-reconstructed locations where an overhead Exit Direction sign exists at the theoretical gore and the existing sign support structure is retained, that Exit Direction sign may continue to be used in conjunction with replacement of the advance signs using the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane design.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04If an existing Exit Direction sign is retained per Paragraph 3, an Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign shall not be used at that location at or near the theoretical gore. New installations of Exit Direction and Pull-Through signs shall not be permitted in conjunction with Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs on new or reconstructed facilities.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs should be located at approximately ½ mile and 1 mile in advance of the exit or split, and at approximately 2 miles in advance where space is available and conditions allow.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs shall be designed per: (A) except as provided in Section 2E.42 for partial-width signs, the sign shall include an upward-pointing (vertical, curved, or bifurcated) arrow for each approach lane; (B) each arrow's shaft shall be located over the approximate center of its lane; (C) arrows for continuing through lanes shall be vertically upward-pointing (Figure 2E-36 and 2E-36(CA)) unless the through lanes are on significantly curved alignment beyond the theoretical gore (Figure 2E-37); (D) the arrow for a lane that must exit shall curve in the exit direction and be accompanied by black-on-yellow EXIT (E11-1a) and ONLY (E11-1b) sign panels adjacent to the lower end of the arrow shaft — these panels shall not be used for a split of two overlapping routes where neither diverging route is designated as an exit; where through lanes curve and the exit continues straight, upward vertical arrows shall be used for the exit movement and curved arrows for the through movement (Figure 2E-37); (E) the arrow for an optional exit lane also carrying the through route shall have a single shaft bifurcating into a vertical arrow and a curving arrow matching the through/exit lane configuration; (F) for splits with an option lane, the arrow for the lane from which either split direction can be accessed shall have a single shaft bifurcating into two upward-pointing curving arrows (Figure 2E-38); (G) a vertical white line shall separate the route shields/destinations for the two diverging movements; (H) the distance to the exit or split shall be displayed below the off-movement destination on advance signs at the 1-mile and 2-mile locations; (I) the number of lanes displayed shall correspond to the number of lanes at that sign's location — an advance sign shall not depict lanes added downstream; (J) for numbered exits, the Exit Number (E1-5P) or Left Exit Number (E1-5bP) plaque shall be used at the top (above and abutting), per Section 2E.23; for unnumbered left exits, a LEFT (E1-5mP) plaque shall be added at the top left-hand edge.
  2. 06aThe ONLY (E11-1b) sign panel shall not be used without the EXIT (E11-1a) sign panel.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs should additionally be designed so: (A) no more than one destination is displayed per movement, and no more than two destinations per sign; (B) the arrowhead(s) for the diverging movement should be positioned lower on the sign than the arrowhead(s) for the movement continuing straight ahead, independent of which movement carries the through route — for freeway/expressway splits (rather than exits), arrowheads should be at approximately the same height; (C) route shields, cardinal directions, and destinations should be positioned so they are clearly related to the arrowhead(s) they apply to; (D) the cardinal direction should be adjacent to the route shield for exits/splits leading in a single cardinal direction; (E) the vertical white line separating the diverging movements should not descend below the top of the through-lane arrowheads, and should be positioned approximately halfway between the diverging arrowheads for the optional movement lane (see Figure 2E-35).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 08Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs shall not be used to depict a downstream split of an exit ramp on a sign located on the mainline.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09Specific guidelines for more detailed design of Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs are in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 10The arrow heights for Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs on freeways and expressways shall be as shown in Table 2E-6.
Option — Permissive
  1. 11Where extra emphasis of an especially low advisory ramp speed is needed, an EXIT XX MPH (E13-2) sign panel (Figure 2E-14) may be placed below the applicable destination legend to supplement, not replace, the exit/ramp advisory speed warning signs.
  2. 12Warning Beacons complying with Section 2E.25 may be used with the E13-2 sign panel.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 13An example of guide signing for a narrow gore at a split with an option lane is shown in Figure 2E-39, and for a narrow gore at a two-lane exit with an option lane in Figure 2E-40.
Option — Permissive
  1. 14Where there is 800 feet or more between the beginning of the lane diverge and the theoretical gore, signs indicating the destinations allowed by each lane may be added near the theoretical gore to reinforce positive guidance (Figures 2E-39 and 2E-40).

2E.41Design of Freeway and Expressway Diagrammatic Advance Guide Signs

Support · Option · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01The Diagrammatic Advance guide sign (Figure 2E-41) shows a simplified graphic view of the exit departure arrangement relative to the main highway at an interchange, to provide advance notice of complex or unexpected geometry/ramp departures and/or depict successive decision points where added context helps interpret subsequent primary Interchange Advance guide signs. Unlike Diagrammatic signs in previous Manual editions, it does not depict which or how many specific lanes serve a particular destination, or lanes added/reduced.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02A Diagrammatic Advance guide sign may be used in advance of the interchange guide sign sequence, or in lieu of an Interchange Advance guide sign located 2 miles in advance of the exit, to supplement conventional or Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs used for a downstream interchange.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Diagrammatic Advance guide signs shall be designed so: (A) the graphic legend is a plan view showing a simplified schematic of the relative through and off-ramp movements; (B) no symbols or route shields are used as a substitute for arrowheads; (C) they are not installed at the Exit Direction sign location (Section 2E.25); (D) the EXIT ONLY sign panel is not used on Diagrammatic Advance guide signs in advance of the interchange; (E) for numbered exits, the Exit Number (E1-5P) or Left Exit Number (E1-5bP) plaque is used at the top per Section 2E.22, and for unnumbered left exits, the LEFT (E1-5aP) plaque is used at the top left edge; (F) the graphic does not depict deceleration or auxiliary lanes; (G) arrow shafts contain no lane lines; (H) destination legends for off-movements are positioned to the side of the arrow from which the ramp departs.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04Diagrammatic Advance guide signs should additionally be designed so: (A) no more than one destination is displayed per movement; (B) the arrowhead for the diverging movement is positioned lower than the arrowhead for the movement continuing straight ahead, independent of which carries the through route (Figure 2E-42) — for splits (rather than exits), arrowheads should be at approximately the same height; (C) arrow shaft widths do not vary by movement; (D) route shields, cardinal directions, and destinations are positioned clearly related to the arrowhead(s), with the off-movement arrowhead pointing toward the route shield or, for unnumbered routes, the upper destination legend line; (E) for exits/splits leading in a single direction, the cardinal direction is placed adjacent to the route shield and the destination below it; (F) where two exits are displayed, the through-route control destination is omitted; (G) the distance legend is placed below the exit destination legend — for splits where neither direction carries a through route, centered below the diagrammatic arrow; where successive exits from the same side are displayed, the distance is below the first exit's destination legend with the second exit's distance omitted; where successive exits from opposite sides are displayed, the first exit's distance is centered below the diagrammatic arrow with the second omitted.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Diagrammatic Advance guide signs shall not be used at cloverleaf interchanges to depict separate downstream departures from a collector-distributor roadway.
  2. 06Diagrammatic Advance guide signs located on the main roadway shall not be used to depict a downstream split of an exit ramp.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 07Specific guidelines for more detailed design of Diagrammatic Advance guide signs are in the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (Section 1A.05).
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Where extra emphasis of an especially low advisory ramp speed is needed, an EXIT XX MPH (E13-2) sign panel (Figure 2E-14) may be placed below the applicable destination legend to supplement, not replace, the exit/ramp advisory speed warning signs.
  2. 09Warning Beacons complying with Section 2E.25 may be used with the E13-2 sign panel.
  3. 10Diagrammatic Advance guide signs may be used on any class of roadway and may be modified to depict relative movements for intersections on conventional roads.

2E.42Signing for Intermediate and Minor Interchange Multi-Lane Exits with an Option Lane

Support · Guidance · Standard · Option

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Intermediate and minor multi-lane exits might have an operational need for an option lane only during peak periods when excessive queues might otherwise develop. In such cases, full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing (Sections 2E.39 and 2E.40) might not be practical, depending on option-lane usage and nearby interchange spacing, particularly in non-rural areas.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02When full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing is not practical, signing for an intermediate or minor interchange with a multi-lane exit and an option lane also carrying the through route should use a partial-width form of the Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign (Figures 2E-43 through 2E-45(CA)), displaying arrows only for the option lane and mandatory exit lane(s), using the same bifurcated arrow type for the option lane and curved arrow type for the exit-only lane(s) as the full-width design. The exit-movement legend should be clearly aligned with the arrows pointing toward the exit, not the vertical arrowhead of the bifurcated through-movement arrow.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03The through route and/or destination shall not be displayed on the partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide sign.
  2. 04Partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signs shall be located per Section 2E.40 for full-width signs (Figures 2E-44 and 2E-45).
Option — Permissive
  1. 05At an intermediate or minor interchange with a multi-lane exit and an option lane also carrying the through route, where full-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signing is not practical, conventional signing per Paragraphs 7 through 9 may be used (Figures 2E-46 and 2E-47).
  2. 06When either full-width or partial-width Overhead Arrow-per-Lane signing is used at existing or non-reconstructed locations where an overhead Exit Direction sign exists at the theoretical gore and the existing sign support structure is retained, that Exit Direction sign may continue to be used in conjunction with replacement of the advance signs using the partial-width design (Figure 2E-44).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 6 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07When conventional signing is used, the option lane should not be signed on the Interchange Advance guide signs. For exits involving an added auxiliary lane not present at the Interchange Advance guide sign locations, but without a lane drop (Figure 2E-47), a sequence of post-mounted or overhead-mounted Interchange Advance guide signs should be used, located per interchange classification (Section 2E.11). The Exit Direction sign should be located at the theoretical gore, displaying a diagonally upward-pointing arrow above each departing lane, without the EXIT ONLY legend.
  2. 08For interchanges that also have a lane drop (Figure 2E-46), the Interchange Advance guide and Exit Direction signs should follow Section 2E.28; the Exit Direction sign should be at the theoretical gore and contain the EXIT ONLY (E11-1e) sign panel.
  3. 09Where the modified Overhead Arrow-per-Lane guide signs are not used, the presence of the option lane should be conveyed by post-mounted lane-use (R3-8 series) signs (Section 2B.30), sized appropriately for conspicuity, located in succession with the Interchange Advance guide signs where the option and exit lanes have developed (Figure 2E-46). Where the exiting lane(s) have not developed and the option lane is created by an auxiliary lane that exits, R3-8 signs should be located only adjacent to where the lanes are fully developed, not in advance of or along the transition (Figure 2E-47).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 9 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
  2. 10The use of a down arrow on overhead freeway/expressway guide signs has been shown to be misinterpreted by road users as an indication of a dedicated lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 11Interchange Advance guide signs that are mounted overhead shall not display a down arrow over an option lane.

2E.43Number of Signs at an Overhead Installation and Sign Spreading

Guidance · Support · Option

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01If overhead signs are warranted (Section 2A.13), the number of signs at these locations should be limited to only those essential in communicating pertinent destination information. Exit Direction signs for a single exit and Interchange Advance Guide signs should have only one sign with one or two destinations. Regulatory signs, such as speed limits, should not be used with overhead guide sign installations. Because road users have limited time to read/comprehend messages, there should not be more than three guide signs displayed at any one location on the overhead structure or its support.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 1 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02At overhead locations, more than one sign may be installed to advise of a multiple exit condition. If the ramp or crossroad has complex/unusual geometrics, additional signs with confirming messages may be provided.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Sign spreading is a concept where major overhead signs are spaced so road users are not overloaded with a group of signs at a single location. Figure 2E-48 illustrates an example.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04Where overhead signing is used, sign spreading should be used at all single-exit interchanges and to the extent possible at multi-exit interchanges, accomplished by: (A) the Exit Direction sign should be the only guide sign used near the gore (other than the Exit Gore sign), located overhead near the theoretical gore, generally on an overhead sign support structure; (B) the Interchange Advance guide sign for the next interchange exit should be placed near the crossroad location — if the crossroad goes over the mainline, on the overcrossing structure or a separate structure immediately in front of it.

2E.44Closely-Spaced Interchanges

Support · Guidance · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Section 2E.43 contains information on sign spreading where the Exit Direction sign and the Interchange Advance guide sign for the next interchange are mounted overhead. Sign spreading is particularly beneficial where interchanges are closely spaced and overhead signing is used with Interchange Sequence signs (Paragraph 2).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Interchange Sequence signs (Section 2E.24) should be used at closely-spaced interchanges, identifying and showing street names and distances for the next two or three exits (Figure 2E-11).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Interchange Advance guide signs for closely-spaced interchanges shall show information for only one interchange.

2E.45Guide Signing in Tunnels and Similar Structures

Support · Option · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Applying freeway/expressway guide sign provisions in tunnels and similar structures presents unique challenges from extended, continuous constrained vertical/horizontal clearances. This is particularly evident where interchange exit ramps are inside the tunnel requiring guide signing, so typical layouts might not always be usable; interchange guide signs might need to be limited to one destination, with others on Supplemental guide signs (Section 2E.51). Acceptable modification methods are in Paragraph 2.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02Overhead-mounted guide signs in tunnels, or other structures with extended constrained clearances, may be modified to accommodate limited vertical clearance: (A) sign legend elements may be arranged side by side, e.g., route shields to the left of the destination instead of above; (B) the Exit Number plaque (Section 2E.22) may be placed at the right-hand edge (right exits) or left-hand edge (left exits) instead of the top edge, with reduced letter height not less than 6 inches for EXIT(S) and not less than 12 inches for numerals/suffixes; (C) destination and roadway names may be displayed in reduced letter heights not less than 10.67 inches when acceptable given reduced speed and other factors, maintaining adequate space for legibility; (D) unusually long names that cannot be shortened/abbreviated may use Series D letters in lieu of Series E (modified).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 2, Item C text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Applicability of Paragraph 2 shall be limited to signs within the limits of the tunnel or other similar structure and shall not extend to the approaches or departures.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Unlike typical guide signs exposed to rain, guide signs in tunnels accumulate grime and residue quickly, reducing visual contrast and retroreflectivity, so they generally need more maintenance.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Overhead signs in tunnels should have external or internal illumination to ensure adequate visibility between scheduled maintenance and cleanings.
  2. 06One or more Interchange Sequence signs (Section 2E.24) should be used on the approach to the tunnel entrance to display distances to the next interchanges with exit ramps inside the tunnel or immediately following it.
  3. 07Supplementary pavement markings (word, arrow, and/or route shield markings) should be considered inside the tunnel in addition to basic lane and edge line markings.
