Part 2 · Signs · Chapter 2G

Preferential and Managed Lane Signs

Regulatory, warning, and guide signs for preferential lanes (HOV, bus, taxi, and similar restricted-use lanes) and managed lanes (including priced/tolled "Express Lanes"), plus part-time shoulder travel, active lane management signals, and variable speed limits on freeways and expressways.

2G.01Scope

Support · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Preferential lanes are lanes designated for special traffic uses such as high-occupancy vehicles (HOVs), light rail, buses, or taxis. Treatments range from restricting a turning lane to a certain vehicle class during peak periods, to a fully separate roadway system within a corridor.
  2. 02Preferential lanes might be barrier-separated (separate alignment or physically separated by a barrier/median), buffer-separated (separated from general-purpose lanes only by a narrow buffer created with pavement markings), or contiguous (separated only by a lane line). They might allow continuous access with adjacent general-purpose lanes or restrict access to designated locations only. They might operate in a constant direction or as reversible lanes (some counter-flow to adjacent traffic on a divided highway).
  3. 03Preferential lanes might operate 24 hours, for extended periods, peak periods only, special events, or other activities.
  4. 04Open-road tolling lanes and toll plaza lanes that segregate traffic by payment method are not considered preferential lanes — see Chapter 2F for their signing.
  5. 05Managed lanes typically restrict access with adjacent general-purpose lanes to designated locations only.
  6. 06Under certain operational strategies, such as an HOV lane's occupancy requirement changing in response to actual congestion levels, a managed lane is a special type of preferential lane (see Sections 2G.03 through 2G.07).
  7. 07A managed lane operated in real time in response to changing conditions might operate as an HOV lane for a period of time as needed to manage congestion.
  8. 08Sections 2G.17 through 2G.19 contain additional information regarding signs for managed lanes that use tolling or pricing as a management strategy.
  9. 09Section 9B.04 contains information regarding Preferential Lane signs for bicycle lanes.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 10Unless otherwise provided, the provisions of this Chapter shall not apply to bicycle lanes.

2G.03Regulatory Signs for Preferential Lanes — General

Standard · Support · Option · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01When a preferential lane is established, the Preferential Lane regulatory signs (see Figure 2G-1 and Figure 2G-1(CA)) and pavement markings (see Chapter 3E) for these lanes shall be used to advise road users.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02Preferential Lane (R3-10, R3-11 series through R3-15 series, R33(CA) series, R82(CA) series, R88(CA), R90-1(CA), R91(CA) series, R94(CA), SR50(CA) series, and SR60(CA) series) regulatory signs consist of several general types: (A) Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs define occupancy requirements for an HOV lane (e.g., "2 OR MORE PERSONS PER VEHICLE") or vehicle types not meeting the minimum occupancy that may still use the lane (motorcycles, Inherently Low Emission Vehicles (ILEVs)) (Section 2G.04); (B) Preferential Lane Operation signs notify road users of the days/hours the restriction is in effect (Section 2G.05); (C) Preferential Lane Advance signs notify that a restriction begins ahead (Section 2G.06); (D) Preferential Lane Ends signs notify of the termination point (Section 2G.07).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Regulatory signs applicable only to a preferential lane shall be distinguished from those applicable to general-purpose lanes by the inclusion of the applicable symbol(s) and/or word(s).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04The symbol and word message displayed will vary based on the type of allowed traffic and related operational constraints established for the lane (HOV, bus, taxi, etc.).
Option — Permissive
  1. 05Changeable message signs may supplement, substitute for, or be incorporated into static Preferential Lane regulatory signs where travel conditions change, or where multiple operational strategies (variable occupancy requirements or vehicle types) are used and varied throughout the day/week or in real time.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06Figure 2G-1 illustrates examples of changeable messages incorporated into static Preferential Lane regulatory signs displaying open and closed status using lane-use control signal indications (see Chapter 4T).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 07When changeable message signs (see Chapter 2L) are used as regulatory signs for preferential lanes, they shall be the required sign size and display the required letter height and legend format corresponding to the roadway facility type and design speed.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 08On conventional roads, whether to use a post-mounted or overhead version of a sign should be based on an engineering study considering available space, existing signs for adjacent general-purpose lanes, roadway/traffic characteristics, proximity to existing overhead signs, ability to install overhead signs, and other local factors.
  2. 09If overhead regulatory signs for a preferential lane are located at approximately the same longitudinal position as overhead signs for general-purpose lanes, the preferential lane signs should be separated laterally to the maximum extent practicable to minimize conflicting information, while maintaining the visual relationship to the lanes below.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 10If used, overhead Preferential Lane (R3-13 series, R3-14 series, and R3-15 series) regulatory signs shall be installed on the side of the roadway where the entrance to the preferential lane is located, with appropriate adjustments to the sign message.
Option — Permissive
  1. 11Where a median of sufficient width is available, the R3-13 series and R3-15 series signs may be post-mounted.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 12Sizes for Preferential Lane regulatory signs differ to reflect the design speeds of each roadway facility type; Table 2G-1 provides sizes for each.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 13The edges of Preferential Lane regulatory signs post-mounted on a median barrier should not project beyond the outer edges of the barrier, including where lateral clearance is limited.
Option — Permissive
  1. 14Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane regulatory signs post-mounted on a median barrier that are 72 inches or less in width may be skewed up to 45 degrees to fit within the barrier width, or mounted higher such that vertical clearance to the bottom of the sign, light fixture, or structural support (whichever is lowest) is not less than 17 feet above any portion of pavement/shoulders. For mounting heights on State highways, refer to Section 2A.15.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 15Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane regulatory signs post-mounted on a median barrier that are wider than 72 inches shall be mounted with vertical clearance complying with Section 2A.15 for overhead mounting if any portion of the sign extends over the roadway or shoulder.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 15aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for errors in Paragraph 15 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 16On conventional roadways, Preferential Lane regulatory sign spacing should be determined by engineering judgment based on speed, block length, distances from adjacent intersections, and other site-specific considerations.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 17Sections 2G.04 and 2G.05 contain provisions regarding placement of Preferential Lane regulatory signs on freeways and expressways.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 18Signs incorporating the diamond symbol (Figure 2G-1 and Figure 2G-1(CA)) shall be used exclusively with HOV preferential lanes to indicate occupancy requirement and time restrictions. Signs without the diamond symbol shall be used with preferential lanes designated for other vehicle types (bus and/or taxi use).
  2. 19The diamond symbol shall not be used on the bus, taxi, or bicycle Preferential Lane signs.
  3. 20Vehicle Occupancy Definition, Preferential Lane Operation, and Preferential Lane Advance regulatory signs for HOV lanes shall display the minimum allowable vehicle occupancy requirement immediately after the word message HOV.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 21Owning/operating agencies determine how HOV lanes are operated and their minimum occupancy requirements. Federal requirements for certain vehicle types not meeting the minimum occupancy (to remain Federal-aid eligible) and requirements for proposed significant operational changes are in the "Federal-Aid Highway Program Guidance on High Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) Lanes."
  2. 22Figures 2G-2 and 2G-3 illustrate regulatory signs for the beginning, along the length, and at the end of contiguous or buffer-separated preferential lanes with continuous access to adjacent general-purpose lanes.
  3. 23For State highways, refer to Caltrans' Managed Lanes Guidelines (Section 1A.05).
  4. 24Refer to CVC § 21655.5 regarding Exclusive- or Preferential-Use Lanes for High Occupancy Vehicles.

In practice: the diamond symbol is the load-bearing visual cue across this whole chapter — it appears on regulatory, warning, and guide signs alike whenever a vehicle-occupancy requirement applies, and its absence on an otherwise similar sign format signals a non-HOV preferential lane (bus/taxi).

2G.04Vehicle Occupancy Definition Signs (R3-10 Series and R3-13 Series)

Standard · Guidance · Support · Option

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The R3-10, R3-13, and R3-13a Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs shall be used where agencies determine it is appropriate to define the minimum occupancy of vehicles allowed to use an HOV lane.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02The Inherently Low Emission Vehicle (ILEV) (R3-10a) sign should be used when a properly labeled and certified ILEV, regardless of occupant count, is permitted to use an HOV lane. When used, ILEV signs should be post-mounted in advance of and at intervals along the HOV lane based on engineering judgment and placement of other Preferential Lane regulatory signs. The R3-10a sign applies only to HOV lanes and should not be used with other preferential lane applications.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 02aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 2 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
  2. 03ILEVs are defined by EPA as vehicles having no fuel vapor (hydrocarbon) emissions, certified by EPA as meeting the standards in 40 CFR §88.311-93 and 40 CFR §88.312-93(c).
Option — Permissive
  1. 03aThe AUTOS/PICKUPS 2 SEATERS WITH 2 PERSONS OK (R91bP(CA)) plaque may be installed below the R3-10 sign for preferential lane facilities at toll plazas that require three or more persons per vehicle but can also be used by vehicles designed by the manufacturer for no more than two occupants. Refer to Streets & Highways Code § 30101.8.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Section 2G.18 contains information regarding legends of Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs for a priced managed lane with an occupancy requirement for non-toll travel.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05For barrier-separated, buffer-separated, or contiguous preferential lanes where access is restricted to designated locations on freeways/expressways, an overhead Vehicle Occupancy Definition (R3-13 or R3-13a) sign shall be installed at least ½ mile in advance of the beginning of or initial entry point to an HOV lane. These signs shall only be displayed in advance of the beginning of, or initial or intermediate entry point to, HOV lanes.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05aThe R3-13 or R3-13a sign should be installed at least ¼ mile in advance of any intermediate access points or gaps in the barrier where vehicles are legally permitted to enter the access-restricted preferential lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06For buffer-separated or contiguous HOV lanes where access is restricted to designated locations on freeways/expressways, the sequence of a post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11a) sign followed by a post-mounted Vehicle Occupancy Definition (R3-10) sign shall be located at intervals not greater than ½ mile along the length of designated gaps where vehicles may legally access the HOV lane, and within designated enforcement areas defined by the operating agency.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07For buffer-separated or contiguous HOV lanes where access is restricted to designated locations, the R3-11a + R3-10 sequence may be located at intervals of approximately ½ mile along the length of the HOV lane.
  2. 08For barrier-separated HOV lanes, the R3-11a + R3-10 sequence may be located at intervals of approximately ½ mile along the HOV lane, at intermediate entry points, and at designated enforcement areas.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08aRefer to Figure 2G-1 for other post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation signs used in lieu of the R3-11a sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09For buffer-separated or contiguous HOV lanes with continuous access to adjacent general-purpose lanes on freeways/expressways, the sequence of a post-mounted R3-11a sign followed by R3-10 and, if appropriate, R3-10a (ILEV) signs shall be located at intervals not greater than ½ mile along the HOV lane.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 10On freeways and expressways, signs within each Preferential Lane regulatory sign sequence should be separated by a minimum of 800 feet and a maximum of 1,000 feet.
  2. 11On conventional roads, the distance between signs within each sequence should be determined by engineering judgment based on speed, block length, distances from adjacent intersections, and other site-specific factors.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 11aBuffer-separated preferential lanes with continuous access to adjacent general-purpose lanes are not used on State Highways in California.
  2. 11bRefer to Figure 2G-1 for other post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation signs used in lieu of the R3-11a sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 12For all types of direct access ramps that provide access to or lead to HOV lanes, an overhead Vehicle Occupancy Definition (R3-13a) sign, and an ILEV (R3-10a) sign if appropriate, shall be used at the beginning or initial entry point for the direct access ramp (see Figure 2G-16(CA)).
Option — Permissive
  1. 13The (HOV) NO TRUCKS 3 AXLES OR MORE – NO VEHICLES WITH TRAILERS (R91-4(CA)) sign may be placed adjacent to the HOV lane, as needed, where trucks or vehicles with trailers are frequently observed using the HOV lanes, and on surface streets approaching direct access ramps that lead to HOV lanes.

2G.05Preferential Lane Operation Signs (R3-11 Series and R3-14 Series)

Support · Guidance · Standard · Option

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Standardized sizes of post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11 series) signs are consistent to accommodate future addition/removal of a single line of legend, with each size accommodating two lines for times/days in effect — beneficial for ordering sign materials and making future legend changes.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Where the regulation is in effect during more than one daily time period (e.g., morning and afternoon peaks), the height of the R3-11 and R3-14 series signs should be suitably increased to accommodate the additional line(s).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03When used, post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11 series, SR60-4(CA), and SR60-6(CA)) signs shall be located adjacent to the preferential lane; overhead Preferential Lane Operation (R3-14 series and SR60-8(CA)) signs shall be mounted directly over the lane.
  2. 04Post-mounted (R3-11 series, SR60-4(CA), SR60-6(CA)) legend sequence: Top lines — lanes applicable ("RIGHT LANE" or "2 RIGHT LANES"); Middle lines — eligible uses ("HOV 2+ ONLY", "BUSES ONLY", "BUSES-TAXIS ONLY", "TRAINS ONLY", or other uses/turning movements); Bottom lines — applicable times/days ("7 AM – 9 AM", "6:30 AM – 9:30 AM, MON-FRI", or "24 HOURS").
  3. 05Overhead (R3-14 series and SR60-8(CA)) legend sequence: Top lines — eligible uses; Bottom lines — applicable times/days with the time/day placed above the down arrow. When operating periods exceed the available line width, hours and days shall be stacked as shown for the R3-14a sign in Figure 2G-1.
  4. 06For full-time (24 hours/day, 7 days/week) restrictions, the Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11b, R3-11c, R3-11e, R3-11g, R3-14b, R3-14e, R3-14g, SR60-4(CA), and SR60-6(CA)) signs that include a period-of-operation legend shall be modified to display no legend relative to the period of operation, except as provided in Paragraph 7.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for errors in Paragraph 6 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07In lieu of removing the period-of-operation text for full-time operation, the legend 24 HOURS may be substituted for times/days on the R3-11, R3-11a, R3-11d, R3-11f, R3-11g, R3-14, R3-14a, R3-14c, R3-14d, R3-14f, and SR60-8(CA) signs.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 07aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for errors in Paragraph 7 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
  2. 08The 24 HOURS legend on Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11 and R3-14 series) signs reinforces full-time operation where nearby facilities have differing hours (some part-time, some full-time), or where the same lane changes from part-time to full-time operation along its length.
  3. 08aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 8 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09Full-time Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11b, R3-11c, R3-11e, R3-11g, R3-14b, R3-14e, R3-14g) signs that omit the period-of-operation legend shall not be used where the restriction is in effect only part-time.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for errors in Paragraph 9 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Option — Permissive
  1. 10Where additional movements are allowed from a preferential lane on the approach to an intersection by vehicles not meeting the regulation, the legend format on the middle lines of R3-11 series signs and top line of R3-14 series signs may be modified to accommodate the allowable movement (e.g., "HOV 2+ AND RIGHT TURNS ONLY").
  2. 10aThe Mandatory/Optional HOV Movement Lane Control (R94(CA)) sign may be installed on local streets when one of the mandatory turn lanes (left or right) is designated HOV-only.
  3. 11The MOTORCYCLES ALLOWED (R3-11hP) plaque may be used where motorcycles, regardless of occupant count, are allowed to use an HOV lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 12If used, the MOTORCYCLES ALLOWED plaque shall be mounted below a post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11, R3-11a, or R3-11b) sign.
  2. 13For all barrier-separated, buffer-separated, or contiguous preferential lanes where access is restricted to designated locations, an overhead Preferential Lane Operation (R3-14 series or SR60-8(CA)) sign shall be used at the beginning/initial entry point on freeways and expressways, and at locations on conventional roadways where the preferential lane is not the outermost lane, and at any intermediate entry points or gaps. Post-mounted (R3-11 series, SR60-4(CA), SR60-6(CA)) signs shall be used only as a supplement to the overhead signs at those locations.
  3. 14For buffer-separated or contiguous preferential lanes with continuous access to adjacent general-purpose lanes (Figures 2G-2 and 2G-3), an overhead Preferential Lane Operation (R3-14 series) sign shall be used at the beginning or initial entry point of the preferential lane on freeways and expressways.
Option — Permissive
  1. 15On conventional roads, R3-11 series post-mounted signs may be used in lieu of or in addition to overhead R3-14 series signs, except where overhead signs are required per Paragraph 13.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 15aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 15 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Option — Permissive
  1. 16Additional overhead (R3-14 series and SR60-8(CA)) or post-mounted (R3-11 series) Preferential Lane Operation signs may be provided along the length of any type of preferential lane.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 16aBuffer-separated preferential lanes with continuous access to adjacent general-purpose lanes are not used on State Highways in California.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 17For all direct access ramps that provide access to or lead to preferential lanes, a post-mounted Preferential Lane Operation (R3-11 series) sign shall be used at the beginning or initial entry point of the direct access ramp.
Option — Permissive
  1. 18For direct access ramps to preferential lanes, an overhead Preferential Lane Operation (R3-14 series) sign may be used at the beginning or initial entry point to supplement the required post-mounted signs.
  2. 19Lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) may be used at access points to preferential lanes to indicate that a ramp/access roadway, or one or more specific lanes, are open or closed (see Figure 2G-1).

2G.06Preferential Lane Advance Signs (R3-12, R3-12e, R3-12f, R3-15, R3-15a, and R3-15d)

Standard · Option · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The Preferential Lane Advance (R3-12, R3-12f, R3-15, and R3-15d, SR60-1(CA), and SR60-2(CA)) signs (see Figure 2G-1 and Figure 2G-1(CA)) shall be used for advance notification of a barrier-separated, buffer-separated, or contiguous preferential lane that is added to the general-purpose lanes (see Figure 2G-2).
  2. 02The Preferential Lane Advance (R3-12e and R3-15a) signs shall be used for advance notification of a general-purpose lane that becomes a preferential lane (see Figure 2G-3).
Option — Permissive
  1. 03The legends on the R3-12f and R3-15d signs may be modified to suit the type of preferential lane.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04On conventional roads, for general-purpose lanes that become preferential lanes, a post-mounted (R3-12e) or overhead (R3-15a) Preferential Lane Advance sign should be installed in advance of the beginning of or initial entry point to the preferential lane, at a distance determined by engineering judgment based on speed, traffic characteristics, and site-specific considerations, providing adequate opportunity for ineligible vehicles to vacate the lane.
  2. 05On freeways and expressways, for general-purpose lanes that become preferential lanes, an overhead R3-15a sign should be installed at least 1 mile in advance of the beginning of the preferential lane restriction.
Option — Permissive
  1. 06Additional post-mounted or overhead Preferential Lane Advance signs may be placed farther in advance of or closer to the beginning or initial entry points.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 07A Specific Hours/Days (R82aP(CA) or R82bP(CA)) plaque shall be used to designate the periods of operation for preferential lanes that operate on a part-time basis.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 08When used, the Specific Hours/Days plaque should be placed below the R3-12, R3-12e, R3-12f, SR60-1(CA), and SR60-2(CA) signs.

2G.07Preferential Lane Ends Signs (R3-12a, R3-12b, R3-12c, R3-12d, R3-12g, R3-12h, R3-15b, R3-15c, and R3-15e)

Standard · Guidance · Option

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01A post-mounted Preferential Lane Ends (R3-12b or R3-12h) sign shall be installed at least ½ mile in advance of the termination of a preferential lane on freeways and expressways.
  2. 02Except as provided in Paragraph 7, a post-mounted Preferential Lane Ends (R3-12a or R3-12g) sign shall be installed at the point where a preferential lane and restriction end and traffic must merge into general-purpose lanes.
  3. 03A post-mounted Preferential Lane Ends (R3-12d) sign shall be installed at least ½ mile in advance of the point where a preferential lane restriction ends and the lane becomes a general-purpose lane on freeways and expressways.
  4. 04Except as provided in Paragraph 8, a post-mounted Preferential Lane Ends (R3-12c) sign shall be installed at the point where a preferential lane restriction ends and the lane becomes a general-purpose lane.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05On conventional roads, the advance distance for Preferential Lane Ends signs should be determined by engineering judgment.
Option — Permissive
  1. 06The legends on the R3-12g and R3-15e signs may be modified to suit the type of preferential lane.
  2. 07An overhead Preferential Lane Ends (R3-15b or R3-15e) sign may be installed instead of or in addition to a post-mounted R3-12a or R3-12g sign where the lane and restriction end and traffic must merge into general-purpose lanes.
  3. 08An overhead Preferential Lane Ends (R3-15c) sign may be installed instead of or in addition to a post-mounted R3-12c sign where the restriction ends and the lane becomes a general-purpose lane.

2G.08Warning Signs on Median Barriers for Preferential Lanes

Option · Standard · Guidance

Option — Permissive
  1. 01When a warning sign applicable only to a preferential lane is installed on a median barrier with limited lateral clearance, the sign may have a vertically-oriented rectangular shape. For an HOV lane, such signs may be used instead of the HOV (W16-11P) plaque (Section 2G.09) with a standard diamond-shaped warning sign.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02When a vertically-oriented rectangular warning sign for a preferential lane is installed on a median barrier, the top portion shall be a white symbol or legend denoting the preferential lane type (diamond symbol for HOV, or legend BUS LANE) on a black background with a white border; the bottom portion shall be the standard word message/symbol of the standard warning sign as black legend on yellow background with black border (see Figure 2G-4).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Where lateral clearance is limited for a post-mounted warning sign on a median barrier, the edges should not project beyond the outer edges of the barrier.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04Where lateral clearance is limited, preferential-lane warning signs post-mounted on a median barrier that are 72 inches or less in width may be skewed up to 45 degrees to fit within the barrier width, or mounted higher such that the vertical clearance to the bottom of the sign, light fixture, or its structural support (whichever is lowest) is not less than 17 feet above pavement/shoulders. For mounting heights on State highways, refer to Section 2A.15.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Where lateral clearance is limited, Preferential Lane warning signs post-mounted on a median barrier and wider than 72 inches shall be mounted with vertical clearance complying with Section 2A.15 for overhead mounting.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06The HOV Lane Reduction (W11-1(CA)) sign should be used to warn motorists of a reduction in the number of HOV lanes.
  2. 07The HOV Merge (W59-1(CA)) sign should be used in advance of locations where HOV lanes converge, including HOV direct access ramps where high speeds/volumes prevail and merging/weaving must occur in a relatively short distance.
  3. 08When used, the HOV Advisory Exit (Ramp) Speed (W72B(CA)) sign should be placed on the left of an HOV drop ramp or freeway-to-freeway connector to advise of the comfortable negotiating speed.
  4. 09The HOV THRU TRAFFIC MERGE LEFT (RIGHT) (W74-1(CA)) sign should be used to inform motorists that the inside or outside lane of an HOV facility with two or more directional HOV lanes is being dropped at the next exit and through HOV traffic must merge into the adjacent HOV lane; it should not be used to indicate a general lane reduction.

2G.09High-Occupancy Vehicle (HOV) Plaque (W16-11P)

Option · Support

2G.17Signs for Priced Managed Lanes — General

Support · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01A priced managed lane is a managed lane that employs tolling or pricing, typically through electronic toll collection, to manage congestion levels and maintain a certain level of service. A priced managed facility typically provides a less-congested alternative to adjacent lanes along the same designated route, or to a nearby facility, that experience recurring peak-period congestion. A priced managed lane might allow non-toll travel by certain vehicles based on occupancy or other criteria, and a variety of operational strategies might be used with tolling/pricing.
  2. 02The number and combination of operational strategies applied to manage congestion or improve efficiency might be practically limited by the amount of information that can be legibly displayed and still be readily comprehended. Factors to consider include locations of signs for general-purpose interchanges and other roadway conditions, the number of intermediate access points and the need to repeat operational information, and the distance over which a signing sequence displaying all eligibility requirements can be shown.
  3. 03Because managed lanes can employ a variety of changing operational strategies, it is not practical to assign a naming convention based on specific strategies, as is more readily done for HOV, bus, or bicycle lanes. Instead, requirements, restrictions, and eligibility criteria are conveyed through a sequence of regulatory and guide signs with a more encompassing designation for directional information.
  4. 04As priced managed lanes have become prevalent, it is important to maintain a uniform naming convention to distinguish lanes that are an alternative to travel on adjacent general-purpose lanes on the same designated route, effectively communicating the range of basic requirements for similar facilities across different regions.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Priced managed lanes adjacent to general-purpose lanes along the same designated route shall be signed using the legend EXPRESS or EXPRESS LANE(S) as provided in this Chapter. This applies when any of the following operational strategies is used: (A) all users are charged a fixed or variable toll; (B) general-purpose traffic is charged a toll but HOV traffic travels without a toll (full- or part-time); (C) general-purpose traffic is charged a toll but HOV traffic is offered a discounted toll (full- or part-time); or (D) general-purpose traffic is charged a toll but HOV traffic registered with a local program travels at a discounted toll or without a toll (full- or part-time), typically requiring a transponder or other identifier for registration verification with electronic/visual enforcement.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 5 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06The legends EXPRESS and EXPRESS LANE(S) shall not be used on signs for entrances to highways on which all lanes are managed and there are no adjacent general-purpose lanes on the same designated route. These legends shall not be used on signs for a managed ramp connection that provides an alternative to a general-purpose ramp connection (see Figure 2F-13), except where the ramp leads directly to a managed lane as described in Section 2G.14. These legends shall not be used on signs for open-road tolling lanes that bypass a conventional toll plaza (see Chapter 2F).

2G.20Signs for Part-Time Travel on a Shoulder — General

Support · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01In some cases, paved shoulders are allowed for driving during peak periods to manage congestion, on freeways, expressways, and conventional roads alike. 2G.25Lane-Use Control Signals for Active Lane Management on Freeways and Expressways

Support · Standard · Option · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Active lane management is a component of active traffic management in which the use of travel lanes and speed limits might be varied in real time in response to traffic conditions to manage congestion. It might employ lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T) and/or changeable message signs (see Chapter 2L). Figure 2G-33 shows an example of lane-use control signals and variable Speed Limit signs for active lane management during an incident.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Except as otherwise provided, lane-use control signals used for active lane management shall comply with Chapter 4T. When used for active lane management on a freeway/expressway, a steady YELLOW X signal indication shall be displayed to warn road users to vacate the lane when the next downstream lane-use control signal over the same lane is displaying a steady RED X.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03A steady YELLOW X signal indication may be displayed on one or more lane-use control signals in advance of the steady YELLOW X required by Paragraph 2, as conditions warrant, to warn road users to vacate the lane.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Using too many steady YELLOW X signal indications could diminish the effectiveness of conveying that the lane is closed a short distance ahead and the road user needs to vacate the lane soon.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05When operated in conjunction with a temporary planned lane closure, lane-use control signals shall only supplement the temporary traffic control devices as provided in Part 6 of this Manual.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06Spacing of lane-use control signals for active lane management on freeways and expressways should be at ½-mile intervals. Closer spacing should be used where viewing distance is limited by roadway geometry, overcrossings, or other sight obstructions, or where traffic entering from intervening interchange ramps is not adequately served by the ½-mile spacing.
  2. 07Combining lane-use control signals with overhead sign support structures should be minimized to avoid overloading road users with too much information or conflicting/incorrect messages, such as exclusive lane use or a lane drop implied by a steady DOWNWARD GREEN ARROW indication below a guide sign.

2G.26Variable Speed Limits for Active Traffic Management on Freeways and Expressways

Support · Standard · Guidance

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Active traffic management on freeways and expressways might employ variable speed limits as an element of an overall congestion management plan using a variable Speed Limit (R2-1) sign (see Section 2B.21).
  2. 02Careful consideration is needed in locating variable Speed Limit signs along the roadway and potential positioning adjacent to guide signs or lane-use control signals so that the speed displayed is clearly associated with the intended lane(s) and not other adjacent lanes, ramps, or roadways. This might require placing variable Speed Limit signs on separate supports away from guide/other signs or lane-use control signals.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03The regulatory speed displayed on a variable Speed Limit sign shall comply with Paragraph 13 of Section 2B.21 and the "Standard Highway Signs" publication (see Section 1A.05).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03aRefer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for an error in Paragraph 3 text. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04The location and positioning of variable Speed Limit signs should clearly associate the displayed speed with the intended lane(s) such that it would not conflict or confuse with other posted speed limits or advisory speeds for adjacent lanes, ramps, or roadways. Variable Speed Limit signs should not be located on overhead guide sign installations (see Section 2E.43).
  2. 05In addition to the post-interchange Speed Limit sign (see Paragraph 17 of Section 2E.47), the spacing of variable Speed Limit signs on freeways/expressways should be based on an engineering study considering factors such as recurring congestion, high-volume interchanges, weaving sections, and other location-specific factors known to affect travel speeds. ★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 2G

  • The white diamond symbol is reserved exclusively for HOV lanes with a vehicle occupancy requirement — it must never appear on bus, taxi, or bicycle preferential lane signs, and guide signs (unlike regulatory signs) show the diamond but never the occupancy number or hours of operation.
  • Three lane-separation geometries recur throughout: barrier-separated, buffer-separated, and contiguous — each drives different rules for whether guide signs are overhead-only, post-mounted-only, or a required overhead/post-mounted supplement pair.
  • Entry-point signs for preferential lanes must never use the word "EXIT" — they use "ENTRANCE" instead, since drivers are not leaving the designated route; conversely, egress/exit signs for leaving the preferential lane use conventional exit terminology.
  • EXPRESS / EXPRESS LANE(S) is the uniform, reserved legend for priced managed lanes adjacent to general-purpose lanes on the same route — it is withheld from all-lanes-managed highways, alternative toll ramps, and open-road-tolling bypass lanes.
  • Priced managed lane signs swap the ETC purple header panel for a black-on-yellow TOLL warning header when ETC registration is not required to travel the lane, and drop the diamond symbol from Vehicle Occupancy Definition signs (R3-40/R3-43) when the occupancy requirement is only a toll-exemption threshold, not an HOV-lane admission requirement.
  • Part-time shoulder travel combines regulatory signs (R3-51 series), warning signs (W3-9/W130(CA)), lane-use control signals (steady green arrow / yellow X / red X), and — only where a lane drop is created solely during shoulder-open periods — a blank-out or changeable EXIT ONLY guide sign panel.
  • Active lane management and variable speed limits are freeway/expressway-only tools: lane-use control signals are spaced at ½-mile intervals (closer where sightlines are limited), and variable Speed Limit signs must never be mounted on overhead guide sign installations to avoid confusing which lane the posted speed governs.
  • California adds an enforcement-signing layer (2G.101(CA), SR50 series) and metered-on-ramp preferential lane signing (2G.102(CA), R88(CA)/R90-1(CA)/R91-1(CA)/R94(CA)) not found in the base FHWA manual.
