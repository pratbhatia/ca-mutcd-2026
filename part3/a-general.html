Part 3 · Markings · Chapter 3A

General

Foundational provisions for all pavement, curb, and other markings covered in Part 3 — standardization of meaning, material properties, the six standard marking colors, the width/pattern vocabulary (normal, wide, double, broken, dotted) for longitudinal lines, and minimum retroreflectivity maintenance. Also hosts the master detail sheets (Figures 3A-101(CA) through 3A-114(CA)) that every other chapter in Part 3 cross-references for exact line widths, gaps, and marker spacing.

3A.01Standardization of Application

Support · Standard · Guidance · Option — organized by paragraph type below.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Markings are used to supplement other traffic control devices such as signs, signals, and other markings. In other instances, markings are used alone to effectively convey regulations, warnings, or guidance in ways not obtainable by the use of other devices.
  2. 02Markings can take many forms, including road surface markings, curb markings, delineators, colored pavements, and channelizing devices.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Each standard marking shall be used only to convey the meaning prescribed for that marking in this Manual, including when used for applications not described in this Manual.
  2. 04Except as provided in Chapter 3H, markings that must be visible at night shall be retroreflective unless the markings are adequately visible under street or highway lighting. All markings on Interstate highways shall be retroreflective.
  3. 05Markings that are no longer applicable for roadway conditions or restrictions and that might cause confusion for the road user shall be removed or obliterated to be unidentifiable as a marking as soon as practicable.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05aLocal authorities may apply lines, arrows, or other symbols to street or highway surfaces to guide visitors and tourists to local points of interest, provided Caltrans approves such markings (CVC § 21374).
Option — Permissive
  1. 06Until they can be removed or obliterated, markings that are no longer applicable for roadway conditions or restrictions may be temporarily masked with non-reflective, preformed tape that is approximately the same color as the pavement surface.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 07Obliteration of obsolete markings, using one of the known permanent removal methods, such as Ultra-High-Water blasting, shot blasting, or grinding, is the preferred action rather than temporary masking, particularly on faded asphalt concrete pavement. This results in the most visually distinct pavement markings and reduces the risk of driver confusion due to sun glare, especially on eastbound and westbound approaches.
  2. 08Use of black tape for temporary "masking" is effective for new Asphalt Concrete pavement. However, for faded Asphalt Concrete pavement or Portland Cement Concrete pavements, black "masking" pavement markings could appear as a stripe in low light conditions and result in confusion to road users.

In practice: obliteration (grinding, shot blasting, water blasting) is favored over black masking tape whenever the roadway surface condition allows it — masking is a stopgap, not a preferred long-term treatment.

3A.02Materials

Guidance · Option — organized by paragraph type below.

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01The materials used for markings should provide the specified color throughout their useful life.
  2. 02Consideration should be given to selecting pavement marking materials that will minimize tripping or loss of traction for road users, including pedestrians, bicyclists, and motorcyclists.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Marking systems that consist of clumps or droplets of material with visible open spaces of bare pavement between the material droplets, which can function in a manner that is similar to the marking systems that completely cover the pavement surface, may be used as pavement markings if they meet the other pavement marking requirements of the highway agency.

3A.03Colors

Standard · Option · Standard · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Markings shall be yellow, white, red, blue, green, or purple. The colors for markings shall conform to the standard highway colors.
  2. 01aThe color of curb markings shall conform to CVC § 21458.
  3. 01bLines, arrows, or other symbols on the street or highway surface to guide visitors and tourists shall be distinct in color from official traffic control devices (CVC § 21374).
Option — Permissive
  1. 02Black markings may be used in combination with the colors mentioned in Paragraph 1 of this Section to enhance the contrast with a light-colored pavement.
  2. 02aIf the material used for centerline marking is paint, a 3-inch-wide black line may be placed between the 4-inch-wide yellow lines on streets and highways under local jurisdiction.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02bIf the material used for centerline marking is paint, a 3-inch-wide black line shall be placed between the 4-inch-wide yellow lines on State highways.
  2. 03When used, yellow markings for longitudinal lines shall delineate: (A) the separation of traffic traveling in opposite directions, (B) the left-hand edge of the roadways of divided highways and one-way streets or ramps, or (C) the separation of two-way left-turn lanes and reversible lanes from other lanes.
  3. 04When used, white markings for longitudinal lines shall delineate: (A) the separation of traffic flows in the same direction, (B) the right-hand edge of the roadway, or (C) both the right-hand edge and left-hand edge of a reversible roadway.
  4. 05When used, red raised pavement markers or delineators shall delineate: (A) truck escape ramps, or (B) one-way roadways, ramps, or travel lanes that shall not be entered or used in the direction from which the markers are visible.
  5. 06When used, blue markings shall supplement white markings for parking spaces for persons with disabilities.
  6. 07When used, purple markings shall be in accordance with the provisions of Chapter 3F to identify toll plaza approach lanes restricted to use only by vehicles with registered electronic toll collection accounts.
  7. 08When pavement markings that simulate route signs are used (see Section 3B.22), the colors shall be the same as those that are used for the route signs (see Section 2D.11).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09Provisions regarding colored pavements are contained in Chapter 3H.

In practice: the black contrast line between double-yellow center lines is mandatory on the State Highway System (Paragraph 2b) but only optional on locally-owned streets and highways (Paragraph 2a) — the same physical detail carries a different obligation level depending on the owning agency.

3A.04Functions, Widths, and Patterns of Longitudinal Pavement Markings

Standard · Option · Guidance · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The general functions of longitudinal lines shall be as follows: (A) a double line indicates maximum or special restrictions; (B) a solid line discourages or prohibits crossing (depending on the specific application); (C) a broken line indicates a permissive condition; (D) a dotted lane line provides warning of a downstream change in lane function; (E) a dotted line used as a lane line or edge line extension guides vehicles through an intersection, a taper area, or an interchange ramp area.
  2. 02The widths and patterns of longitudinal lines shall be as follows: (A) Normal line — 4 to 6 inches wide; (B) Wide line — at least twice the width of a normal line; (C) Double line — two parallel lines separated by a discernible space, with the pavement surface visible between the lines in the same way that it is visible outside the lines, except where contrast markings are used in combination with the double line (see Section 3A.03); (D) Broken line — normal width line segments separated by gaps; (E) Dotted line — noticeably shorter line segments separated by shorter gaps than used for a broken line, with the width of a dotted line extension at least the same as the width of the line it extends.
  3. 02aAll longitudinal traffic lines for lane lines, edge lines, and centerlines on state highways shall be 6 inches wide.
  4. 02bAll longitudinal traffic lines on local agency roadways shall be a minimum of 4 inches wide.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02cLongitudinal traffic lines on local agency roadways may be 6 inches wide.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03To be recognized as a double line rather than two separate, disassociated single lines, the discernible space separating the parallel lines of a double line should not exceed two times the line width of a single line.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04The width of the line indicates the degree of emphasis.
  2. 05Increasing edge line width from 4 inches to 6 inches has been shown to be a beneficial countermeasure to enhance safety at locations with a history of run-off-the-road crashes (see Section 3B.09). Wider normal lines with a 6-inch width instead of the minimum 4-inch width can be beneficial to both human drivers and driving automation systems (see Section 5B.02).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06Broken lines should consist of 10-foot line segments and 30-foot gaps, or dimensions in a similar ratio of line segments to gaps as appropriate for traffic speeds and the need for delineation.
  2. 07A dotted line used as a lane line (see Section 3B.07) should consist of 3-foot line segments and 9-foot gaps. A dotted line for line extensions within an intersection, taper area, or interchange ramp area (see Section 3B.11) should consist of 2-foot line segments and 2-foot to 6-foot gaps.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 07aThe widths and patterns of longitudinal lines shall conform to the details shown in Figures 3A-101(CA) through 3A-114(CA).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Section 5B.02 contains information on pavement marking considerations for driving automation systems.

3A.05Maintaining Minimum Pavement Marking Retroreflectivity

Standard · Guidance · Support · Option — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, a method designed to maintain retroreflectivity at or above 50 mcd/m2/lx under dry conditions shall be used for longitudinal markings on roadways with speed limits of 35 mph or greater.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Except as provided in Paragraph 5 of this Section, a method designed to maintain retroreflectivity at or above 100 mcd/m2/lx under dry conditions should be used for longitudinal markings on roadways with speed limits of 70 mph or greater.
  2. 03The method used to maintain retroreflectivity should be one or more of those described in "Methods for Maintaining Pavement Marking Retroreflectivity" (FHWA-SA-22-028), 2022 Edition, FHWA, or developed from an engineering study based on the values in Paragraphs 1 and 2 of this Section.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Retroreflectivity levels for pavement markings are measured with an entrance angle of 88.76 degrees and an observation angle of 1.05 degrees. This geometry is also referred to as 30-meter geometry. The units of pavement marking retroreflectivity are reported in mcd/m2/lx, which means millicandelas per square meter per lux.
Option — Permissive
  1. 05The following markings may be excluded from the provisions established in Paragraphs 1 and 2 of this Section: (A) markings where ambient illumination assures that the markings are adequately visible; (B) markings on streets or highways that have an ADT of less than 6,000 vehicles per day, and markings to alert possible wrong-way drivers, as shown in Figure 3A-102(CA) and Figure 3A-105(CA), Details 9A, 12A, and 25A; (C) dotted extension lines that extend a longitudinal line through an intersection, major driveway, or interchange area (see Section 3B.11); (D) curb markings; (E) parking space markings; and (F) shared-use path markings.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06The provisions of this Section do not apply to non-longitudinal pavement markings including, but not limited to, the following: (A) transverse markings; (B) word, symbol, and arrow markings; (C) crosswalk markings; and (D) chevron, diagonal, and crosshatch markings.
  2. 07Special circumstances will periodically cause pavement marking retroreflectivity to be below the minimum levels. These circumstances include, but are not limited to, the following: (A) isolated locations of abnormal degradation; (B) periods preceding imminent resurfacing or reconstruction; (C) unanticipated events such as equipment breakdowns, material shortages, and contracting problems; and (D) loss of retroreflectivity resulting from snow maintenance operations.
  3. 08When such circumstances occur, compliance with Paragraphs 1 and 2 of this Section is still considered to be achieved if a reasonable course of action is taken to resume maintenance of minimum retroreflectivity in a timely manner according to the maintaining agency's method(s), policies, and procedures.

In practice: the 50 mcd/m2/lx floor (35+ mph) is a Standard — mandatory — while the 100 mcd/m2/lx level for 70+ mph roadways is only a Guidance recommendation; six specific marking types (curb markings, parking space markings, shared-use path markings, dotted intersection/driveway/interchange extensions, low-ADT roads, and wrong-way alert markings) are optionally excluded from the minimum-retroreflectivity program entirely.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 3A

  • Markings are limited to six standard colors — yellow, white, red, blue, green, purple — each tied to a specific meaning (yellow = opposing/left-side separation; white = same-direction/right-side separation; red = do-not-enter/escape ramp; blue = accessible parking; purple = registered electronic toll accounts).
  • A 3-inch black contrast line between double-yellow center lines (painted markings) is mandatory on State highways but only optional on local streets and highways.
  • Normal lines are 4–6 inches wide; State highway lane, edge, and center lines are always 6 inches; local agency lines are a minimum of 4 inches (6 inches permitted). Wide lines are at least double a normal line's width.
  • Broken lines are typically 10-ft segments/30-ft gaps; dotted lane-line markings are typically 3-ft segments/9-ft gaps; dotted intersection/taper/ramp extensions are typically 2-ft segments with 2- to 6-ft gaps.
  • Figures 3A-101(CA) through 3A-114(CA) are the master construction detail sheets that every marking type in Chapter 3B (and beyond) is required to conform to for exact widths, gaps, and raised-pavement-marker placement.
  • Minimum retroreflectivity maintenance (50 mcd/m2/lx at ≥35 mph; 100 mcd/m2/lx recommended at ≥70 mph, 30-meter geometry) applies only to longitudinal markings, with defined exclusions for curb markings, parking spaces, shared-use paths, low-ADT roads, and dotted intersection extensions.
