Part 3 · Markings · Chapter 3B

Pavement and Curb Markings

The core marking chapter — center lines, lane lines, edge lines, no-passing zones, reversible-lane and two-way left-turn markings, channelizing lines, raised pavement markers, curb markings, stop/yield lines, word/symbol/arrow markings, chevron and diagonal markings, parking space markings, speed reduction and speed hump markings, and lane-reduction and obstruction-approach markings.

3B.01Yellow Center Line Pavement Markings

Standard · Option · Standard · Guidance · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Center line pavement markings, when used, shall be the pavement markings used to delineate the separation of traffic lanes that have opposite directions of travel on a roadway and shall be yellow.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02Center line pavement markings may be placed at a location that is not the geometric center of the roadway.
  2. 03On roadways without continuous center line pavement markings, short sections may be marked with center line pavement markings to control the position of traffic at specific locations, such as around curves, over hills, on approaches to grade crossings, at grade crossings, and at bridges.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04The center line markings on two-lane, two-way roadways shall be one of the following as shown in Figure 3B-1: (A) two-direction passing zone markings consisting of a normal width broken yellow line where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for traffic traveling in either direction; (B) one-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of a double yellow line, one of which is a normal width broken yellow line and the other is a normal width solid yellow line, where crossing the center line markings for passing with care is permitted for the traffic traveling adjacent to the broken line, but is prohibited for traffic traveling adjacent to the solid line; or (C) two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of two normal width solid yellow lines where crossing the center line markings for passing is prohibited for traffic traveling in either direction.
  2. 05A single solid yellow line shall not be used as a center line marking on a two-way roadway.
  3. 06Except where a reversible lane (see Section 3B.04) or a two-way left-turn lane (see Section 3B.05) is present, the center line markings on undivided two-way roadways with four or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic always available shall be the two-direction no-passing zone markings consisting of normal width double solid yellow lines as shown in Figure 3B-2.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07Section 3B.11 contains information for application of pavement markings through intersections or interchanges.
  2. 08On two-way roadways with three through lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic, two lanes should be designated for traffic in one direction by using one-direction or two-direction no-passing zone markings as shown in Figure 3B-3.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09Refer to FHWA's List of Known Errors for error in labeling Paragraph 7. Refer to Section 1A.04 for more details.

3B.02Warrants for Yellow Center Lines

Standard · Guidance · Option · Standard · Option · Support · Standard · Option — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Center line markings shall be placed on all paved undivided two-way urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 6,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings shall also be placed on all paved undivided two-way streets or highways that have three or more lanes for moving motor vehicle traffic.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Center line markings should be placed on paved urban arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 20 feet or more in width and an ADT of 4,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on all rural arterials and collectors that have a traveled way of 18 feet or more in width and an ADT of 3,000 vehicles per day or greater. Center line markings should also be placed on other traveled ways where an engineering study indicates such a need.
  2. 03Engineering judgment should be used in determining whether to place center line markings on traveled ways that are less than 16 feet wide because of the potential for traffic encroaching on the pavement edges, traffic being affected by parked vehicles, and traffic encroaching into the opposing traffic lane.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04Center line markings may be placed on other paved two-way traveled ways that are 16 feet or more in width.
  2. 05If a traffic count is not available, the ADTs described in this Section may be estimates that are based on engineering judgment.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06Centerline patterns shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3A-101(CA) and 3A-104(CA).
  2. 07Raised retroreflective pavement markers shall be used to supplement the centerline markings on State highways. If used in snow areas, they shall be recessed.
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Raised retroreflective pavement markers may be used to supplement the centerline markings on highways and conventional roads.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 09On horizontal curves with radii less than 3280 feet and without street lighting, Detail 22 instead of Detail 21 can be helpful in improving the delineation for centerline markings, as it includes retroreflective raised pavement markers. Detail 22 can be applied in advance of the approach to the curve per Table 2C-3 and continued throughout the length of the curve.
  2. 10Refer to CVC § 21460 for Double Lines.
  3. 11Refer to CVC § 21460.5 for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 12A left edge line shall consist of a solid yellow line of 4 to 6 inches wide for local streets and roads and 6 inches wide on the State Highway System, yellow reflective pavement markers, yellow-red retroreflective markers, or a combination of line and markers as shown in Figure 3A-105(CA).
Option — Permissive
  1. 13Two normal solid yellow lines may be used as a left edge line on a divided roadway for more emphasis when motorists tend to use the shoulder for a through lane or where encroachments onto the shoulder occasionally occur.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 14Left edge line patterns for median islands are shown in Figure 3A-107(CA).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 15When a passing lane is provided, a two-direction no passing marking (refer to Figure 3A-104(CA)) shall be used when the Average Daily Traffic (ADT) exceeds 3,000. Refer to Figure 3B-104(CA).
Option — Permissive
  1. 16Passing in both directions may be provided by alternating the direction of the middle lane at about 1 mile intervals.
  2. 17A one-direction no passing marking (refer to Figure 3A-103(CA)) with one or more YIELD TO UPHILL TRAFFIC (R55(CA)) signs may be used when the ADT is 3,000 or less.

In practice: the center-line warrant thresholds step down from mandatory (20 ft / 6,000 ADT urban) to recommended (20 ft / 4,000 ADT urban, 18 ft / 3,000 ADT rural) to discretionary (16 ft, any ADT) — engineering judgment governs below 16 feet because of edge encroachment and parked-vehicle conflicts.

3B.03No-Passing Zone Pavement Markings

Standard · Support · Guidance · Standard · Support · Standard · Guidance — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01No-passing zones shall be marked by either the one-direction no-passing zone pavement markings or the two-direction no-passing zone pavement markings described in Section 3B.01 and shown in Figures 3B-1 and 3B-3.
  2. 02No-passing zone markings shall be used on: (A) two-way roadways at lane-reduction transitions (see Section 3B.12), (B) approaches to obstructions that must be passed on the right (see Section 3B.13), (C) approaches to grade crossings (see Section 8C.02), and (D) approaches to crosswalks.
  3. 03On two-way, two-lane or three-lane roadways where center line markings are installed, no-passing zones shall be established at vertical and horizontal curves and other locations where an engineering study indicates that passing must be prohibited because of inadequate sight distances or other special conditions.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 05The passing sight distance on a vertical curve is the distance at which an object 3.5 feet above the pavement surface can be seen from a point 3.5 feet above the pavement (see Figure 3B-4). Similarly, the passing sight distance on a horizontal curve is the distance measured along the center line (or right-hand lane line of a three-lane roadway) between two points 3.5 feet above the pavement on a line tangent to the embankment or other obstruction that cuts off the view on the inside of the curve (see Figure 3B-4).

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07Where the distance between successive no-passing zones is less than 400 feet, no-passing zone markings should connect the zones.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 07aIf the gap between successive no-passing zones is less than the sight distance for the prevailing speed shown in Table 3B-1, or 400 feet, whichever is greater, the no-passing zone shall be continuous.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08No-passing zone signs (see Sections 2B.36, 2B.37, and 2C.53) are sometimes used to emphasize the existence and extent of a no-passing zone.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09On three-lane roadways where the direction of travel in the center lane transitions from one direction to the other, a no-passing buffer zone, consisting of a flush median island (see Section 3J.03) at least 50 feet in length, shall be provided in the center lane as shown in Figure 3B-5. A lane-reduction transition (see Section 3B.12) shall be provided approaching each end of the buffer zone.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 10Refer to CVC §§ 21750 through 21759 for overtaking and passing.
  2. 11Refer to CVC § 21460 for Double Lines.
  3. 12CVC § 21752 restricts passing (driving on the left side of a two-way roadway) when approaching within 100 feet of or when traversing any intersection or railroad grade crossing. CVC § 21752 also restricts passing (driving on the left side of a two-way roadway) when the view is obstructed upon approaching within 100 feet of any bridge, viaduct, or tunnel. The patterns and policy for intersection markings are shown in Figure 3A-109(CA).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 13No-passing zone patterns shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3A-103(CA) and 3A-104(CA).
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 14The no-passing zone markings at intersections, when used, should be between 100 feet and 300 feet in length at the approach to an intersection and placed in a pattern as shown in Figure 3A-109(CA).

3B.04Yellow Pavement Markings for Reversible Lanes

Standard · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01If reversible lanes are used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of reversible lanes shall consist of a normal width broken double yellow line to delineate the edge of a lane in which the direction of travel is reversed from time to time, such that each of these markings serve as the center line markings of the roadway during some period (see Figure 3B-6).
  2. 02Signs (see Section 2B.34), lane-use control signals (see Chapter 4T), or both shall be used to supplement reversible lane pavement markings.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Section 3E.02 contains additional applications of pavement markings for counter-flow preferential lanes that also operate as reversible lanes.

Standard · Guidance · Option · Standard · Guidance · Option · Support · Standard · Option · Guidance · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01If a two-way left-turn lane that is never operated as a reversible lane is used, the lane line pavement markings on each side of the two-way left-turn lane shall consist of a normal width broken yellow line and a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the edges of a lane that can be used by traffic in either direction as part of a left-turn maneuver. These markings shall be placed with the broken line toward the two-way left-turn lane and the solid line toward the adjacent traffic lane as shown in Figure 3B-7.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02White two-way left-turn lane-use arrows should be used at or just downstream from the beginning of a two-way left-turn lane.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Additional two-way left-turn lane-use arrow markings may be used at other locations along a two-way left-turn lane where engineering judgment determines that such additional markings are needed to emphasize the proper use of the lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04A single-direction lane-use arrow shall not be used in a lane bordered on both sides by yellow two-way left-turn lane longitudinal markings.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05Signs should be used in conjunction with the two-way left-turn markings (see Section 2B.32).
  2. 06Two-way left-turn lane markings should not extend to intersections (see definition in Section 1C.02).
Option — Permissive
  1. 07Two-way left-turn lanes may be transitioned to mandatory left-turn lanes as shown in Figure 3B-7 or painted median islands where they approach an intersection.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Section 8A.06 contains guidance information for discontinuing a two-way left-turn lane in the immediate vicinity of a highway-rail or highway-LRT grade crossing.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09On State highways, reversible lanes shall be separated by physical barriers or delineators.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 10A two-way left-turn lane is a lane reserved in the center of a highway for exclusive use of left or U-turning vehicles. Refer to CVC § 21460.5. It is normally used where there are many points of access.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 11The two-way left-turn lane markings shall be selected from those shown in Figures 3B-7 and 3A-108(CA).
Option — Permissive

  2. 13Two-way opposing pavement arrows may be used as shown in Figure 3B-7(CA). The arrows may be supplemented by a Two-Way Left Turn Lane (R3-9a or R3-9b) sign at new installations and problem locations.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 14A gap in the markings should be made at all intersections.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 15For left turn channelization, refer to Figure 3B-101(CA) and Caltrans' Highway Design Manual, Section 405.2. Refer to Section 1A.11 for information regarding this publication.
  2. 16Channelized left-turn lanes in combination with continuous raised-curb medians are used instead of two-way left-turn lanes (TWLTL) if one or more of the following conditions exist: (A) average daily traffic volumes exceed 20,000 vehicles per day; (B) for remediation where there is a demonstrated crash problem; (C) wherever a need is demonstrated through engineering study.
  3. 17Refer to CVC § 21460.5 for Two-Way Left-Turn Lanes.
  4. 18For details of two-way left-turn lanes, refer to Figure 3B-7(CA). For left turn channelization, refer to Figure 3B-101(CA) and Caltrans' Highway Design Manual, Section 405.2. Refer to Section 1A.05 for information regarding this publication.

3B.06White Lane Line Pavement Markings

Standard · Guidance · Support · Standard · Guidance · Option · Support · Standard — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01When used, lane line pavement markings delineating the separation of traffic lanes that have the same direction of travel shall be white.
  2. 02Lane line markings shall be used on all freeways and Interstate highways.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Lane line markings should be used: (A) on all roadways that are intended to operate with two or more adjacent traffic lanes in the same direction of travel, except as otherwise required for reversible lanes; (B) at congested locations where the roadway will accommodate more traffic lanes with lane line markings than without the markings.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Examples of lane line markings are shown in Figures 3B-2, 3B-3, and 3B-7 through 3B-13, and 3B-7(CA) through 3B-10(CA).
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05Except as provided in Paragraph 1 of Section 3B.07, where crossing the lane line markings with care is not discouraged or prohibited, the lane line markings shall consist of a normal width broken white line.
  2. 06Where crossing the lane line markings is discouraged, the lane line markings shall consist of a normal width solid white line.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07A solid white lane line marking should be used on approaches to: (A) intersections to separate a through lane from a mandatory turn lane; (B) intersections to separate contiguous mandatory turn lanes from each other; (C) toll collection points to separate toll lanes, payment methods, channelized movements, or obstructions.
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Solid white lane line markings may be used: (A) on approaches to intersections to separate contiguous through lanes; (B) to separate through traffic lanes from auxiliary lanes, such as an added uphill truck lane; (C) on approaches to crosswalks across multi-lane roadways.
  2. 09Wide solid lane line markings may be used for greater emphasis.
  3. 10A curved transition may be used where an edge line, channelizing line, or dotted extension line changes direction.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 11Examples of locations where a curved transition can have value include freeway exit and entrance ramps, and turn lanes.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 12Where crossing the lane line markings is prohibited, the lane line markings shall consist of a double solid white line (see Figure 3B-8).
  2. 13Lane line patterns shall be selected from those shown in Figure 3A-102(CA). Detail 9 or 10 (40 mph or less) or Detail 12, 12A, or 13 (45 mph or more) shall be used on freeways, expressways, freeway ramps, freeway to freeway connectors and collector roads, except when used in snow areas, the raised pavement markers, if used, shall be recessed; otherwise, use Detail 8 or 11.
  3. 14When a climbing lane is provided on an upgrade and truck lane control signs are used (refer to Section 2B.38), an 8-inch solid white line shall be used in place of the standard lane line stripe.

  1. 06aWhite chevron markings (refer to Section 3B.25) shall be placed in the neutral area of exit ramp gores.
  2. 07A wide dotted white lane line shall be used: (A) as a lane drop marking in advance of lane drops at exit ramps to distinguish a lane drop from a normal exit ramp (see Drawings A, B, and C in Figure 3B-11); (B) in advance of freeway route splits with dedicated lanes (see Drawing D in Figure 3B-11); (C) in advance of freeway route splits with an option lane (see Drawing E in Figure 3B-11); (D) to separate a through lane that continues beyond an interchange from an adjacent continuous auxiliary lane between an entrance ramp and an exit ramp (see Drawing F in Figure 3B-11 and Figure 3B-11(CA) Sheet 1 of 2); (E) as a lane drop marking in advance of lane drops at intersections to distinguish a lane drop from an intersection through lane (see Drawing A in Figure 3B-12); and (F) to separate a through lane that continues beyond an intersection from an adjacent auxiliary lane between two intersections (see Drawing B in Figure 3B-12).

  2. 09On the approach to a multi-lane exit ramp having an optional exit lane that also carries through traffic, lane line markings should be used as illustrated in Drawing B in Figure 3B-11.

The lane drop markings should begin no closer to the intersection than the most upstream regulatory or warning sign associated with the lane drop.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 11aThe lane drop line pattern shall be as shown in Figure 3A-111(CA).

  1. 11bRefer to Figures 3A-111(CA), 3B-9(CA), 3B-10(CA), 3B-14(CA) and 3B-104(CA) for further details of markings and signing.
Option — Permissive
  1. 11cAn 8-inch wide single solid white line preceded by an 8-inch wide dotted white line may be placed in advance of an intersection where the outside lane is dropped at the intersection, and as a result, creates a mandatory turn lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 11dIf used, diagonal lines shall be the same color as the edge line.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 12Sections 3B.21 and 3B.23 contain information regarding other markings that are associated with lane drops, such as ONLY word pavement markings and lane-use arrows.
  2. 13Section 3B.12 contains information about the lane line markings that are to be used for transition areas where the number of through lanes is reduced at a location that is not at an interchange or intersection.
Option — Permissive
  1. 14In the case of a lane drop at an exit ramp or intersection, a solid white line may replace a portion, but not all of the length, of the wide dotted white lane line.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 15Left-turn or right-turn lanes shall be separated from the through lanes by a single solid 8-inch-wide white line as shown in Figure 3A-112(CA) except as provided in Paragraph 16.
Option — Permissive
  1. 16Left-turn or right-turn lanes may be separated from the through lanes by multiple solid 8-inch-wide white lines or two longitudinal solid 8-inch-wide lines with diagonal lines used for crosshatch markings.

3B.08Channelizing Lines

Support · Standard · Support · Standard · Option · Standard · Support — organized by paragraph type below.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Channelizing lines are used to form neutral areas where traffic traveling in the same general direction is permitted on both sides including entrance and exit ramps, access and egress points to and from managed lanes, toll-plaza bypasses, and left-turn lanes separated from through lanes. Channelizing lines are also sometimes used to alter travel paths for speed management or other purposes.
  2. 02Chapter 3J contains information for the application of channelizing lines used in conjunction with islands.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03Except as provided in Section 3E.04 and Paragraph 6 of Section 3J.05, a channelizing line shall be a solid wide or double solid white line.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Examples of channelizing line applications are shown in Figures 3B-9, 3B-10, 3B-10(CA), 3B-11, Drawing C in Figure 3B-15, Figures 3J-1 through 3J-5, and Drawing B in Figure 3J-6.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 05For all exit ramps and for entrance ramps with parallel acceleration lanes, channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area (see Figures 3B-9 and 3B-11, 3B-9(CA), 3B-10(CA) Sheet 2 of 2, and Drawing A in Figure 3B-10).
  2. 06For entrance ramps with tapered acceleration lanes, channelizing lines shall be placed along both sides of the neutral area to a point at least one-half of the distance to the theoretical gore (see Drawing C in Figure 3B-10 and Figure 3B-10(CA) Sheet 1 of 2).
  3. 07Channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area for bifurcations created from open-road tolling lanes that bypass a conventional toll plaza.
  4. 08Where neutral areas are formed at access and egress points to and from a managed-lane facility, channelizing lines shall be placed on both sides of the neutral area (see Figures 2G-8, 2G-10, 2G-13, 2G-16, 2G-22, 2G-23, 2G-27, and 2G-28).
Option — Permissive
  1. 09For entrance ramps with tapered acceleration lanes, the channelizing lines may extend to the theoretical gore as shown in Drawing B in Figure 3B-10 and 3B-10(CA) Sheet 1 of 2.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 10Other pavement markings in the neutral area shall be white.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 11Pavement markings within the neutral area include chevron markings (see Section 3B.25), retroreflective raised pavement markers (see Section 3B.16), and internally illuminated raised pavement markers (see Section 3B.16).

3B.09Edge Line Pavement Markings

Standard · Support · Option · Support · Guidance · Support · Standard · Guidance · Option · Support · Standard — organized by paragraph type below.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01If used, edge line pavement markings shall delineate the right or left edges of a roadway.
  2. 02Except as provided in Section 3E.04, right edge line pavement markings, if used, shall consist of a normal width solid white line to delineate the right-hand edge of the roadway.
  3. 03If used on the roadways of divided highways or one-way streets, or on any ramp in the direction of travel, left edge line pavement markings shall consist of a normal width solid yellow line to delineate the left-hand edge of a roadway or to indicate driving or passing restrictions left of these markings, except as provided in Section 3E.04.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 04Edge line markings provide visual references to guide road users during adverse weather and visibility conditions.
Option — Permissive
  1. 05Wide solid edge line markings may be used for greater emphasis.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 06Increasing edge line width from 4 inches to at least 6 inches can be a beneficial countermeasure on all facility types in both urban and rural areas.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 07Edge line markings should not be continued through intersections, except for the following situations: (A) dotted edge line extensions (see Section 3B.11), or (B) through that part of an intersection with no intersection approach (such as the far side of a T-intersection).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 08Section 3B.11 contains information on the use and application of edge lines through intersections, interchanges, and driveways.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 09A right edge line shall consist of a solid white line of 4 to 6 inches for local streets and roads, and 6 inches on the State Highway System.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 3B

  • Center lines are always yellow and take one of three forms (two-direction passing, one-direction no-passing, two-direction no-passing); a single solid yellow line is never used alone as a center line. Warrant thresholds step from mandatory (20 ft/6,000 ADT urban, 3+ lanes) to recommended (20 ft/4,000 ADT urban, 18 ft/3,000 ADT rural) to discretionary judgment below 16 feet.
  • Lane lines separating same-direction traffic are white: broken where crossing is permitted, solid where discouraged, double solid where prohibited; dotted white lines mark non-continuing lanes (deceleration/acceleration lanes, lane drops) and are built from a defined vocabulary of normal/wide widths and 3-ft/9-ft or 2-ft/2-to-6-ft segment-gap patterns.
  • Edge lines are white on the right (solid, 4-6 in. local / 6 in. State) and yellow on the left of divided highways/one-way roadways/ramps; mandatory on freeways, expressways, and rural arterials meeting width/ADT thresholds; exit ramps get enhanced retroreflective marker spacing (120 ft setback, then 12-ft then 6-ft centers) as a wrong-way-crash countermeasure.
  • Raised pavement markers follow a single spacing unit "N" (one broken-line segment plus one gap) defined in Section 3B.14 and reused throughout Sections 3B.15-3B.17 for positioning-guide, supplementing, and substituting applications, with color always matching the marking they serve.
  • Curb marking colors are fixed by CVC §21458 (red = no stop/stand/park; yellow = load/unload; white = passenger load/mail; green = time-limited; blue = disabled parking) and require an underlying local ordinance.
  • Stop lines (solid white, 12-24 in. wide) and yield lines (rows of solid white isosceles triangles, 12-24 in. base, height = 1.5× base) are mutually exclusive by device type — stop lines pair only with STOP-type devices/signals, yield lines only with YIELD-type devices — and "limit line" is legally synonymous with "stop line" under CVC §377.
  • Chevron and diagonal markings, arrow types (Type I through XI and curved-stem CS I-V), speed reduction markings, and speed hump/table markings each have specific dimensional standards (widths from 4 to 12+ inches, arrow spacing of 100-300 ft, transverse line counts of 8 for advance speed hump warnings) that must be selected from the Figure 3A-101(CA) through 3A-114(CA) and 3B-series detail sheets.
