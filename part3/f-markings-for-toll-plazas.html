Part 3 · Markings · Chapter 3F

Markings for Toll Plazas

Longitudinal, purple ETC-supplement, and pavement word/symbol marking requirements that channelize toll-plaza approaches by payment method, including open-road tolling (ORT) lane separation.

3F.01General

Support — background/explanatory.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01At toll plazas, pavement markings help road users identify the proper lane(s) to use for the type of toll payment they plan to use, to channelize movements into the various lanes, and to delineate obstructions in the roadway.
  2. 02Section 3H.08 contains information on the use and application of purple-colored pavement at toll plazas for vehicles with registered electronic toll collection (ETC) accounts.

3F.02Longitudinal Markings

Guidance · Option · Standard · Guidance · Standard · Option — organized by paragraph type below.

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Solid white lane line markings should be used to separate toll lanes, payment methods, or to channelize movements at toll plazas.
  2. 02Solid white lane line markings should begin at the upstream end of the full-width toll lane and be continued to the toll plaza.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03For a toll plaza approach lane that is restricted to use only by vehicles with registered ETC accounts, the solid white lane line or edge line on the right-hand side of the ETC Account-Only lane and the solid white lane line or solid yellow edge line on the left-hand side of the ETC Account-Only lane may be supplemented with purple solid longitudinal markings placed contiguous to the inside edges of the lines defining the lane.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04If the purple solid longitudinal markings described in Paragraph 3 of this Section are used, the purple markings shall be at least 3 inches wide.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05If the purple solid longitudinal markings described in Paragraph 3 of this Section are used, the purple markings should not be wider than the line they supplement.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 06Toll booths and the islands on which they are located are considered to be obstructions in the roadway and they shall be provided with markings that comply with the provisions of Section 3B.13 and Chapter 3J.
Option — Permissive
  1. 07Longitudinal pavement markings may be omitted alongside toll booth islands between the approach markings and any departure markings.

In practice: the 3-inch minimum width for the purple ETC-supplement marking, and the requirement that it not exceed the width of the line it supplements, are the two numbers most likely to get checked during a toll-plaza marking plan review.

3F.03Pavement Word and Symbol Markings

Support · Standard · Option · Guidance — organized by paragraph type below.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Section 3E.03 contains information on the use of pavement word and symbol markings for ETC Account-Only lanes not specific to toll plazas.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 02Except as provided in Paragraph 4 of this Section, when a lane on the approach to a toll plaza is restricted to use only by vehicles with registered ETC accounts, the ETC Account-Only lane word markings or pictograph described in Section 3E.03 shall be used (see Drawing A in Figure 3H-6).
  2. 03When one or more open-road tolling (ORT) lanes that are restricted to use only by vehicles with registered ETC accounts bypass a mainline toll plaza on a separate alignment, pavement word markings or pictographs shall be used on the approach to the point where the ORT lanes diverge from the lanes destined for the mainline toll plaza (see Drawings B and C in Figure 3H-6).
Option — Permissive
  1. 04Preferential lane-use markings may be omitted at toll plazas where physical conditions preclude the use of the markings.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 05If an ORT lane that is immediately adjacent to a mainline toll plaza is not separated from adjacent cash payment toll plaza lanes by a curb or barrier, then channelizing devices (see Section 3I.01), and/or longitudinal pavement markings that discourage or prohibit lane changing should be used to separate the ORT lane from the adjacent cash payment lane. This separation should begin on the approach to the mainline toll plaza at approximately the point where the vehicle speeds in the adjacent cash lanes drop below 30 mph during off-peak periods and should extend downstream beyond the toll plaza approximately to the point where the vehicles departing the toll plaza in the adjacent cash lanes have accelerated to 30 mph.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 3F

  • Solid white lane lines channelize toll-plaza approach lanes by payment method; an optional purple longitudinal marking (at least 3 inches wide, no wider than the line it supplements) can flag ETC Account-Only lanes.
  • Toll booths and their islands are treated as roadway obstructions and must be marked per Section 3B.13 and Chapter 3J.
  • ETC Account-Only word/pictograph markings from Section 3E.03 are required on ETC-restricted approach lanes and on approaches to where separate-alignment open-road tolling (ORT) lanes diverge, except where physical conditions preclude their use.
  • Where an ORT lane adjacent to the mainline plaza lacks a curb or barrier, separation (channelizing devices and/or lane-change-discouraging markings) should span from where adjacent cash-lane speeds drop below 30 mph on approach to where departing cash-lane speeds return to 30 mph.
