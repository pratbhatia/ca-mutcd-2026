Part 4 · Highway Traffic Signals · Chapter 4O

Traffic Control Signals for One-Lane, Two-Way Facilities

Application, design exceptions, and flashing-red operation for the alternating signal used at narrow bridges, tunnels, and roadway sections too narrow for opposing traffic to pass.

4O.01Application of Traffic Control Signals for One-Lane, Two-Way Facilities

Support · Guidance · Option

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01A traffic control signal at a narrow bridge, tunnel, or roadway section that is not of sufficient width for two opposing vehicles to pass is a special signal that alternates which direction of travel is permitted to proceed.
  2. 02Temporary traffic control signals (see Sections 4D.11 and 6L.01) are the most frequent application of traffic control signals for one-lane, two-way facilities.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 03Sight distance across or through the one-lane, two-way facility should be considered as well as the approach speed and sight distance approaching the facility when determining whether traffic control signals should be installed.
Option — Permissive
  1. 04At a narrow bridge, tunnel, or roadway section where a traffic control signal is not justified under the conditions of Chapter 4C, a traffic control signal may be used if gaps in opposing traffic do not permit the flow of traffic through the one-lane section of roadway.

4O.02Design of Traffic Control Signals for One-Lane, Two-Way Facilities

Standard

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The provisions of Chapters 4D through 4G shall apply to traffic control signals for one-lane, two-way facilities, except that:
    1. A. The durations of the red clearance intervals shall be adequate to clear the one-lane section of conflicting vehicles; and
    2. B. Adequate means, such as interconnection, shall be provided to prevent conflicting signal indications, such as green and green, at opposite ends of the section.

4O.03Operation of Traffic Control Signals for One-Lane, Two-Way Facilities

Guidance · Standard

Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 01Traffic control signals at one-lane, two-way facilities should operate in a manner consistent with traffic requirements.
  2. 02Adequate time should be provided to allow traffic to clear the narrow facility before opposing traffic is allowed to move. Engineering judgment should be used to determine the proper timing for the signal.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 03When in the flashing mode, the signal indications shall flash red.

In practice: this is the signal type engineers most commonly encounter as a temporary traffic control signal for construction staging on a two-lane road reduced to one lane — the critical design detail is the interconnection between the two signal heads at opposite ends, which must positively prevent both ends from showing green at the same time.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 4O

  • Used at narrow bridges, tunnels, or roadway sections too narrow for two opposing vehicles to pass; most commonly implemented as a temporary traffic control signal (Sections 4D.11, 6L.01).
  • Follows the general provisions of Chapters 4D–4G, with two key exceptions: red clearance intervals must be long enough to clear the one-lane section, and the two ends must be interconnected to positively prevent simultaneous green/green conflicts.
  • Timing is set by engineering judgment to allow traffic to fully clear the narrow facility before releasing opposing traffic.
  • In flashing mode, the signal indications flash red (not yellow) at both ends.
