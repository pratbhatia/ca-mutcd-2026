Part 4 · Highway Traffic Signals · Chapter 4Q

Traffic Control for Movable Bridges

Coordinated signals and gates that stop road users for bridge openings — when both are required, their design, location, and interlocked operation with the movable span.

4Q.01Application of Traffic Control for Movable Bridges

Support · Standard

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Traffic signals for movable bridges are a special type of highway traffic signal installed at movable bridges to notify road users to stop because of a road closure rather than alternately controlling the flow of conflicting traffic movements. The signals are operated in coordination with the opening and closing of the movable bridge, and with the operation of movable bridge warning and resistance gates, or other devices and features used to warn, control, and stop traffic.
  2. 02Movable bridge warning gates installed at movable bridges decrease the likelihood of vehicles and pedestrians passing the stop line and entering an area where potential hazards exist because of bridge operations.
  3. 03A movable bridge resistance gate is sometimes used at movable bridges and located downstream of the movable bridge warning gate. A movable bridge resistance gate provides a physical deterrent to road users when placed in the appropriate position. The movable bridge resistance gates are considered a design feature and not a traffic control device; requirements for them are contained in AASHTO's "Standard Specifications for Movable Highway Bridges."
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Traffic control at movable bridges shall include both signals and gates, except in the following cases:
    1. A. Neither is required if other traffic control devices or measures considered appropriate are used under either of the following conditions: (1) on low-volume roads (roads of less than 400 vehicles average daily traffic), or (2) at manually-operated bridges if electric power is not available.
    2. B. Only signals are required in urban areas if intersecting streets or driveways make gates ineffective.
    3. C. Only movable bridge warning gates are required if a traffic control signal that is controlled as part of the bridge operations exists within 500 feet of the movable bridge resistance gates and no intervening traffic entrances exist.

4Q.02Design and Location of Movable Bridge Signals and Gates

Standard · Option · Standard · Guidance · Option · Guidance · Standard · Option · Support · Standard · Guidance · Option · Guidance · Option · Guidance · Support · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01The signal faces and mountings of movable bridge signals shall comply with the provisions of Chapters 4D through 4G except as provided in this Section.
  2. 02Signal faces with 12-inch diameter signal indications shall be used for all new movable bridge signals.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Existing signal faces with 8-inch diameter lenses may be retained for the remainder of their useful service life.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 04Since movable bridge operations cover a variable range of time periods between openings, the signal faces shall be one of the following types:
    1. A. Three-section signal faces with red, yellow, and green signal indications; or
    2. B. Two one-section signal faces with red signal indications in a vertical array separated by a STOP HERE ON RED (R10-6) sign (see Section 2B.59).
  2. 05Regardless of which signal type is selected, at least two signal faces shall be provided for each approach to the movable span and a stop line (see Section 3B.19) shall be installed to indicate the point behind which vehicles are required to stop.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 06If movable bridge operation is frequent, the use of three-section signal faces should be considered.
  2. 07Insofar as practicable, the height and lateral placement of signal faces should comply with the requirements for other traffic control signals in accordance with Chapter 4D. They should be located no more than 50 feet in advance of the movable bridge warning gate.
Option — Permissive
  1. 08Movable bridge signals may be supplemented with audible warning devices to provide additional warning to drivers and pedestrians.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 09A DRAW BRIDGE (W3-6) sign (see Section 2C.36) should be used in advance of movable bridge signals and gates to give warning to road users, except in urban conditions where such signing would not be practical.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 10If physical conditions prevent a road user from having a continuous view of at least two signal indications for the distance specified in Table 4D-2, an auxiliary device (either a supplemental signal face or the DRAW BRIDGE (W3-6) sign to which has been added a Warning Beacon that is interconnected with the movable bridge controller unit) shall be provided in advance of movable bridge signals and gates.
Option — Permissive
  1. 11The DRAW BRIDGE (W3-6) sign may be supplemented by a Warning Beacon (see Section 4S.03).
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 12If two sets of gates (both a warning and a resistance gate) are used for a single direction, highway traffic signals are not required to accompany the resistance gate nearest the span opening.
Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 13Movable bridge warning gates, if used, shall be at least standard railroad size, striped with 16-inch alternate vertical, fully-reflectorized red and white stripes. Flashing red lights in accordance with the Standards for those on railroad gates (see Section 8D.03) shall be included on the gate arm and they shall only be operated if the gate is closed or in the process of being opened or closed.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 14In the horizontal position, the top of the gate should be approximately 4 feet above the pavement.
  2. 15Movable bridge warning gates should be of lightweight construction. In its normal upright position, the gate arm should provide adequate lateral clearance.
Option — Permissive
  1. 16The movable bridge resistance gates may be delineated, if practical, in a manner similar to the movable bridge warning gate.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 17Movable bridge warning gates, if used, should extend at least across the full width of the approach lanes if movable bridge resistance gates are used. On divided highways in which the roadways are separated by a barrier median, movable bridge warning gates, if used, should extend across all roadway lanes approaching the span openings.
  2. 18If movable bridge resistance gates are not used on undivided highways, movable bridge warning gates, if used, should extend across the full width of the roadway.
Option — Permissive
  1. 19A single full-width gate or two half-width gates may be used.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 20The locations of movable bridge signals and gates are determined by the location of the movable bridge resistance gate (if used) rather than by the location of the movable spans. The movable bridge resistance gates for high-speed highways are preferably located 50 feet or more from the span opening except for bascule and lift bridges, where they are often attached to, or are a part of, the structure.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 21Except where physical conditions make it impracticable, movable bridge warning gates should be located 100 feet or more from the movable bridge resistance gates or, if movable bridge resistance gates are not used, 100 feet or more from the movable span.
  2. 22On bridges or causeways that cross a long reach of water and that might be hit by large marine vessels, within the limits of practicality, traffic should not be halted on a section of the bridge or causeway that is subject to impact.
  3. 23In cases where it is impractical to halt traffic on a span that is not subject to impact, traffic should be halted at least one span from the opening. If traffic is halted by signals and gates more than 330 feet from the movable bridge warning gates (or from the span opening if movable bridge warning gates are not used), a second set of gates should be installed approximately 100 feet from the gate or span opening.
  4. 24If the movable bridge is close to a grade crossing and traffic might possibly be stopped on the crossing as a result of the bridge opening, a traffic control device should notify the road users to not stop on the railroad tracks.

4Q.03Operation of Movable Bridge Signals and Gates

Standard · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Traffic control devices at movable bridges shall be coordinated with the movable spans, so that the signals, gates, and movable spans are controlled by the bridge tender through an interlocked control.
  2. 02If the three-section type of signal face is used, the green signal indication shall be displayed at all times between bridge openings, except that if the bridge is not expected to open during continuous periods in excess of 5 hours, a flashing yellow signal indication shall be permitted to be used. The signal shall display a steady red signal indication when traffic is required to stop. The duration of the yellow change interval between the display of the green and steady red signal indications, or flashing yellow and steady red signal indications, shall be determined using engineering practices (see Section 4F.17).
  3. 03If the vertical array of red signal indications is the type of signal face selected, the red signal indications shall flash alternately only when traffic is required to stop.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 04Traffic control signals on adjacent streets and highways should be interconnected with the movable bridge control if indicated by engineering judgment. When such interconnection is provided, the traffic control signals at adjacent intersections should be preempted by the operation of the movable bridge in the manner described in Section 4F.19.

In practice: the choice between the two allowed signal-face types tracks how the bridge is normally used — a three-section red/yellow/green face (green resting between openings, or flashing yellow if openings are infrequent, i.e. less than every 5 hours) suits a bridge with frequent traffic in the "go" state, while the vertical two-lens alternating-flashing-red array paired with a STOP HERE ON RED sign is a simpler, beacon-like alternative that only ever needs to say "stop" or say nothing.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 4Q

  • Movable bridges generally require both signals and gates, coordinated via interlocked control with the bridge tender; exceptions exist for low-volume roads (<400 ADT), manually-operated bridges without power, urban areas where gates would be ineffective, and where an existing nearby signal within 500 feet already serves the function.
  • New signal faces use 12-inch indications (8-inch existing lenses may be retained); face type is either a standard 3-section red/yellow/green face or two vertically-arrayed one-section red lenses with a STOP HERE ON RED (R10-6) sign — at least 2 faces per approach, plus a stop line.
  • Movable bridge warning gates must be standard railroad size with 16-inch red/white reflectorized stripes and railroad-standard flashing red lights, operated only while closing/closed/opening; resistance gates are a design feature (not a TCD) governed by AASHTO specifications.
  • Warning gates should generally sit 100+ feet from resistance gates (or from the span if no resistance gate); if traffic must be halted more than 330 feet back, a second gate set is added about 100 feet from the gate/span.
  • On the 3-section face, green (or flashing yellow, if openings occur less than every 5 hours) is the default "go" state between openings, switching to steady red to stop traffic; the vertical 2-lens face uses alternating flashing red only when stopping traffic is required.
