Highway Traffic Signals at Toll Plazas

Restrictions on signal-like devices at toll lanes, and the specific use of lane-use control signals and Warning Beacons within a toll plaza.

4R.01Traffic Signals at Toll Plazas

Standard · Guidance

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Traffic control signals or devices that closely resemble traffic control signals that use red or green circular indications shall not be used at toll plazas to indicate the open or closed status of the toll plaza lanes.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Traffic control signals or devices that closely resemble traffic control signals that use red or green circular indications should not be used for new or reconstructed installations at toll plazas to indicate the success or failure of electronic toll payments or to alternately direct drivers making cash toll payments to stop and then proceed.

4R.02Lane-Use Control Signals at or Near Toll Plazas

Standard · Option

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Lane-use control signals used at toll plazas shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 4T except as otherwise provided in this Section.
  2. 02At toll plazas with multiple lanes where one or more lanes is sometimes closed to traffic, a lane-use control signal shall be installed above the center of each toll plaza lane to indicate the open or closed status of the controlled lane.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03The bottom of the signal housing of a lane-use control signal above a toll plaza lane having a canopy may be mounted lower than 15 feet above the pavement, but not lower than the vertical clearance of the canopy structure.
  2. 04Lane-use control signals may also be used to indicate the open or closed status of an Open-Road ETC lane as a supplement to other devices used for the temporary closure of a lane (see Part 6).

4R.03Warning Beacons at Toll Plazas

Standard · Guidance · Option

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01Warning Beacons used at toll plazas shall comply with the provisions of Chapter 4S except as otherwise provided in this Section.
Guidance — Recommended Practice
  1. 02Warning Beacons, if used with a toll plaza canopy sign (see Section 2F.16) to assist drivers of such vehicles in locating the dedicated ETC Account-Only lane(s), should be installed in a manner such that the beacons are distinctly separate from the lane-use control signals (see Section 4T.01) for the toll plaza lane.
Option — Permissive
  1. 03Warning Beacons that are mounted on toll plaza islands, behind impact attenuators in front of toll plaza islands, and/or on toll booth pylons (ramparts) to identify them as objects in the roadway may be mounted at a height that is appropriate for viewing in a toll plaza context, even if that height is lower than the normal minimum of 8 feet above the pavement.

In practice: the driving concern behind Section 4R.01 is driver confusion — a red/green circular indication at a toll booth looks exactly like a "stop/go" traffic signal, so any device communicating toll-payment success or lane status at the booth itself must use a different visual vocabulary (lane-use control signal symbols, or a dedicated canopy sign) rather than a signal face.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 4R

  • Red/green circular signal-like indications are prohibited at toll plazas for indicating lane open/closed status, and should not be used (new/reconstructed installations) to indicate electronic toll payment success/failure or to direct cash-toll drivers to stop-then-proceed.
  • Lane status at multi-lane toll plazas is instead communicated with a lane-use control signal (Chapter 4T) mounted above the center of each lane; canopy-mounted units may sit lower than the normal 15-foot minimum, down to the canopy's vertical clearance.
  • Lane-use control signals may also mark Open-Road ETC lane status as a supplement to Part 6 temporary lane-closure devices.
  • Warning Beacons at toll plazas follow Chapter 4S, but may be mounted lower than the usual 8-foot minimum when placed on islands, behind impact attenuators, or on toll booth pylons; ETC-lane-locating beacons should be kept visually distinct from lane-use control signals.
