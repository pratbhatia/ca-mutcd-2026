Part 9 · Traffic Control for Bicycle Facilities · Chapter 9F

Signals

The shortest chapter in Part 9 — a single page pointing engineers to Part 4's signal provisions as they apply to bicycles, with three brief sections covering signal warrant application, bicycle signal faces, and signal operations adjustments for bicyclists. The substantive bicycle signal design content lives in Part 4, Chapter 4H.

Cross-Reference Notice

Chapter 9F is intentionally brief. It does not duplicate signal design content — it directs the reader to Part 4, Chapter 4H (Bicycle Signals) for the substantive Standard/Guidance/Option/Support provisions governing bicycle signal face design, placement, and detection. Every requirement that Chapter 9F itself imposes is captured in full below; anything about signal face design, signal head layout, or detection zone geometry is in Chapter 4H, not here.

9F.01Application

Standard · Guidance · Option · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Part 4 contains information regarding signal warrants and other requirements relating to signal installations.
Option — Permissive
  1. 02For purposes of signal warrant evaluation, bicycles may be counted as either vehicles or pedestrians.
Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 03Also refer to Part 4 of this Manual for highway traffic signals, Chapter 4H Bicycle Signals, in particular:
    A. Section 4H.101(CA) — Optional Use of Bicycle Signal Faces.
    B. Section 4H.102(CA) — Limit Line Bicycle Detection Zone.

In practice, this is the section to cite whenever a project needs a signal-warrant analysis on a corridor with meaningful bicycle volumes — Paragraph 2's flexibility to count bicycles as either vehicles or pedestrians in the warrant analysis (per Part 4) can materially change whether a warrant is met.

9F.02Bicycle Signal Faces

Standard · Guidance · Option · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Support — Background/Explanatory
  1. 01Chapter 4H contains information on the design and application of bicycle signal faces.
  2. 02Section 9B.22 contains information for the Bicycle Signal sign that is required to be installed with a bicycle signal face.

In practice, remember the pairing rule embedded in Paragraph 2: wherever a bicycle signal face is installed, the corresponding Bicycle Signal sign from Section 9B.22 is a companion requirement, not an independent option — check Chapter 9B when specifying the signal face.

9F.03Signal Operations for Bicycles

Standard · Guidance · Option · Support — paraphrased faithfully below, organized by paragraph type.

Standard — Mandatory Requirement
  1. 01At installations where visibility-limited signal faces are used, signal faces shall be adjusted so bicyclists for whom the indications are intended can see the signal indications. If the visibility-limited signal faces cannot be aimed to serve the bicyclist, then separate signal faces shall be provided for the bicyclist.
  2. 02On bikeways, signal timing and actuation shall be reviewed and adjusted to consider the needs of bicyclists.

In practice, Paragraph 1 is frequently relevant at skewed or offset intersection approaches where a visibility-limited (louvered or shielded) vehicle signal face is aimed narrowly at motor traffic — if that aiming cannot also serve waiting bicyclists in an adjacent bike lane or bikeway, a dedicated bicycle signal face is mandatory, not discretionary. Paragraph 2's review of timing and actuation is the hook for ensuring detection (loop, video, or push-button) and minimum green/clearance intervals actually account for bicycle approach speeds, which are slower and more variable than motor vehicle speeds.

★ Key Takeaways — Chapter 9F

  • Chapter 9F is a compact, three-section chapter (9F.01–9F.03) that exists to route the reader to Part 4, Chapter 4H for the substantive design of bicycle signal faces and detection zones — it does not duplicate that content.
  • Bicycles may be counted as either vehicles or pedestrians for signal warrant evaluation purposes (9F.01, Paragraph 2), which can affect whether a warrant is satisfied on a bicycle-heavy corridor.
  • Any bicycle signal face installation must be paired with the Bicycle Signal sign required under Section 9B.22 (9F.02, Paragraph 2).
  • Two firm Standard-level requirements govern signal operations for bicycles: visibility-limited signal faces must be adjusted or supplemented with separate faces so intended bicyclists can see the indication (9F.03, Paragraph 1), and signal timing/actuation on bikeways must be reviewed and adjusted for bicyclist needs (9F.03, Paragraph 2).
  • Key cross-references: Section 4H.101(CA) (Optional Use of Bicycle Signal Faces) and Section 4H.102(CA) (Limit Line Bicycle Detection Zone) are the two provisions Chapter 9F points to most directly.
